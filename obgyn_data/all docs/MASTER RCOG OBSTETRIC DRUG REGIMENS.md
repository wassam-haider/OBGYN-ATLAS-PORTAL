Important guideline hierarchy for your exam

For an MRCOG/UK-style question, I would rank the sources:

1. RCOG Green-top / Scientific Impact Papers
↓
2. NICE
↓
3. BNF/UK antimicrobial guidance
↓
4. ACOG for comparison where UK guidance differs

RCOG's current catalogue includes dedicated guidance covering PPH, GBS, PPROM, preterm birth, thrombosis, placenta praevia/PAS, FGR, thyroid disease, sepsis, multiple pregnancy and other obstetric conditions.

ACOG should therefore be used as the secondary comparison, not mixed into the RCOG regimen. For example, ACOG's hypertension guidance confirms the same major emergency-treatment framework, while its PPH guidance similarly includes uterotonics and TXA.

One final safety point

A truly exhaustive list of every obstetric medication would also include anaesthetic agents, blood products, antiarrhythmics, antibiotics for every organism, fetal cardiac drugs, chemotherapy/immunotherapy for maternal malignancy, psychiatric medicines, epilepsy medicines, transplant medicines and drugs for every maternal medical disorder. Those do not have one universal RCOG "drug of choice." The table above therefore focuses on the core obstetric conditions and obstetric-specific medications that are realistically tested in MRCOG/MCPS and commonly encountered clinically, while flagging specialist situations rather than inventing a universal dose.
