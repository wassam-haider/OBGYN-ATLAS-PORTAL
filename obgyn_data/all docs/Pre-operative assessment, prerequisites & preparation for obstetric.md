Pre-operative assessment, prerequisites & preparation for obstetric procedures — RCOG/MRCOG framework

This is a practical MRCOG/obstetric theatre checklist covering the major obstetric procedures you may encounter in labour ward, emergency theatre, elective theatre and postpartum haemorrhage (PPH). RCOG guidance is procedure-specific rather than one single universal “pre-op checklist”; the framework below synthesises the RCOG curriculum, consent guidance and relevant Green-top Guidelines. RCOG specifically expects knowledge of operative vaginal birth, breech/twin delivery, caesarean section, perineal trauma, retained placenta, anaesthesia, resuscitation and obstetric emergencies.

MRCOG principle: Before any obstetric procedure, think INDICATION → MATERNAL → FETAL → ANAESTHESIA → CONSENT → BLOOD → TEAM → THEATRE → SAFETY → PROCEDURE → POST-OP PLAN.

20. THE “10-POINT MRCOG PRE-OP” ANSWER

If the examiner asks:

“How would you prepare this patient for the procedure?”

Give this structure:

1. Indication

“First, I would confirm the indication and urgency.”

2. Maternal assessment

“I would perform ABCDE assessment and assess comorbidities and haemodynamic stability.”

3. Fetal assessment

“I would assess fetal wellbeing, presentation, position, gestation and estimated weight as appropriate.”

4. Investigations

“I would obtain appropriate blood tests including FBC and group-and-save/crossmatch depending on haemorrhage risk.”

5. Anaesthesia

“I would involve the anaesthetist and assess airway, aspiration risk, coagulation and suitability for regional versus general anaesthesia.”

6. Consent

“I would explain the procedure, benefits, alternatives, material risks and consequences of refusing or delaying it.”

7. Blood

“I would assess haemorrhage risk and ensure appropriate blood and blood products are available.”

8. Team

“I would involve the appropriate senior obstetric, anaesthetic, midwifery and neonatal teams.”

9. Theatre safety

“I would complete the WHO surgical safety checks, antibiotics where indicated, VTE prophylaxis and equipment preparation.”

10. Post-procedure plan

“I would have a clear plan for maternal observations, analgesia, antibiotics where indicated, thromboprophylaxis, catheter/urine output, neonatal care, complications and follow-up.”

21. MASTER TABLE — PROCEDURE → MOST IMPORTANT PREREQUISITES
Procedure	Critical prerequisites
Operative vaginal birth	Fully dilated, ruptured membranes, known position, appropriate station, adequate analgesia, empty bladder, skilled operator, CS backup
Forceps	Above + correct position, appropriate station, experienced operator
Ventouse	Above + appropriate gestation/position, suitable cup, backup plan
Breech vaginal birth	Appropriate selection, experienced team, fetal assessment, CS capability
Twin vaginal birth	Presentation of both twins, experienced team, 2 neonatal teams/equipment, CS capability
ECV	Appropriate gestation, reassuring fetal assessment, ultrasound, skilled operator, emergency CS capability
Episiotomy	Indication, consent/communication, analgesia, sterile equipment
OASI repair	Correct classification, adequate anaesthesia, good exposure, experienced operator, correct sutures
Manual removal placenta	Diagnosis, anaesthesia, IV access, antibiotics according to protocol, blood availability
Emergency CS	Rapid maternal/fetal assessment, theatre, anaesthesia, neonatal team, IV access, blood preparation
Elective CS	Indication, consent, anaesthetic assessment, blood risk assessment, antibiotics, VTE plan
Cord prolapse	Emergency team, decompression, theatre, anaesthesia, neonatal team
Shoulder dystocia	Emergency call, senior obstetrician, neonatal team, timed manoeuvres, resuscitation
PPH surgery	ABC, IV access, bloods, major haemorrhage protocol, senior team, theatre
Balloon tamponade	Ongoing appropriate uterine bleeding, theatre capability, blood, catheter, monitoring
B-Lynch	Laparotomy, senior surgeon, blood, anaesthesia, escalation plan
Uterine artery embolisation	Haemodynamic suitability, IR, blood, anaesthesia/support
