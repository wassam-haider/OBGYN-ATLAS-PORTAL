2. INITIAL TRIAGE — TARGET WITHIN 15 MINUTES

RCOG recommends that women attending maternity triage receive an initial midwife assessment within 15 minutes and then be prioritised using symptom-specific algorithms.

The initial triage is not the complete medical assessment.

Its purpose is:

Identify who cannot safely wait.

6. ASSIGN TRIAGE CATEGORY
🔴 LEVEL 1 — RED / IMMEDIATE
Principle

The patient cannot safely wait in triage.

Move directly to Labour Ward/Labour Room Operating Room/resuscitation area according to the emergency.

Senior obstetric review immediately.

RCOG similarly recommends that women classified as the highest urgency be transferred to the labour ward for assessment and that urgent cases receive senior obstetric review.

4. PATHOLOGICAL CTG

If CTG shows severe fetal compromise:

➡️ Do not wait for routine triage assessment.

Immediate steps
Call senior obstetrician
Review CTG
Identify reversible causes
Maternal observations
Left lateral positioning where appropriate
Stop oxytocin if running
Treat hypotension
Assess contractions
Consider expedited birth
5. CORD PROLAPSE
Immediate actions
Call emergency team
Relieve cord compression
Knee-chest/left lateral with elevation of presenting part
Avoid unnecessary handling of cord
Prepare immediate birth
Usually emergency CS unless vaginal birth is immediately achievable

14. FINAL DISPOSITION

Every triage patient should finish with a clear documented disposition.

There are essentially four possibilities:

A. ADMIT LABOUR WARD

Examples:

Active labour
Preterm labour
Significant PPROM
Chorioamnionitis
Abnormal CTG
Significant APH
Severe hypertension
High-risk labour
Previous uterine scar with labour
Need for induction/augmentation
B. ADMIT ANTENATAL/WARD

Examples:

Stable PPROM
Pre-eclampsia requiring monitoring
Hyperemesis/dehydration
Pyelonephritis
DVT requiring treatment
Reduced fetal movements requiring further surveillance
Other maternal conditions
C. OBSERVATION → REASSESS

Useful when:

Early labour
Borderline BP
Uncertain membrane rupture
Mild symptoms
Awaiting CTG
Awaiting blood tests
Awaiting ultrasound
Awaiting senior review
D. DISCHARGE HOME

Only when:

Maternal condition stable + fetal assessment reassuring + diagnosis reasonably established + no indication for admission.

Every discharged woman should know:

Return immediately for:

🔴 Vaginal bleeding
🔴 Reduced/absent fetal movements
🔴 Waters breaking
🔴 Green/foul-smelling liquor
🔴 Regular painful contractions
🔴 Severe abdominal pain
🔴 Severe headache
🔴 Visual disturbance
🔴 RUQ/epigastric pain
🔴 Chest pain
🔴 Severe breathlessness
🔴 Fever/rigors
🔴 Collapse
🔴 Seizure
🔴 Any significant deterioration or feeling seriously unwell

For PPROM specifically, NHS advice includes urgent return for fever, offensive/green fluid, bleeding, abdominal/back pain, contractions or reduced fetal movements.
