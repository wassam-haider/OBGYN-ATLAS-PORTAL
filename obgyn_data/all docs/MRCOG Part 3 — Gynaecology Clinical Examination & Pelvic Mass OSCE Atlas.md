MRCOG Part 3 — Gynaecology Clinical Examination & Pelvic Mass OSCE Atlas

This is structured for MRCOG Part 3 performance, not simply textbook knowledge. The examiner is assessing information gathering, applied clinical knowledge, patient safety and communication, with some stations also involving lay examiners. The current Part 3 circuit has 14 tasks, 12 minutes each including 2 minutes reading time.

For every examination station, aim to demonstrate:

Introduce → consent → chaperone → explain → position/privacy → examination → findings → interpretation → differential → investigations → management → safety-net.

PART XI — ENDOMETRIOSIS

NICE recommends considering endometriosis with chronic pelvic pain, significant period-related pain, deep dyspareunia, cyclical bowel/urinary symptoms and infertility associated with these symptoms.

26. Examination findings

May be completely normal.

A normal pelvic examination does NOT exclude endometriosis.

NICE specifically states that normal examination and even normal ultrasound do not exclude endometriosis.

Possible findings:

PART XVII — MRCOG "EXAMINER THINKING"

When given a pelvic examination finding, don't stop at:

"This is a fibroid."

Say:

FINDING → INTERPRETATION → DIFFERENTIAL → INVESTIGATION → MANAGEMENT

Example:

"The uterus is approximately 14-week size, firm, irregular and mobile. This is most consistent with uterine fibroids. I would assess the severity of symptoms, particularly heavy menstrual bleeding, pressure symptoms and fertility wishes. I would arrange pelvic ultrasound to define number, size and location of fibroids and assess the endometrial cavity where relevant. Management would then be individualised according to symptoms, fibroid characteristics, age, fertility wishes and patient preference."

That demonstrates applied clinical knowledge, rather than simple examination recall.

PART XVIII — HIGH-YIELD MRCOG PART 3 SAFETY POINTS
Never forget:
Pregnancy test in reproductive-age woman with acute pelvic pain.
ABCDE before detailed examination if unstable.
Consent and chaperone for intimate examination.
Stop if the patient asks you to stop.
Normal examination does not exclude endometriosis.
Normal ultrasound does not exclude endometriosis.
CA-125 is not a diagnostic test for endometriosis.
TVUS is recommended for suspected endometriosis.
Acute unilateral pain + vomiting → consider torsion.
Pain + positive pregnancy test → ectopic until excluded.
Ascites + adnexal/pelvic mass → malignancy must be considered.
Fixed pelvic mass → think malignancy, endometriosis or adhesions.
Irregular firm uterus → fibroid.
Globular tender/boggy uterus → adenomyosis.
Posterior fornix/uterosacral nodularity → deep endometriosis.
Do not over-reassure based on a normal cervical screening history when the cervix looks suspicious.
Always establish fertility wishes before recommending definitive gynaecological treatment.

The RCOG specifically expects candidates to demonstrate effective communication, logical information gathering, interpretation of findings, justification of investigations and discussion of management options—not merely perform a physical examination.

CONSENT → SAFETY → EXAMINE → DESCRIBE → INTERPRET → DIFFERENTIAL → INVESTIGATE → MANAGE → SAFETY-NET
