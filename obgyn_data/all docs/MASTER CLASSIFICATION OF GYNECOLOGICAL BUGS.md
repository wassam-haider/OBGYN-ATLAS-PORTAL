🦠 MASTER CLASSIFICATION OF GYNECOLOGICAL BUGS
I. BACTERIA
A. Sexually transmitted bacterial pathogens
Organism	Main disease/condition	High-yield association
Chlamydia trachomatis	Cervicitis, urethritis, PID, salpingitis, infertility, Fitz-Hugh–Curtis syndrome	Cervicitis + PID + infertility
Neisseria gonorrhoeae	Gonococcal cervicitis, urethritis, PID, salpingitis	Purulent cervicitis
Mycoplasma genitalium	Cervicitis, urethritis, PID	Cervicitis/PID
Treponema pallidum	Syphilis	Painless chancre
Haemophilus ducreyi	Chancroid	Painful genital ulcer
Klebsiella granulomatis	Donovanosis/granuloma inguinale	Beefy-red painless ulcer
C. trachomatis L1–L3	Lymphogranuloma venereum	Genital ulcer → lymphadenopathy/proctitis

NICE's evidence base specifically includes chlamydia, gonorrhoea, syphilis, LGV and M. genitalium among STI-related organisms.

B. Mollicutes
Organism	Disease/association
Mycoplasma genitalium	Cervicitis, urethritis, PID
Mycoplasma hominis	Genital tract/pelvic infection associations
Ureaplasma urealyticum	Mainly colonisation; association with urethritis/genital tract disease
⭐ Exam priority

M. genitalium → cervicitis + PID

NICE notes increasing recognition of M. genitalium, including significant antimicrobial resistance.

III. ANAEROBIC BACTERIA

Important particularly in PID and tubo-ovarian abscess.

Organism/group	Conditions
Bacteroides spp.	BV, PID, pelvic abscess
Prevotella spp.	BV, PID
Fusobacterium spp.	Pelvic infection/PID
Peptostreptococcus spp.	Pelvic infection
Other anaerobes	PID, tubo-ovarian abscess
⭐ Key concept

PID is usually polymicrobial, not a single-organism infection.

Major pathogens include Chlamydia + Gonorrhoea + M. genitalium, with anaerobic and vaginal flora also contributing.

IV. ENTERIC / GRAM-NEGATIVE BACTERIA
Organism	Gynecological relevance
Escherichia coli	UTI, Bartholin abscess, pelvic infection
Klebsiella spp.	UTI, pelvic infection
Proteus spp.	UTI
Other enteric Gram-negative organisms	PID/pelvic infection
⭐ Most important

E. coli → commonest bacterial cause of UTI

V. GRAM-POSITIVE BACTERIA
Organism	Gynecological conditions
Staphylococcus aureus	Bartholin abscess, vulval/skin infection
Streptococcus pyogenes	Vulvitis, cellulitis, severe genital infection
Enterococcus spp.	Pelvic/postoperative infection
Streptococcus agalactiae (GBS)	Genital colonisation; mainly obstetric significance
Other streptococci	Pelvic/genital infection

GBS is deliberately not a core non-obstetric gynecological bug in this classification because its major clinical importance is obstetric/neonatal.

XI. HEPATITIS VIRUSES

These are sexual-health pathogens rather than primary gynecological pathogens.

Virus	Relevance
HBV	Sexually transmissible hepatitis B
HCV	Sexual transmission possible, especially in higher-risk networks
HAV	Sexual transmission possible in particular sexual practices

NICE addresses hepatitis A and B vaccination within its STI guidance.

XII. OTHER VIRUSES

Important mainly in:

Immunocompromised patients

Epstein–Barr virus — EBV

Can be sexually transmitted but:

This is the consolidated non-obstetric gynecology infection classification. RCOG's curriculum explicitly places BV, candidiasis, STIs, genital ulcers/warts, PID, Bartholin abscess and lower urinary infection within its gynaecology curriculum, while NICE provides the broader UK STI framework.

Excluded from the core list: chorioamnionitis, congenital infections, intra-amniotic infection, puerperal sepsis, neonatal GBS disease and other pregnancy-specific infections, because those belong to obstetric infection, not the non-obstetric gynecology bug list.
