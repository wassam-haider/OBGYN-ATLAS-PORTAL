2. 💧 Suspected rupture of membranes
Cases
PPROM
Term PROM
Preterm labour with ruptured membranes
High leak
Urinary incontinence mistaken for liquor
Physiological vaginal discharge

PPROM = rupture of membranes before 37+0 weeks and before onset of labour.

RCOG GTG 73 specifically covers suspected PPROM from 24+0 to 36+6 weeks.

First-line assessment

History

When did leaking start?
Continuous/intermittent?
Colour?
Odour?
Amount?
Blood/meconium?
Contractions?
Fever/rigors?
Fetal movements?

Examination

Maternal observations
Fetal assessment
Sterile speculum examination

Avoid unnecessary digital vaginal examination when PPROM is suspected.

Diagnosis

If fluid is seen at speculum → supports diagnosis.

If no fluid is seen, RCOG recommends considering IGFBP-1 or PAMG-1 vaginal-fluid testing to aid diagnosis.

Important complications
Chorioamnionitis
Preterm birth
Cord prolapse
Placental abruption
Pulmonary hypoplasia in very early PPROM
3. 🩸 Antepartum haemorrhage

Very important: RCOG defines APH as bleeding from/in the genital tract from 24+0 weeks until birth.

Differential diagnosis
Condition	Typical clues
Placenta praevia	Usually painless bright-red bleeding
Placental abruption	Painful bleeding, uterine tenderness/rigidity
Vasa praevia	Bleeding after ROM + acute fetal compromise
Bloody show	Small amount with labour
Cervical ectropion	Contact bleeding
Cervical polyp	Contact bleeding
Cervical malignancy	Persistent/irregular bleeding
Vaginal trauma	History of intercourse/trauma
Uterine rupture	Pain, shock, fetal compromise, previous uterine scar
APH immediate approach

ABC → maternal resuscitation → fetal assessment → identify source → prepare blood products if significant.

Check:

FBC
Group and save/crossmatch
Coagulation profile if significant bleeding/abruption
RhD status
Kleihauer where indicated
Ultrasound for placental location/fetal assessment
🚨 Never forget

Do NOT perform a digital vaginal examination until placenta praevia has been excluded if placenta location is unknown and significant APH is present.

4. 👶 Reduced fetal movements

One of the highest-yield triage presentations.

24–28 weeks
Confirm fetal heartbeat with Doppler
Maternal assessment
BP
Urinalysis
Fundal-height assessment
Further ultrasound if indicated.
>28 weeks
Immediate maternity assessment
Maternal assessment
Fetal heart assessment
Usually CTG
Ultrasound if indicated, particularly where growth, liquor or other risk factors are concerning.
Important

Do not rely on a fixed kick-count number as the primary diagnostic test.

The important issue is a change/reduction from the baby's usual pattern.

Causes
Placental insufficiency/FGR
Fetal hypoxia
Maternal medication/sedation
Maternal illness
Fetal sleep cycle
Fetal demise
5. ❤️ Abnormal CTG / suspected fetal compromise

Patient may present because of:

Reduced fetal movements
Abnormal CTG from antenatal clinic
Abnormal Doppler
Bleeding
Labour complications
Meconium
Maternal hypotension
Fever/sepsis
Immediate approach

CALL FOR HELP

Then:

Maternal ABC
Check maternal pulse/BP/temperature/SpO₂
Confirm fetal heart rate
Review CTG
Identify reversible causes
Left/right lateral positioning as appropriate
Stop/reduce uterotonic infusion if excessive contractions
Treat maternal hypotension
Consider urgent birth if persistent pathological CTG despite corrective measures

🚨 Emergency

Eclampsia = seizure in pregnancy/postpartum associated with pre-eclampsia until proven otherwise.

Immediate priority:

14. 🧷 Cord prolapse
Presentation

Sudden abnormal CTG
Immediate management

Measures may include:

Manual elevation of presenting part
Maternal knee-chest/left lateral exaggerated position
Avoid handling the cord unnecessarily
Immediate preparation for emergency birth

RCOG specifically recommends that maternity triage uses standardized initial assessment within 15 minutes and symptom-specific algorithms to determine urgency and seniority of review.
