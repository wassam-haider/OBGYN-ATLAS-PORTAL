2. The most important RCOG numbers
🩸 Major APH

For major or massive antepartum haemorrhage, RCOG recommends:

4 units of blood crossmatched

along with FBC and coagulation studies.

So remember:

APH → 4 units crossmatched

Especially:

placenta praevia with significant bleeding
placental abruption with major bleeding
major unexplained APH

For minor APH:

FBC + group and save is generally sufficient rather than automatically crossmatching 4 units.

3. Placenta praevia

Placenta praevia is one of the classic high-risk blood-loss conditions.

RCOG recommends close liaison with the transfusion laboratory, and for women at high risk of emergency transfusion, such as placenta praevia, group-and-screen samples should be repeated weekly when necessary to ensure blood can be rapidly provided.

Exam approach

Placenta praevia →

group & screen
recent sample
blood readily available
anaemia correction
consultant obstetrician
senior anaesthetist
theatre readiness
rapid infusion/warming equipment
cell salvage when substantial blood loss is anticipated

The updated 2026 RCOG PAS/placenta praevia guidance continues to emphasise blood-bank coordination and haemorrhage preparedness.

Don't write:

"All placenta praevia = 4 units."

That is too rigid.

Instead write:

Placenta praevia: crossmatch/ensure immediate availability of blood according to local major-haemorrhage protocol; more extensive preparation for anterior placenta praevia/PAS.

4. Placenta accreta spectrum — the BIG blood preparation condition
PAS includes:
Placenta accreta
Placenta increta
Placenta percreta

Risk increases particularly with:

previous CS + placenta praevia/low placenta

but the new RCOG guidance also recognises other uterine surgical trauma as risk factors.

Before planned PAS surgery

Think:

“Massive haemorrhage theatre.”

Prepare:

multiple large-bore IV access
arterial access as appropriate
experienced obstetric surgeon
anaesthesia team
blood bank alerted
crossmatched RBCs
FFP
platelets
cryoprecipitate/fibrinogen strategy
rapid infuser
warming equipment
cell salvage
ICU/HDU capability
massive transfusion protocol
neonatal team

RCOG recommends cell salvage when anticipated blood loss is sufficient to cause anaemia or exceed approximately 20% of estimated blood volume.

MRCOG memory:

PAS = RBC + FFP + platelets + cryo/fibrinogen + cell salvage + MTP

Don't memorise a single fixed number of RBC units as an RCOG universal rule; hospitals use local PAS protocols based on severity.

5. Major obstetric haemorrhage — blood-product sequence

This is one of the most important numerical areas.

Red cells

There is no fixed RCOG transfusion trigger for RBCs during active major haemorrhage. Clinical condition and laboratory findings determine transfusion.

FFP
RCOG:

FFP 12–15 mL/kg for every 6 units RBC

during major obstetric haemorrhage.

Then guide subsequent FFP by:

PT
APTT
clinical bleeding

Target:

PT/APTT ratio <1.5 × normal.

Easy memory

6 RBC → FFP 12–15 mL/kg

6. Cryoprecipitate

RCOG recommends:

2 × 5-unit pools

early in major obstetric haemorrhage.

Then guide further treatment using fibrinogen.

Target:

Fibrinogen >1.5 g/L

during major obstetric haemorrhage.

7. Platelets

In actively bleeding patients:

Maintain platelets >50 × 10⁹/L

RCOG recommends a transfusion trigger of approximately:

75 × 10⁹/L

to provide a safety margin.

8. Massive PPH — practical blood-product table
Product	RCOG principle
RBC	Clinical + haematological indication
FFP	12–15 mL/kg per 6 RBC
Cryoprecipitate	2 × 5-unit pools early
Platelets	Maintain >50 ×10⁹/L; consider transfusion around 75 ×10⁹/L
Fibrinogen	Maintain >1.5 g/L
Cell salvage	If anticipated blood loss >20% estimated blood volume / sufficient to cause anaemia
O-negative RBC	Emergency option if blood group unavailable; don't delay life-saving transfusion

9. Uterine rupture
Suspected uterine rupture

Treat as:

Major obstetric haemorrhage until proven otherwise.

Preparation:

immediate theatre
senior obstetrician
anaesthetist
2 large-bore IVs
crossmatched blood
massive haemorrhage protocol if bleeding
RBC + FFP + platelets + cryoprecipitate/fibrinogen as indicated
cell salvage where available/appropriate

Potential procedures:

repair → uterine artery/internal iliac interventions in selected cases → hysterectomy

depending on bleeding, rupture characteristics and fertility considerations.

10. Ruptured ectopic pregnancy
Stable, unruptured ectopic

Usually:

Group & screen

rather than automatically crossmatching several units.

Ruptured/unstable ectopic

Think:

Emergency haemorrhage.

Prepare:

urgent crossmatch
RBC immediately available
massive haemorrhage protocol if significant blood loss
FFP/platelets/cryo according to bleeding/coagulation

If life-threatening bleeding occurs before compatible blood is available, emergency uncrossmatched/group-specific blood may be required; RCOG states that transfusion should not be delayed in life-threatening haemorrhage.

12. Myomectomy

This is a particularly important gynaecological procedure.

Low-risk small fibroid

Usually:

Group & screen

Large intramural/submucosal fibroid

Consider:

Crossmatch multiple units according to expected blood loss

and:

preoperative Hb optimisation
anaesthetic planning
cell salvage if major blood loss anticipated
haemorrhage-control strategy

There is no RCOG universal “myomectomy = X units” rule.

13. Hysterectomy
Simple laparoscopic hysterectomy

Usually:

Group & screen / risk-based crossmatch

Large uterus / difficult abdominal hysterectomy

Consider:

crossmatched RBCs

Radical hysterectomy / advanced pelvic malignancy

Higher blood-loss risk:

multiple RBC units + blood-product availability + major haemorrhage capability

Ovarian cancer debulking

Because extensive upper-abdominal/pelvic surgery may be required:

multiple-unit blood preparation + FFP/platelet availability according to institutional protocol.
