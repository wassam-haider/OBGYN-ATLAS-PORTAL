3. WHO/UKMEC-style eligibility classification

For MRCOG, understand the principle:

Category	Meaning
1	No restriction

4. Contraceptive effectiveness

Typical-use effectiveness is important for counselling.

Long-acting reversible contraception — highest effectiveness
Implant.
LNG-IUS.
Copper IUD.

Failure is generally <1% per year with typical use.

Injection
More user-dependent.
Typical-use failure approximately 4%/year.
Combined hormonal contraception
Typical-use failure approximately 7%/year.
Progestogen-only pill
Typical-use failure approximately 7%/year.
Condoms
Typical-use failure approximately 13%/year.
Fertility awareness methods

OSCE principle:

“The most effective reversible methods are LARC methods because they remove adherence problems.”

8. Blood pressure before CHC

Always demonstrate this in an OSCE.

Technique
Patient seated.
Rest before measurement.
Correct cuff size.
Arm supported.
Repeat elevated reading.

A persistent BP around:

≥160/100 mmHg

is generally incompatible with CHC.

Even lower hypertension may alter eligibility depending on severity and cardiovascular risk.

OSCE phrase:

“I would check blood pressure before prescribing combined hormonal contraception.”

30. Important ulipristal interaction

After ulipristal acetate, starting/restarting a progestogen-containing contraceptive immediately can reduce ulipristal effectiveness.

Therefore, follow current guidance regarding the interval before starting hormonal contraception and use condoms/abstinence during that interval and until effective contraception is established.

This is a high-yield MRCOG counselling point.

43. Contraception + migraine
Migraine without aura

CHC may be possible depending on individual risk assessment.

Migraine with aura

Avoid CHC.

44. Contraception + hypertension

Severe hypertension

50. "Contraceptive choice" comparison table
Method	Typical failure	Duration	Main advantage	Main issue
Implant	<1%	~3 years	Very effective	Irregular bleeding
LNG-IUS	<1%	3–8 years depending device	Reduces bleeding	Initial irregular bleeding
Copper IUD	<1%	5–10+ years depending device	Hormone-free	Heavier periods
Injection	~4%	12–13 weeks	No daily pill	Delay in fertility return

52. DMPA and bone density

Important MRCOG question:

DMPA is associated with a reversible reduction in bone mineral density during use.

Don't tell every woman:

“You will develop osteoporosis.”

Instead:

“There can be a temporary reduction in bone mineral density, which generally improves after stopping. I would consider your individual osteoporosis risk factors.”

53. Contraception and STIs

Critical distinction:

Hormonal contraception does NOT protect against STIs.

Condoms reduce transmission of:

HIV.
gonorrhoea.
chlamydia.
some other STIs.

For patients at STI risk:

“I would recommend condoms even if you choose another highly effective contraceptive method.”

54. Pregnancy while using contraception

Always consider:

Ectopic pregnancy

Especially:

pregnancy with IUD/IUS in situ.

However, remember:

An IUD/IUS reduces the overall risk of pregnancy, but if pregnancy occurs with an IUD in situ, the proportion of pregnancies that are ectopic is higher.

63. MRCOG communication phrases to score marks
Shared decision-making

“I can explain the options, but ultimately the choice should be based on your medical eligibility and what fits your priorities.”

Risk communication

“I want to put this risk into perspective. The relative risk may increase, but the absolute risk in a healthy woman is still low.”

If patient refuses

“That's completely your choice. Can I check what concerns you most so I can address them?”

If patient is undecided

“You don't have to decide immediately. I can give you information about the options and we can make a plan that you're comfortable with.”

Safeguarding

“I ask all patients about whether their sexual relationships are consensual and whether anyone is pressuring them, because this is an important part of safe care.”

Safety-netting

“If you develop severe abdominal pain, heavy bleeding, fever, collapse or suspect pregnancy, you should seek urgent medical assessment.”
