MASTER OBGYN DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA
Initial + Definitive Diagnosis — MRCOG / FCPS Atlas
PART I — EARLY PREGNANCY
1. Pregnancy
Initial diagnosis
Urine pregnancy test.
Serum β-hCG if required.
Definitive diagnosis
Ultrasound demonstration of an intrauterine pregnancy with embryo/fetal cardiac activity establishes a viable IUP.
Key point

β-hCG confirms pregnancy, not pregnancy location or viability.

2. Viable intrauterine pregnancy
Initial
Positive pregnancy test.
TVUS.
Definitive

Embryo with cardiac activity on ultrasound.

Important
Yolk sac → confirms intrauterine gestational sac.
Embryo → establishes embryonic pregnancy.
Cardiac activity → viability.
3. Pregnancy of unknown location — PUL
Initial
Positive pregnancy test.
TVUS shows:
no definite IUP
no definite ectopic.
Definitive diagnosis

PUL is itself a temporary ultrasound classification, not a final pregnancy diagnosis.

Follow:

serial β-hCG
repeat TVUS
clinical symptoms.
Final possibilities
viable IUP
failed IUP
ectopic pregnancy.

NICE stresses that ectopic pregnancy must remain in the differential even without classic risk factors.

4. Ectopic pregnancy
Initial

Pregnancy test + TVUS.

Definitive diagnosis

TVUS demonstrating:

Extrauterine gestational sac containing yolk sac/embryo ± fetal cardiac activity.

Supportive findings
adnexal mass
tubal ring
empty uterus
free fluid
haemoperitoneum.
Laboratory

Serial β-hCG is supportive but does not diagnose ectopic location by itself.

Clinical suspicion
pelvic/abdominal pain
vaginal bleeding
positive pregnancy test.

RCOG defines ectopic pregnancy as implantation outside the endometrial cavity and recognises tubal, cervical, caesarean-scar, interstitial, ovarian, abdominal and heterotopic forms.

5. Miscarriage
Threatened miscarriage
Initial
vaginal bleeding
TVUS.
Definitive

Viable IUP + bleeding + closed cervix.

Inevitable miscarriage
Initial
bleeding/cramping
pelvic examination
TVUS.
Definitive

Open cervical os with pregnancy loss in progress.

Incomplete miscarriage
Initial
bleeding
pelvic examination
TVUS.
Definitive

Retained products of conception after partial expulsion.

Complete miscarriage
Initial
history
examination
TVUS.
Definitive

Complete expulsion with no retained products and appropriate clinical resolution.

Missed miscarriage
Initial
absent/reduced pregnancy symptoms
TVUS.
Definitive

Ultrasound evidence of non-viable IUP, using strict criteria.

High-yield thresholds:

CRL ≥7 mm + no cardiac activity
MSD ≥25 mm + no embryo

Borderline measurements should not be used alone to diagnose pregnancy failure.

NICE specifically emphasises appropriate ultrasound-based diagnosis of miscarriage.

6. Recurrent miscarriage
Initial

Detailed history + pregnancy records + examination.

Definitive clinical diagnosis

Classically:

≥3 consecutive miscarriages.

Modern practice

Investigation may be considered after 2 miscarriages, depending on circumstances.

Investigations
parental/fetal genetic assessment where indicated
APS testing
uterine assessment
endocrine/metabolic assessment where clinically indicated.
7. Antiphospholipid syndrome
Initial

Clinical history suggesting:

recurrent miscarriage
fetal death
early severe placental disease.
Definitive

Clinical criterion + persistent laboratory antibody positivity.

Laboratory
lupus anticoagulant
anticardiolipin
anti-β2-glycoprotein-I.

Persistence:

positive on 2 occasions ≥12 weeks apart.

PART II — HYPERTENSIVE DISORDERS
8. Chronic hypertension
Initial

Repeated BP measurement.

Definitive

Hypertension:

known before pregnancy, OR
detected before 20 weeks.
Typical threshold

≥140/90 mmHg

9. Gestational hypertension
Initial

BP measurement after 20 weeks.

Definitive

New hypertension ≥20 weeks

without:

proteinuria sufficient for PE
maternal organ dysfunction
uteroplacental dysfunction diagnosing PE.
10. Pre-eclampsia
Initial

BP ≥140/90 after 20 weeks

then assess:

urine protein
FBC/platelets
renal function
liver function
fetal growth
Doppler.
Definitive

New hypertension after 20 weeks plus maternal organ dysfunction and/or uteroplacental dysfunction.

Proteinuria

Examples of significant proteinuria:

PCR ≥30 mg/mmol
ACR ≥8 mg/mmol.
Important

Proteinuria is NOT mandatory.

Pre-eclampsia may be diagnosed with:

renal dysfunction
liver involvement
thrombocytopenia
neurological complications
pulmonary oedema
fetal growth/placental dysfunction.
11. Severe hypertension
Initial

Repeat BP promptly.

Diagnostic threshold

≥160 systolic and/or ≥110 diastolic mmHg.

Definitive

Persistent severe hypertension confirmed by appropriate measurement.

Important

Severe hypertension is a severity marker, not synonymous with severe pre-eclampsia.

12. Eclampsia
Initial

Seizure in pregnancy/postpartum.

Immediate assessment
BP
urine protein
neurological examination
glucose
electrolytes
imaging when indicated.
Definitive

Generalised seizure associated with pre-eclampsia after exclusion of alternative causes.

Important

Can occur:

antepartum
intrapartum
postpartum
even without marked hypertension/proteinuria.
13. HELLP syndrome
Initial

Suspect with:

RUQ/epigastric pain
nausea/vomiting
hypertension
thrombocytopenia.
Definitive

H — Haemolysis

plus

EL — Elevated liver enzymes

plus

LP — Low platelets.

Tests
FBC
AST/ALT
bilirubin
LDH
blood film
haptoglobin.
PART III — DIABETES & METABOLIC OBSTETRICS
14. Gestational diabetes
Initial

Risk assessment / glucose testing according to local pathway.

Definitive

75-g OGTT according to the applicable diagnostic thresholds.

Common UK/NICE thresholds:

fasting ≥5.6 mmol/L
2-hour ≥7.8 mmol/L
Important

Use the local/current testing protocol in clinical practice because diagnostic thresholds vary internationally.

15. Pre-existing diabetes
Initial

Glucose/HbA1c assessment.

Definitive diabetes-range results

Examples:

fasting glucose ≥7.0 mmol/L
HbA1c ≥48 mmol/mol (6.5%), where appropriate
random glucose ≥11.1 mmol/L with compatible clinical context.
PART IV — PRETERM / MEMBRANE CONDITIONS
16. PPROM
Initial

History + sterile speculum examination.

Look for

Pooling of liquor.

Definitive

Evidence of ruptured membranes before labour and before 37 weeks.

If uncertain

Validated biochemical testing can support diagnosis.

17. Term PROM
Initial

History + sterile speculum.

Definitive

Confirmed rupture of membranes:

≥37 weeks + before labour.

18. Preterm labour
Initial
contractions
cervical assessment
gestational age.
Definitive

Regular uterine contractions with progressive cervical change before 37+0 weeks.

Depending on gestation and pathway:

cervical length
fetal fibronectin
speculum examination
may assist diagnosis/risk stratification.
19. Chorioamnionitis / intrauterine infection
Initial

Clinical assessment:

maternal temperature
pulse
fetal heart rate
uterine tenderness
liquor
inflammatory markers where appropriate.
Definitive

Usually clinical diagnosis, supported by:

maternal fever
fetal tachycardia
maternal tachycardia
uterine tenderness
purulent/foul liquor
laboratory/inflammatory evidence.

Histological chorioamnionitis is a pathological confirmation, often retrospective.

PART V — FETAL GROWTH / LIQUOR / MOVEMENT
20. Small-for-gestational-age fetus
Initial

Fundal-height surveillance or risk-based ultrasound.

Definitive

EFW or AC <10th centile.

RCOG explicitly distinguishes SGA from pathological FGR.

21. Fetal growth restriction — FGR
Initial
small EFW/AC
reduced growth velocity
risk factors
abnormal Doppler.
Definitive diagnosis

Evidence that fetal growth is pathologically restricted, using:

serial ultrasound
EFW/AC
growth velocity
umbilical artery Doppler
MCA/CPR where appropriate
liquor
maternal/fetal risk factors.
Critical distinction

SGA ≠ FGR.

RCOG describes SGA commonly as EFW or AC <10th centile, whereas FGR represents pathological restriction of genetic growth potential.

22. Severe SGA
Diagnostic threshold

EFW <3rd centile

is a major marker of significant smallness.

23. Oligohydramnios
Initial

Ultrasound.

Definitive

Common criteria:

single deepest pocket <2 cm

or

AFI ≤5 cm.
Preferred modern assessment

SDP is generally more useful than AFI in many singleton settings.

24. Polyhydramnios
Initial

Ultrasound.

Definitive

Common thresholds:

SDP ≥8 cm
or
AFI ≥24–25 cm depending on reference system.
25. Reduced fetal movements
Initial

Maternal history.

Ask:

when movement changed
pattern
complete absence vs reduction.

Then:

CTG according to gestation/clinical pathway
ultrasound where indicated.
Definitive diagnosis

There is no single universal kick-count number that diagnoses/excludes fetal compromise.

Diagnosis begins with maternal perception of reduced/change in movements.

PART VI — PLACENTAL DISORDERS
26. Placenta praevia
Initial

Routine obstetric ultrasound.

Definitive

TVUS demonstrating placental location relative to internal cervical os.

Assess:

placental edge
internal os
relationship between them.
Important

TVUS is safe and more accurate than transabdominal scanning for placental location.

RCOG's current guideline on placenta praevia/PAS was updated June 2026.

27. Placenta accreta spectrum
Initial

Identify risk:

placenta praevia
previous caesarean
previous uterine surgery.

Then expert ultrasound.

Definitive diagnosis

Expert ultrasound ± colour Doppler, with MRI as an adjunct in selected cases.

Definitive pathological confirmation

Placental invasion confirmed histologically after delivery/hysterectomy.

Important

Do not say:

“PAS requires previous caesarean + placenta praevia.”

It can occur after other uterine trauma including curettage, hysteroscopy, myomectomy, endometrial ablation and uterine artery embolisation.

28. Placental abruption
Initial

Clinical diagnosis.

Assess:

pain
bleeding
uterine tenderness/rigidity
fetal heart
maternal haemodynamic status.
Definitive

Usually remains a clinical diagnosis.

Placental histopathology may confirm retrospectively.

Critical point

Normal ultrasound does NOT exclude abruption.

29. Vasa praevia
Initial

Ultrasound.

Definitive

TVUS + colour Doppler demonstrating fetal vessels crossing/near the internal os.

30. Umbilical cord prolapse
Initial
sudden fetal bradycardia
cord seen/felt after ROM.
Definitive

Cord palpated/visualised below or alongside the presenting part after membrane rupture.

PART VII — LABOUR
31. Established labour
Initial

History:

painful contractions
frequency
duration
show/ROM.
Definitive

Regular contractions with progressive cervical dilatation/effacement.

32. Active/established first stage
Initial

Vaginal examination.

Diagnostic assessment
cervical dilatation
effacement
station
position
contraction pattern.

Modern labour definitions should be interpreted according to the current guideline rather than relying on the old universal “4 cm = active labour” rule.

33. Prolonged/slow labour
Initial

Partogram/clinical assessment.

Definitive

Diagnosis requires assessment of:

cervical progress
fetal descent
contraction adequacy
parity
maternal/fetal condition.

Do not diagnose solely from a single cervical dilatation rate.

34. Obstructed labour
Initial
poor descent
poor cervical progress
caput
moulding
maternal exhaustion
abnormal FHR.
Definitive

Clinical evidence of mechanical obstruction preventing progress despite adequate uterine activity/time.

Common causes:

CPD
malposition
malpresentation
pelvic mass.
35. Shoulder dystocia
Initial

Failure of shoulders after head delivery.

Definitive diagnostic criterion

Shoulders fail to deliver with routine gentle axial traction and additional manoeuvres are required.

Classic:

turtle sign
failure of restitution
difficulty delivering anterior shoulder.
36. Breech presentation
Initial

Abdominal palpation.

Definitive

Ultrasound confirming buttocks/feet as presenting part.

Types:

frank
complete
footling.
37. Transverse lie
Initial

Leopold manoeuvres.

Definitive

Ultrasound demonstrating transverse fetal long axis.

PART VIII — POSTPARTUM
38. Postpartum haemorrhage
Initial

Estimate blood loss + maternal clinical condition.

Assess:

pulse
BP
uterine tone
bleeding
placenta
genital tract trauma.
Traditional diagnostic threshold

≥500 mL after vaginal birth.

≥1000 mL after caesarean birth.

Major PPH

≥1500 mL

Important

Modern management should respond to ongoing bleeding and physiological deterioration, not wait for a numerical threshold.

39. Secondary PPH
Initial

History + examination + ultrasound when indicated.

Definition

Abnormal/excessive genital tract bleeding:

from 24 hours to 12 weeks postpartum.

Differential
retained products
endometritis
subinvolution
vascular lesions.
PART IX — GYNAECOLOGICAL BLEEDING
40. Heavy menstrual bleeding
Initial

History + menstrual bleeding/QOL assessment.

Initial investigations
FBC
pregnancy test where relevant
targeted investigations based on history.
Definitive diagnosis

There is no universal required measured blood-loss volume.

NICE defines management around the woman's perception and impact on quality of life rather than requiring quantified menstrual blood loss.

Then determine cause using PALM-COEIN.

41. Abnormal uterine bleeding — PALM-COEIN
Initial

History + examination + pregnancy test + FBC.

Definitive diagnosis

Depends on cause:

PALM

Polyp → hysteroscopy/US ± histology
Adenomyosis → TVUS/MRI
Leiomyoma → ultrasound
Malignancy/hyperplasia → histology

COEIN

Coagulopathy → coagulation/haematology assessment
Ovulatory dysfunction → clinical/endocrine assessment
Endometrial → exclusion/clinical diagnosis
Iatrogenic → medication/device history
Not otherwise classified → targeted investigation.
42. Amenorrhoea
Initial

Pregnancy test.

Then:

FSH/LH
prolactin
TSH
androgen testing where indicated
pelvic ultrasound.
Definitive

Cause-specific diagnosis.

43. Primary amenorrhoea
Initial

History + pubertal development + examination + pregnancy test where relevant.

Investigation
pelvic ultrasound
FSH/LH
karyotype in selected cases
prolactin/TSH
androgen assessment.
Definitive

Depends on cause:

Müllerian agenesis → imaging
androgen insensitivity → karyotype/hormonal/genetic assessment
gonadal dysgenesis → endocrine/genetic assessment.
44. Secondary amenorrhoea
Initial

Pregnancy test.

Then:

FSH
LH
prolactin
TSH
ultrasound.
Definitive

Underlying cause established through targeted evaluation.

PART X — PCOS / ENDOCRINE
45. PCOS
Initial

History:

irregular cycles
hirsutism
acne
infertility.

Laboratory:

testosterone/androgens
metabolic assessment.
Definitive

2 of 3 Rotterdam criteria:

oligo-/anovulation
clinical/biochemical hyperandrogenism
polycystic ovarian morphology

AND exclusion of alternative diagnoses.

Important

All 3 are not required.

46. Hyperandrogenism
Initial

Clinical:

hirsutism
acne
alopecia.
Definitive

Biochemical androgen excess using reliable assays:

total testosterone
free testosterone/FAI where appropriate.

Exclude:

CAH
androgen-secreting tumour
Cushing syndrome
thyroid disease
hyperprolactinaemia.
47. Hirsutism
Initial

Clinical examination.

Quantification

Modified Ferriman-Gallwey score can quantify hair growth.

Definitive

Clinical hirsutism ± biochemical androgen excess.

PART XI — ENDOMETRIOSIS / ADENOMYOSIS / FIBROIDS
48. Endometriosis
Initial

Clinical suspicion from:

dysmenorrhoea
chronic pelvic pain
deep dyspareunia
cyclical bowel symptoms
cyclical urinary symptoms
infertility.
First-line investigation

TVUS.

Definitive diagnosis

Modern NICE practice permits a clinical/imaging diagnosis and treatment without mandatory diagnostic laparoscopy.

Laparoscopy can provide surgical confirmation, particularly where imaging is negative/inconclusive and suspicion remains or operative treatment is needed.

Critical trap

Normal TVUS does NOT exclude endometriosis.

NICE recommends ultrasound for suspected endometriosis and does not recommend CA-125 as a diagnostic test.

49. Endometrioma
Initial

TVUS.

Definitive

Typical ultrasound appearance:

homogeneous “ground-glass” ovarian cyst.
Histological confirmation

Endometriotic tissue on pathology after surgery.

50. Adenomyosis
Initial

Clinical:

HMB
dysmenorrhoea
bulky/tender uterus.
First-line

TVUS.

Definitive

Characteristic imaging:

globular uterus
heterogeneous myometrium
myometrial cysts
fan-shaped shadowing
abnormal junctional zone.

MRI may be used when ultrasound is inconclusive.

51. Fibroid / leiomyoma
Initial

Pelvic examination + ultrasound.

Definitive

Ultrasound identification of uterine leiomyoma.

Classification

FIGO 0–8 based on anatomical location.

Histology

Provides tissue confirmation after surgery but is not normally necessary for diagnosing a typical fibroid.

52. Endometrial hyperplasia

53. Endometrial carcinoma

Definitive

Endometrial biopsy/histology.

Further

PART XVI — PID / TUBAL DISEASE
62. Pelvic inflammatory disease
Initial

Clinical diagnosis.

pelvic/lower abdominal pain

Definitive

⭐ The most important MRCOG rule

When asked “What is the diagnosis?”, distinguish four levels:

1. SCREENING TEST
→ identifies risk

2. INITIAL INVESTIGATION
→ first test after clinical suspicion

3. DIAGNOSTIC TEST
→ establishes the disease with adequate certainty

4. DEFINITIVE/GOLD-STANDARD CONFIRMATION
→ highest level of confirmation, often histology, direct visualisation or specific imaging.

For example:

Ovarian cancer: CA-125 = supportive → ultrasound = imaging assessment → CT = staging → histology = definitive diagnosis.

Whereas:

Pre-eclampsia: there is no histological “gold standard”; the diagnosis is clinical, based on new hypertension after 20 weeks plus proteinuria and/or maternal/uteroplacental dysfunction.

And:

Endometriosis: laparoscopy is capable of direct surgical confirmation, but modern NICE practice does not require diagnostic laparoscopy for every woman; TVUS is recommended during diagnostic assessment and a normal scan does not exclude disease.

This distinction between screening → initial → diagnostic → definitive is the framework I would use for the entire MRCOG/FCPS OBGYN diagnostic atlas.
