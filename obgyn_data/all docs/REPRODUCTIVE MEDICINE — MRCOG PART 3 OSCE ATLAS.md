REPRODUCTIVE MEDICINE — MRCOG PART 3 OSCE ATLAS

This is structured as an MRCOG Part 3 station guide: what to ask, what to examine, what numbers to know, how to interpret results, common differentials, investigations, management, counselling, and the “why?” behind each finding.

Exam principle: In reproductive medicine, do not jump directly to “ovarian reserve” or IVF. Demonstrate a couple-based, systematic fertility assessment: ovulation → ovarian reserve/age → tubal/uterine factors → endometriosis → male factor → endocrine causes → treatment according to cause and duration. NICE specifically recommends assessing both partners and using the clinical history to guide investigations.

1. INFERTILITY / SUBFERTILITY
Definition
Primary infertility

No previous pregnancy despite regular unprotected intercourse.

Secondary infertility

Difficulty conceiving after a previous pregnancy, regardless of outcome.

Important OSCE definition

Traditionally:

12 months of regular unprotected intercourse without conception.

But investigate earlier when there are risk factors such as:

age ≥36 years
irregular/absent periods
previous pelvic infection
previous ectopic pregnancy
previous pelvic surgery

sexual dysfunction.
Frequency of intercourse

Ask:

Previous contraception?

A practical recommendation:

intercourse every 2–3 days throughout the cycle optimises chance of conception.

4. OVULATION ASSESSMENT
History

Evidence suggesting ovulation:

regular cycles
premenstrual symptoms
mittelschmerz
cyclical breast tenderness
cervical mucus changes.

But symptoms alone do not prove ovulation.

Mid-luteal progesterone

Classic OSCE point:

Day 21 progesterone

Only appropriate for:

28-day cycle.

For other cycles:

Measure approximately 7 days before the expected next period.

Examples:

Cycle	Approximate progesterone timing
28 days	Day 21
30 days	Day 23
35 days	Day 28

NICE specifically recommends timing progesterone according to cycle length rather than blindly using day 21 in women with prolonged/irregular cycles.

Interpretation

A clearly elevated mid-luteal progesterone supports ovulation.

Common exam teaching:

>30 nmol/L → strong evidence of ovulation.
Lower values do not necessarily mean anovulation if sampling was mistimed.

Important MRCOG point: do not interpret one low progesterone as definitive anovulation.

8. OVARIAN RESERVE
AMH

Anti-Müllerian hormone reflects:

pool of growing follicles
ovarian response to stimulation.
Low AMH

Suggests:

reduced ovarian reserve.

Possible causes:

increasing age
POI
previous ovarian surgery
chemotherapy
radiotherapy.
High AMH

Common in:

PCOS.
Crucial OSCE distinction

AMH does NOT reliably predict natural fertility.

It is primarily useful for:

predicting ovarian response to stimulation
identifying risk of excessive response/OHSS
planning ART.

Do not tell a patient:

“Your AMH is low, therefore you cannot get pregnant.”

That is incorrect.

12. MALE INFERTILITY
Semen analysis

First-line male investigation.

Important parameters:

Current WHO reference limits vary with edition/laboratory; for MRCOG, know the commonly used WHO 2021 lower reference limits:

Parameter	Lower reference value
Volume	≥1.4 mL
Sperm concentration	≥16 million/mL
Total sperm number	≥39 million/ejaculate
Total motility	≥42%
Progressive motility	≥30%
Normal morphology	≥4%
Vitality	≥54%
pH	≥7.2
OSCE warning

These are reference limits, not a fertility/infertility cut-off.

A man below a reference value can still father a child, and a man above all values can still be infertile.

13. SEMEN SAMPLE TECHNIQUE

Tell the patient:

abstinence approximately 2–7 days
collect entire ejaculate
preferably by masturbation
sterile container
avoid lubricants unless fertility-safe
deliver promptly according to laboratory instructions
keep sample near body temperature.

If abnormal:

Repeat semen analysis, generally after approximately 3 months, because spermatogenesis takes about 74 days.

If severe abnormality:

repeat sooner according to fertility-service protocol
examine testes
hormonal testing
consider genetic testing.

2. Ovulation induction

Current international guidance generally favours:

Letrozole as first-line pharmacological ovulation induction for anovulatory infertility in PCOS when there are no other infertility factors.

Older MRCOG material may mention clomifene as first-line, so know the distinction between older NICE evidence and current international practice. NICE's older fertility evidence describes clomifene/metformin strategies, whereas contemporary PCOS guidance has moved toward letrozole.

Letrozole

Common regimen:

2.5–5 mg daily
usually for 5 days early in cycle
dose adjusted according to response.
Clomifene

Traditional regimen:

50 mg daily × 5 days
increase to 100–150 mg if necessary.

Clomifene can cause:

multiple pregnancy
ovarian enlargement
anti-oestrogenic endometrial/cervical effects.

Older NICE evidence notes approximately 10% multiple pregnancy risk with clomifene and recommends ultrasound monitoring particularly in the first cycle.

Metformin

Useful particularly when:

insulin resistance/metabolic features
glucose intolerance
PCOS.

But it is not the most effective ovulation-induction drug compared with letrozole.

52. HIGH-YIELD MRCOG COUNSELLING PHRASES
When AMH is low

“AMH is primarily a marker of ovarian reserve and expected response to ovarian stimulation. It does not by itself diagnose infertility or predict whether you can conceive naturally.”

When PCOS is diagnosed

“PCOS is a diagnosis of exclusion. I would ensure alternative causes of menstrual irregularity and androgen excess have been considered.”

When prolactin is raised

“I would first confirm the abnormality and exclude physiological, drug-related and secondary causes before assuming a prolactinoma.”

When semen analysis is abnormal

“A single abnormal semen analysis does not establish permanent male infertility; I would repeat the test and assess the man clinically.”

With endometriosis

“I would balance symptom control with reproductive goals and avoid unnecessary ovarian surgery where it could compromise ovarian reserve.”

With POI

“POI significantly reduces fertility but does not mean spontaneous conception is impossible because intermittent ovarian activity can occur.”

53. MRCOG PART 3 — COMMON EXAMINER TRAPS
Trap 1

“AMH 0.8 = infertile.”

❌ Wrong.

Trap 2

“Day 21 progesterone for everyone.”

❌ Wrong.

Use approximately 7 days before the expected next period.

Trap 3

“Polycystic ovaries = PCOS.”

❌ Wrong.

Need the appropriate diagnostic criteria and exclusion of other causes.

Trap 4

“High prolactin = prolactinoma.”

❌ Wrong.

Check pregnancy, medications, hypothyroidism, macroprolactin and other causes.

Trap 5

“Normal semen analysis excludes male infertility.”

❌ Wrong.

Male fertility is multifactorial.

Trap 6

“Endometriosis → laparoscopy immediately.”

❌ Not necessarily.

Use appropriate imaging and individualised management.

Trap 7

“POI means no chance of pregnancy.”

❌ Wrong.

Intermittent ovarian activity can occur, although fertility is markedly reduced.

Trap 8

“Infertility is the woman's problem.”

❌ Major MRCOG communication error.

Always assess both partners.
