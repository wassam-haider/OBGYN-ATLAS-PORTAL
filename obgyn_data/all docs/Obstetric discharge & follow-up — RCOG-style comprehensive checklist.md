Obstetric discharge & follow-up — RCOG-style comprehensive checklist

For MRCOG/MCPS OSCEs, think of discharge as “safe discharge + clear follow-up + safety-netting + communication.” RCOG maternity standards specifically state that postnatal follow-up should be arranged before discharge, with clear escalation/communication pathways; women with complex medical problems should have senior multidisciplinary review and a documented postnatal plan.

Important: RCOG does not have one single universal “discharge follow-up schedule” covering every obstetric patient. Timing depends on the indication and local maternity pathway. The framework below combines current RCOG standards with condition-specific RCOG guidance and is designed for clinical practice/OSCE revision.

15. PPROM / PRETERM PROM — ANTENATAL DISCHARGE

This is an important distinction:

Discharge may be considered only in selected stable patients.

Before discharge:

No infection
No labour
No significant bleeding
Reassuring fetal wellbeing
Appropriate gestational age
Clear plan
Reliable access to hospital
Patient understands warning signs

RCOG patient guidance states that some women with PPROM may be offered discharge after a period of inpatient observation if they are well and not considered at high risk of early birth.

Return immediately for
Fever
Chills/flu-like symptoms
Vaginal bleeding
Green/smelly fluid
Contractions
Abdominal pain
Back pain

RCOG notes that women discharged with PPROM should have regular follow-up, commonly once or twice weekly depending on the clinical situation.

38. THE “5 C” DISCHARGE FORMULA — OSCE

Use this in an exam:

1. CLINICALLY STABLE

“I would confirm maternal and fetal/neonatal wellbeing and ensure there are no ongoing complications.”

2. CONDITION-SPECIFIC PLAN

“I would ensure her specific obstetric complication has an appropriate treatment and follow-up plan.”

3. CONTRACEPTION

“I would discuss postpartum contraception according to her wishes and medical eligibility.”

4. COMMUNICATION

“I would provide a clear written discharge summary and communicate the plan to the GP/community/multidisciplinary team.”

5. CRISIS/Safety-netting

“I would explain clearly which symptoms require urgent assessment and who she should contact.”

This matches the RCOG emphasis on communication, documented care plans, appropriate follow-up and escalation pathways.
