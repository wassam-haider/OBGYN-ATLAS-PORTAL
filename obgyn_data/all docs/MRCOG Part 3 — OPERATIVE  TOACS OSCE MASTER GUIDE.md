MRCOG Part 3 — OPERATIVE / TOACS OSCE MASTER GUIDE

The operative/TOACS stations are not simply “name the instrument and describe the operation.” The examiner is looking for whether you can:

Identify the instrument/procedure.
State its indication.
Recognise contraindications/limitations.
Demonstrate safe technique.
Know important measurements/numbers.
Anticipate complications.
Explain how you would prevent and manage complications.
Communicate appropriately with the patient and obtain valid consent.
Demonstrate awareness of documentation, swab/instrument counts, specimen handling and WHO surgical safety principles.

The MRCOG Part 3 clinical assessment tests application of the Part 2 curriculum and core clinical skills rather than isolated factual recall.

Exam rule: If you are asked to demonstrate an operation on a model, verbalise what you are doing while demonstrating the movements. If the station is a counselling/consent station, do not launch into the operation—first establish understanding, indication, alternatives and patient priorities.

11. EPISIOTOMY
Indications

RCOG notes that episiotomy may be used when the baby needs rapid birth, including instrumental birth, or when serious perineal tearing is a concern.

Mediolateral episiotomy

High-yield number:

Start at the posterior fourchette and direct approximately 60° away from the midline at the time of incision.

Why?

Because an incision made at approximately 60° is associated with a lower risk of extension toward the anal sphincter than a midline episiotomy.

72. OPERATIVE BLOOD LOSS — NUMERICAL PEARLS
Obstetric haemorrhage

Traditional PPH:

vaginal birth ≥500 mL
caesarean ≥1000 mL.

But clinical deterioration matters more than waiting for a threshold.

Shock index

Shock index = heart rate / systolic BP

Normal non-pregnant range approximately:

0.5–0.7

A rising shock index in obstetric haemorrhage is concerning.

Example:

HR 120 / SBP 90 =

1.33 → highly concerning.

73. URINE OUTPUT

89. MOST COMMON MRCOG PART 3 OPERATIVE EXAMINER TRAPS
Trap 1

“Perform a vacuum delivery.”

Don't immediately put on the cup.

First say:

“I would confirm full dilatation, ruptured membranes, engagement, position, station, absence of contraindications, adequate analgesia, empty bladder and informed consent.”

Trap 2

“Repair this perineal tear.”

Don't start suturing.

First:

“I would inspect the entire perineum, vagina and cervix and perform a rectal examination to exclude OASI.”

Trap 3

“This is a 3rd-degree tear.”

Don't simply say “repair it.”

Classify:

3a / 3b / 3c / 4th degree.

Trap 4

“There is PPH despite uterotonics.”

Don't jump straight to hysterectomy.

Say:

“I would simultaneously assess the 4 Ts and escalate through uterine tamponade and surgical measures while activating the major haemorrhage protocol.”

Trap 5

“Perform hysterectomy.”

Examiner wants anatomy.

Say:

“My key structures to identify and protect are the ureters, uterine vessels, bladder and bowel.”

Trap 6

“Perform ovarian cystectomy.”

Mention:

“I will aim to preserve maximal healthy ovarian tissue and minimise thermal damage because ovarian reserve is important.”

Trap 7

“Perform laparoscopy.”

Mention:

“I would assess previous abdominal surgery and entry risk before choosing the safest entry technique.”

90. GOLDEN MRCOG OPERATIVE PHRASES

Memorise these.

Before cutting

“I have confirmed the indication, consent, patient identity, allergies and relevant investigations.”

Before instrument application

“I have confirmed the anatomy and prerequisites and will use the instrument in a controlled manner.”

During surgery

“I will maintain good exposure, identify anatomy, minimise tissue trauma and maintain haemostasis.”

Before closure

“I will confirm haemostasis, inspect for organ injury, complete swab/needle/instrument counts and ensure correct specimen handling.”

If complication occurs

“I would recognise the complication, stop the procedure where appropriate, call for senior assistance, stabilise the patient and manage the injury definitively.”

If you don't know

Never invent.

Say:

“I would refer to the relevant local guideline/device instructions and seek senior assistance before proceeding.”

That is much safer than giving a falsely precise number.

⭐ THE OPERATIVE STATION MASTER ALGORITHM

For virtually every MRCOG Part 3 operative station, mentally run:

INDICATION → CONSENT → CONTRAINDICATIONS → PREPARATION → ANAESTHESIA → POSITION → ANTIBIOTICS/VTE → ANATOMY → INSTRUMENT → TECHNIQUE → NUMBERS → COMPLICATIONS → HAEMOSTASIS → COUNTS → SPECIMEN → POST-OP → FOLLOW-UP

And for obstetric procedures:

MOTHER + BABY + SPEED + SAFETY + BLOOD LOSS + FUTURE FERTILITY

The RCOG specifically emphasises prompt recognition and repair of OASI because delayed diagnosis can worsen long-term outcomes.
