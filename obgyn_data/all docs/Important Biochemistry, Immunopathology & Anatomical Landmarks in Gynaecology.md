Important Biochemistry, Immunopathology & Anatomical Landmarks in Gynaecology
High-yield MCPS/MRCOG Part 2/3 + OSCE revision

Below is a gynaecology-focused high-yield list, emphasizing normal values, pathological changes, mechanisms, clinical correlations and examination/surgical landmarks.

1. REPRODUCTIVE ENDOCRINE BIOCHEMISTRY
A. Hypothalamic–pituitary–ovarian axis

GnRH → LH/FSH → ovary → oestradiol/progesterone/inhibin → feedback

GnRH
Produced by hypothalamus.
Pulsatile secretion is essential.
Rapid/high-frequency pulses → relatively more LH.
Slow-frequency pulses → relatively more FSH.
Continuous GnRH → pituitary GnRH-receptor downregulation → ↓LH/FSH.
Clinical applications

Continuous GnRH agonists:

Leuprorelin
Goserelin
Triptorelin

Initially:

↑LH/FSH → flare

Then:

receptor downregulation → ↓LH/FSH → hypo-oestrogenic state.

Used in:

Fibroids

Precocious puberty
2. FSH
Main actions

FSH acts primarily on granulosa cells.

Stimulates:

Follicular growth
Aromatase activity
Conversion of androgens → oestradiol
Inhibin B production
FSH and ovarian reserve

Early follicular-phase FSH:

Usually measured day 2–5.
Higher FSH can indicate reduced ovarian reserve.

But:

FSH has substantial cycle-to-cycle variation.
AMH and AFC are generally more useful markers of ovarian reserve.
3. LH

LH acts primarily on theca cells.

LH → theca cells → androgen production.

Androgens diffuse to granulosa cells:

Androgen → aromatase → oestradiol

LH surge

Sustained high oestradiol causes positive feedback.

→ LH surge
→ ovulation
→ luteinisation
→ corpus luteum formation.
