Key

1. NORMAL / GENERAL PREGNANCY
Condition	Ideal timing of birth
Uncomplicated singleton pregnancy	40+0 weeks; spontaneous labour acceptable
Uncomplicated pregnancy approaching 41 weeks	Offer induction by 41+0 weeks under NICE
Previous uncomplicated LSCS — planned ERCS	≥39+0 weeks
Previous CS + planned VBAC	Await spontaneous labour unless another indication
Maternal request for elective CS without medical indication	≥39+0 weeks
Obesity alone	No routine early delivery solely for obesity
Well-controlled hypothyroidism	No alteration in timing
Well-controlled hyperthyroidism	No routine early birth solely for thyroid disease
Well-controlled epilepsy	No routine early birth solely because of epilepsy

RCOG specifically states that well-controlled thyroid disease should not alter when/how birth occurs, and epilepsy alone is not an indication for early birth or planned CS.

2. HYPERTENSIVE DISORDERS
Chronic hypertension
Situation	Timing
Chronic HTN, BP <160/110, no complications	Do not routinely deliver <37 weeks
≥37 weeks	Timing individualized with senior obstetrician
BP ≥160/110 despite treatment / complications	Earlier birth may be required
Superimposed pre-eclampsia	Follow pre-eclampsia timing

NICE states that planned early birth should not routinely be offered before 37 weeks when BP is below 160/110 mmHg.

Gestational hypertension
Situation	Timing
BP <160/110 and uncomplicated	No planned birth <37 weeks
≥37 weeks	Plan birth according to maternal/fetal circumstances
Severe hypertension / maternal or fetal compromise	Earlier delivery

6. FETAL GROWTH RESTRICTION / SGA

This is one of the most important RCOG timing questions.

First distinguish:

SGA

EFW or AC <10th centile

FGR

Pathological failure to reach genetic growth potential, particularly when accompanied by abnormal Doppler/other evidence of compromise.

Timing depends on Doppler.
Fetal condition	Typical timing
SGA, normal UA Doppler, no other concern	Around 39 weeks / by 40 weeks
FGR with abnormal UA Doppler	Earlier, commonly 37 weeks range depending on severity
Absent/reversed end-diastolic flow	Much earlier delivery according to Doppler/CTG/DV and gestation
Abnormal DV / severe fetal compromise	Urgent delivery depending on gestation
Non-reassuring CTG	Immediate delivery

RCOG emphasizes that delivery timing in FGR must integrate umbilical artery, MCA and ductus venosus Doppler, fetal surveillance and gestational age, rather than applying one universal week.

RCOG's updated 2026 guidance also confirms that SGA and FGR should not simply be treated as synonymous.

PPROM + established labour	Manage labour
PPROM + GBS carriage	RCOG patient guidance indicates birth may be advised from 34 weeks

RCOG's current patient guidance says that if mother and baby remain well without infection, pregnancy may be continued until 37 weeks; known GBS carriage may alter this to birth from 34 weeks.

⚠️ One crucial RCOG exam warning

Do not learn statements such as:

“FGR = deliver at 37 weeks.”

That is too simplistic.

Similarly:

“Pre-eclampsia = deliver at 34 weeks.”

Also incorrect as a universal rule.

RCOG/NICE timing is condition + severity + maternal status + fetal surveillance + Doppler + gestational age. This is particularly important for FGR, pre-eclampsia, PPROM, ICP, multiple pregnancy and placental disease. RCOG's current Green-top library itself contains guidelines at different stages of update, so the exact source matters.

If you want this for MRCOG, the next useful version would be a single giant “Obstetric condition → cutoff/threshold → investigation → treatment → timing of delivery → mode of delivery” table, including all the numerical cut-offs (BP, proteinuria/PCR, bile acids, MCA-PSV, UA Doppler, EFW centiles, cervical length, AFI/SDP, glucose targets, twin chorionicity, placenta distance from os, etc.).
