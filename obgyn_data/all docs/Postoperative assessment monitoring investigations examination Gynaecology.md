Post-operative assessment, monitoring, investigations & examination — Gynaecology

Below is a comprehensive MRCOG/OSCE-oriented postoperative framework for non-obstetric gynaecology, aligned with the RCOG postoperative-care curriculum and RCOG gynaecology standards. RCOG expects competence in immediate, short- and long-term postoperative care, recognition/prevention of complications, investigations, management and follow-up.

Scope: hysteroscopy, D&C/endometrial sampling, laparoscopy, ovarian/tubal surgery, myomectomy, hysterectomy, endometriosis surgery, prolapse/urogynaecology, vulval/vaginal surgery, oncology surgery and other benign gynaecological procedures. Obstetric procedures are excluded.

52. COMPLICATION → FINDING → INVESTIGATION
Complication	Typical findings	First investigations
Haemorrhage	Tachycardia, hypotension, bleeding, pallor	FBC, group/crossmatch, coagulation ± imaging
Infection/sepsis	Fever, tachycardia, pain, discharge	FBC, CRP, lactate, cultures, imaging/source assessment
Wound infection	Redness, warmth, discharge	Clinical ± wound culture
Pelvic collection	Fever, pelvic pain, discharge	US ± CT
Ileus	Distension, vomiting, no flatus	U&E ± abdominal imaging
Bowel injury	Peritonism, sepsis, distension	FBC/CRP/lactate + CT ± surgical assessment
Urinary retention	Unable to void, suprapubic pain	Bladder scan
Bladder injury	Haematuria, leakage, oliguria	Urinalysis, renal function, imaging/cystoscopy as appropriate
Ureteric injury	Flank pain, oliguria, urinary leakage	Creatinine, imaging ± CT urogram
DVT	Unilateral leg swelling/pain	Compression ultrasound
PE	Dyspnoea, chest pain, tachycardia/hypoxia	ECG, CXR ± CTPA according to pathway
Electrolyte disturbance	Weakness, confusion, arrhythmia	U&E
Anaemia	Fatigue, tachycardia, dizziness	FBC
Wound dehiscence	Wound separation, discharge	Clinical assessment ± urgent surgical review
Vault/cuff dehiscence	Pelvic pain, bleeding, discharge, tissue prolapse	Urgent pelvic assessment + surgical review
53. THE MRCOG POST-OP OSCE ANSWER

If the examiner says:

"Please assess this postoperative gynaecology patient."

A strong opening is:

"I would first review the operation, anaesthetic record, intraoperative findings, blood loss, complications, drains, catheter and postoperative instructions. I would assess the patient using an ABCDE approach, review observations and fluid balance, assess pain and bleeding, examine the abdomen and wound, assess urinary and bowel function, assess for infection and thromboembolism, and investigate selectively according to my clinical findings. I would then formulate a postoperative plan including analgesia, thromboprophylaxis, mobilisation, nutrition, catheter/drain management, histology and discharge/follow-up."

That structure directly addresses the RCOG expectation of immediate, short- and long-term postoperative assessment, prevention/recognition of complications, investigation, management and follow-up.
