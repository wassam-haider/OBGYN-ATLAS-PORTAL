Presentation

Classically:

Sudden unilateral pelvic pain

Severe pain

Nausea/vomiting

Adnexal mass may be present

Pain may be intermittent due to intermittent torsion/detorsion

Examination

Unilateral lower abdominal tenderness

Guarding may occur

Adnexal tenderness/mass

Diagnosis

TVUS

Enlarged ovary

Oedematous ovary

Peripheral follicles

Ovarian mass

Free fluid

Reduced/absent venous flow may occur

Reduced/absent arterial flow may occur

Critical exam point

Normal Doppler flow does NOT exclude torsion.

Clinical suspicion remains important.

Management

Suspected torsion = urgent gynaecological review.

Usually:

Analgesia

Antiemetic

IV access

NPO

Urgent theatre if significant suspicion

Surgery

Usually laparoscopic:

Detorsion

Preserve ovary whenever feasible

Cystectomy if appropriate

Oophorectomy only when ovarian tissue is clearly nonviable or malignancy/other indication dictates

Follow-up

Histology if tissue removed

Review pathology

Assess contralateral ovary when relevant

Recurrence counselling

Fertility discussion

4

ACUTE AUB / MASSIVE NON-PREGNANCY UTERINE BLEEDING 🔴

This is one of the most important ACOG emergency topics.

ACOG recommends:

➔ Stabilize → identify cause → stop bleeding → prevent recurrence.

ACOG classifies causes using PALM-COEIN.

PALM

Polyp

Adenomyosis

Leiomyoma

Malignancy/hyperplasia

COEIN

Coagulopathy

Ovulatory dysfunction

Endometrial

Iatrogenic

Not otherwise classified

Assessment

History

Quantity/duration

Clots

Flooding

Soaking pads/hour

Previous episodes

Menstrual history

Intermenstrual/postcoital bleeding

Fibroids

PCOS/anovulation

Medications

Anticoagulants

Bleeding disorder

Previous PPH/surgical bleeding

Easy bruising/epistaxis

Family history

Examination

Vital signs

Pallor

Shock

Abdomen

Speculum

Bimanual examination

Look for:

Cervical lesion

Vaginal bleeding

Trauma

Enlarged/irregular uterus

Fibroid

Adnexal mass

ACOG specifically recommends pelvic examination to confirm the source of bleeding and assess cervical/vaginal pathology and uterine enlargement.

Investigations

Initial

β-hCG

CBC

Blood group/Rh

Crossmatch if severe

Coagulation screen when indicated

Ferritin when appropriate

U&E

LFT when indicated

TSH when clinically indicated

Bleeding disorder

Consider:

von Willebrand disease

Platelet disorder

Factor deficiency

ACOG highlights the importance of considering an underlying bleeding disorder, particularly with heavy bleeding since menarche or personal/family bleeding history.

Imaging

TVUS ± TAS

Hysteroscopy for suspected intrauterine pathology

Endometrial biopsy

ACOG recommends first-line sampling in AUB at >45 years, and in younger patients with risk factors such as prolonged unopposed estrogen exposure, persistent bleeding or failed medical treatment.

Management
Unstable

ABC + haemorrhage management

Two large-bore IVs

Crystalloid judiciously

Crossmatched blood

Major haemorrhage protocol where indicated

Correct coagulopathy

Urgent senior review

Gynaecology theatre/interventional radiology as appropriate

Stable

Medical treatment is generally first-line.

Options include:

Tranexamic acid

High-dose combined hormonal therapy when appropriate

High-dose progestogen

IV conjugated estrogen in selected patients

ACOG recommends medical therapy for most clinically appropriate patients, with surgery for instability, contraindication or failure of medical therapy.

Surgical/interventional options

Depending on cause and fertility:

Hysteroscopy + polypectomy

Hysteroscopic myomectomy

D&C with hysteroscopy in selected circumstances

Uterine artery embolization

Endometrial ablation

Hysterectomy

Important
D&C alone is not an adequate diagnostic treatment for intrauterine pathology; hysteroscopy is preferable when intracavitary disease is suspected.

SECTION 4

8. BARTHOLIN ABSCESS

Presentation

Severe unilateral vulval pain

Swelling

Difficulty sitting/walking

Dyspareunia

Fever occasionally

Examination

Usually fluctuant tender mass in posterior introitus

Assess for cellulitis/necrosis

Treatment

For abscess:

Drainage

Word catheter is a common first-line approach

Marsupialization for selected recurrent cases

Avoid simple incision-and-drainage alone when a definitive drainage technique is appropriate because recurrence can be higher.

Follow-up

Wound review

Recurrence

Persistent mass

In women ≥40 or postmenopausal, persistent/new Bartholin mass warrants assessment for malignancy and biopsy/excision consideration.
