SECTION 1

Overview & Core Guidance

The universal RED algorithm

For any red presentation, say:

“I would immediately move the woman out of routine triage to an appropriate resuscitation/labour ward area, call for senior help, assess ABCDE, obtain maternal observations and fetal assessment where appropriate, establish IV access, investigate and treat simultaneously, identify the cause, and reassess continuously.”

Remember
➔ CALL → ABCDE → MONITOR → IV ACCESS → BLOOD → FETUS → CAUSE → TREAT → DELIVERY DECISION → REASSESS → DOCUMENT

RCOG specifically describes maternity triage as an emergency portal and emphasises prompt standardised assessment, symptom-specific algorithms and access to senior decision-makers.

SECTION 2

1. 🔴 IMMINENT DELIVERY / ADVANCED LABOUR

Recognise

Red flags:

Crowning

Head visible

Fully dilated and actively pushing

Presenting part on perineum

Urge to push with advanced labour

Previous very rapid labour

Bulging perineum

Involuntary pushing

Step 1 — Call for help

Immediately call:

Senior midwife

Obstetric registrar

Consultant obstetrician if high-risk/emergency

Neonatal team if indicated

Anaesthetic team if complications anticipated

Do not send the woman back to routine triage.

Step 2 — Rapid maternal assessment

ABCDE:

Airway

Breathing

Circulation

Disability

Exposure

Record:

BP

Pulse

RR

SpO₂

Temperature

Pain

Consciousness

Step 3 — Obstetric assessment

Rapidly establish:

Gestational age

Gravida/para

Presentation

Membrane status

Bleeding

Fetal movements

Fetal heart rate

Previous CS/uterine surgery

Known placenta praevia/accreta

Multiple pregnancy

If delivery is imminent, do not delay birth for a prolonged history.

Step 4 — Prepare for birth

Prepare:

Delivery equipment

Neonatal resuscitation equipment

IV access

Blood if haemorrhage risk

Appropriate analgesia/anaesthesia

Newborn team

Step 5 — Look for complications

Immediately consider:

Cord prolapse

Shoulder dystocia

Breech delivery

Fetal bradycardia

Maternal haemorrhage

Uterine rupture

Meconium

Preterm birth

MRCOG phrase

“I would prioritise safe delivery over completing a routine triage assessment because the woman is in advanced labour.”

SECTION 3

2. 🔴 MAJOR ANTEPARTUM HAEMORRHAGE

RCOG defines APH as bleeding from/in the genital tract from 24+0 weeks until birth. Major causes include placenta praevia and placental abruption.

This is one of the most important RED presentations.

Step 1 — Call it an obstetric haemorrhage

Do not underestimate blood loss.

Call:

Senior obstetrician

Consultant

Anaesthetist

Senior midwife

Blood bank/transfusion laboratory

Theatre team if significant

Neonatal team if fetal compromise/preterm birth possible

If major haemorrhage, activate the local major obstetric haemorrhage protocol.

Step 2 — ABCDE

A — Airway

Ensure patent airway.

B — Breathing

RR

SpO₂

Respiratory effort

Give oxygen if clinically indicated by hypoxaemia/critical illness.

C — Circulation

Immediately:

BP

Pulse

Capillary refill

Mental status

Estimate ongoing blood loss

Two large-bore IV cannulas if significant haemorrhage

Step 3 — Blood investigations

Send urgently:

FBC

Group and save

Crossmatch according to severity

Coagulation screen

Fibrinogen

U&E

LFT when clinically appropriate

Repeat blood tests according to ongoing blood loss and clinical condition.

Step 4 — Identify the likely cause

Placenta praevia

Typical:

Painless

Bright-red bleeding

May be recurrent

Soft, non-tender uterus

Placental abruption

Typical:

Painful bleeding

Abdominal/back pain

Tender/tense uterus

Frequent contractions

Fetal compromise

Concealed haemorrhage possible

Vasa praevia

Think:

➔ ROM → vaginal bleeding + acute fetal bradycardia

This is a fetal emergency.

RCOG specifically recognises placenta praevia, abruption and vasa praevia as major obstetric haemorrhage considerations.

🚨 VERY IMPORTANT: DO NOT PERFORM DIGITAL VE

If placenta praevia has not been excluded:

No digital vaginal examination.

A sterile speculum examination may be considered when appropriate, but first stabilise the woman and establish the placental situation.

Step 5 — Fetal assessment

If fetus is viable and maternal condition permits:

Confirm fetal heart

CTG

Assess fetal compromise

If maternal instability or severe fetal compromise:

Do not delay definitive management for investigations.

Step 6 — Decide: expectant management or delivery?

Deliver urgently if:

Maternal instability

Ongoing major haemorrhage

Maternal deterioration

Significant fetal compromise

Term pregnancy with indication for birth

Other obstetric indication

Mode depends on:

Gestation

Maternal condition

Fetal condition

Presentation

Cause of bleeding

Labour status

Placental location

Step 7 — Anti-D

For an RhD-negative woman:

Assess need for anti-D

Kleihauer testing according to gestation/local protocol

Dose anti-D according to local/RCOG guidance

MRCOG GOLDEN STATEMENT

“In APH, maternal resuscitation takes priority, but maternal and fetal assessment occur simultaneously. I would avoid digital vaginal examination until placenta praevia has been excluded and would involve senior obstetric, anaesthetic, transfusion and neonatal teams early.”

SECTION 4

3. 🔴 ECLAMPSIA

Definition

Eclampsia = seizure in pregnancy/postpartum associated with pre-eclampsia, after excluding other causes.

It is an immediate maternal neurological emergency.

Step 1 — Call emergency team

Immediately:

Obstetrician

Anaesthetist

Midwife

Neonatal team if appropriate

Medical/neurology support if diagnostic uncertainty

Step 2 — During seizure

Protect the woman

Do not restrain

Prevent injury

Protect airway

Position appropriately, usually lateral

Suction available

Oxygen if clinically indicated/hypoxaemic

Do not put anything in her mouth.

Step 3 — ABC

After seizure:

Airway

Ensure airway.

Breathing

Assess:

RR

SpO₂

Aspiration

Pulmonary oedema

Circulation

BP

Pulse

IV access

Disability

GCS

Pupils

Glucose

Neurological status

Check glucose because hypoglycaemia can cause seizure.

Step 4 — Magnesium sulfate

Magnesium sulfate is the drug of choice for eclamptic seizures, according to the established RCOG/NICE obstetric emergency pathway.

Use the local IV/IM regimen and monitoring protocol.

Monitor:

Respiratory rate

Oxygen saturation

Reflexes

Urine output

Conscious level

Magnesium toxicity

Watch for:

Loss of patellar reflexes

Respiratory depression

Reduced consciousness

Oliguria

Antidote

Calcium gluconate

Step 5 — Control severe hypertension

If severe hypertension is present:

Treat urgently using the local pregnancy-safe antihypertensive protocol, commonly:

Labetalol

Nifedipine

Hydralazine

Target is safe reduction, not abrupt normalization.

Step 6 — Investigations

Send:

FBC/platelets

LFT

Creatinine/U&E

Urine protein assessment

Coagulation if indicated

Glucose

Consider:

CT/MRI brain

Neurology review

if atypical features, persistent neurological deficit, prolonged/recurrent seizures despite treatment or another intracranial diagnosis is suspected.

Step 7 — Fetal assessment

After maternal stabilisation:

CTG

Fetal assessment

Critical MRCOG point:

Do NOT perform immediate caesarean solely because the woman has had an eclamptic seizure. Stabilise the mother first.

Then decide timing and mode of birth.

SECTION 5

4. 🔴 SEVERE HYPERTENSIVE EMERGENCY

Think RED when there is:

BP

≥160 systolic and/or ≥110 diastolic

especially if persistent or associated with:

Severe headache

Visual symptoms

Epigastric/RUQ pain

Hyperreflexia/clonus

Pulmonary oedema

Neurological symptoms

Abnormal laboratory results

Fetal compromise

Step 1

Move to high-dependency/emergency area.

Call:

Senior obstetrician

Anaesthetist if severe

Medical physician if needed

Step 2 — Confirm BP

Repeat using:

Correct cuff

Correct positioning

Appropriate technique

But do not delay treatment if severe hypertension is confirmed and persistent.

Step 3 — ABCDE

Assess:

Airway

Respiratory status

SpO₂

BP

Pulse

Neurological state

Urine output

Look specifically for pulmonary oedema.

Step 4 — Treat BP urgently

Use pregnancy-compatible emergency antihypertensive therapy according to local protocol.

Common agents:

Labetalol

or

Nifedipine

or

Hydralazine

Step 5 — Assess for severe pre-eclampsia/HELLP

Blood:

FBC

Platelets

AST/ALT

Creatinine

U&E

Urine:

Protein assessment

Clinical:

Headache

Vision

RUQ/epigastric pain

Reflexes

Clonus

Pulmonary oedema

Step 6 — Fetus

CTG

Ultrasound when appropriate

Growth

Liquor

Dopplers where indicated

Step 7 — Magnesium sulfate?

Consider/use according to the severe pre-eclampsia/eclampsia protocol when there is significant risk of eclampsia or neurological features.

Step 8 — Delivery decision

The definitive treatment for pre-eclampsia is delivery, but timing depends on:

Gestational age

Maternal stability

Severe features

Fetal condition

Response to treatment

Cervical status

Availability of neonatal care

MRCOG phrase:

“I would stabilise the mother first, control severe hypertension and prevent/treat seizures, then determine the timing and mode of delivery.”

SECTION 6

5. 🔴 PATHOLOGICAL CTG / ACUTE FETAL COMPROMISE

This is a fetal emergency.

Step 1 — Call for help

Immediately:

Senior midwife

Obstetric registrar

Consultant obstetrician

Anaesthetist if operative delivery likely

Neonatal team

Step 2 — Rapid maternal assessment

Check:

BP

Pulse

SpO₂

Temperature

Pain

Bleeding

Contractions

Step 3 — Identify reversible causes

Think:

Maternal hypotension

Especially after:

Epidural

Spinal

Bleeding

Excessive uterine activity

Oxytocin

Prostaglandins

Cord compression/prolapse

Placental abruption

Uterine rupture

Maternal hypoxia

Fever/sepsis

Aortocaval compression

Step 4 — Immediate corrective actions

Depending on cause:

Stop oxytocin

If excessive contractions/tachysystole.

Correct hypotension

Appropriate IV fluid/vasopressor management according to anaesthetic protocol.

Maternal positioning

Avoid supine aortocaval compression.

Treat hypoxia

If present.

Treat uterine hyperstimulation

Consider acute tocolysis according to local protocol.

Step 5 — Vaginal examination

If indicated, assess:

Cervical dilatation

Station

Presentation

Cord prolapse

Step 6 — Decide whether birth is required

If fetal compromise persists despite corrective measures:

Expedite birth.

Mode depends on:

Cervical dilatation

Station

Presentation

Gestation

Clinical circumstances

Whether vaginal birth can be achieved immediately

MRCOG GOLDEN RULE

“A pathological CTG is not treated by staring at the CTG.”

You:

➔ Recognise → call → assess mother → identify reversible causes → correct → reassess → expedite birth if unresolved.
SECTION 7

6. 🔴 UMBILICAL CORD PROLAPSE

RCOG Green-top Guideline No. 50 specifically covers prevention, diagnosis and management of cord prolapse.

Recognition

Think:

Cord seen at vulva

Cord felt on vaginal examination

Sudden prolonged fetal bradycardia

Variable decelerations immediately after ROM

Sudden abnormal CTG following membrane rupture

Step 1 — Shout for help

Say:

“This is an obstetric emergency. I would call for immediate assistance and prepare for emergency birth.”

Call:

Obstetrician

Theatre team

Anaesthetist

Neonatal team

Step 2 — Relieve cord compression

Elevate presenting part

Use:

Hand vaginally to elevate presenting part

Maintain until delivery.

Step 3 — Maternal positioning

Use a position that reduces compression, e.g.:

Knee-chest

Left lateral with head-down/elevated pelvis as appropriate

Step 4 — Do NOT manipulate the cord unnecessarily

If cord is visible:

Avoid excessive handling.

Do not repeatedly push it back above the presenting part.

Step 5 — Stop oxytocin

If running:

Stop oxytocin.

If uterine hyperstimulation is contributing, consider acute tocolysis according to local protocol.

Step 6 — CTG

Continuous fetal assessment while preparing for birth.

Step 7 — Decide mode of birth

Usually:

Emergency caesarean birth

unless:

Vaginal birth is already imminent

Cervix fully dilated

Head sufficiently low

Birth can be achieved more rapidly and safely vaginally

MRCOG key phrase:

“The objective is to minimise the interval between diagnosis and birth while maintaining maternal and fetal safety.”

SECTION 8

7. 🔴 MATERNAL COLLAPSE

RCOG Green-top Guideline No. 56 covers maternal collapse throughout pregnancy and the puerperium and emphasises that outcome depends on prompt, effective resuscitation.

Causes — think broadly

4 Hs

Hypoxia

Hypovolaemia

Hypo/hyperkalaemia/metabolic causes

Hypothermia

4 Ts

Thrombosis

Tension pneumothorax

Tamponade

Toxins

Plus obstetric causes:

Haemorrhage

Eclampsia

PE

Amniotic fluid embolism

Sepsis

Uterine rupture

Step 1 — Call cardiac arrest/resuscitation team

Immediately activate:

Obstetric emergency

Anaesthesia

Medical/emergency resuscitation team

Neonatal team

Blood bank if haemorrhage

Step 2 — ABCDE

If cardiac arrest:

Start CPR immediately.

Use standard adult ALS principles adapted to pregnancy.

Step 3 — Position

Pregnant women require modification of resuscitation because of aortocaval compression.

Use appropriate manual uterine displacement/left lateral tilt according to resuscitation protocol.

Step 4 — IV/IO access

Establish rapid vascular access.

Send:

FBC

Crossmatch

Coagulation

Fibrinogen

U&E

Glucose

Blood gas/lactate

depending on cause.

Step 5 — Treat the cause simultaneously

Haemorrhage

➔ → Blood/haemorrhage protocol

Eclampsia

➔ → Magnesium sulfate

PE

➔ → PE emergency pathway

Sepsis

➔ → Sepsis bundle

Hypoglycaemia

➔ → Glucose

Anaphylaxis

➔ → Immediate anaphylaxis treatment

Step 6 — Perimortem caesarean/resuscitative hysterotomy

If maternal cardiac arrest occurs in advanced pregnancy and ROSC is not rapidly achieved:

Prepare for resuscitative hysterotomy/perimortem caesarean birth according to the current maternal resuscitation protocol.

The RCOG material historically specifies performing this by approximately 5 minutes when indicated; current practice emphasises starting preparation immediately rather than waiting for an arbitrary timer.

Step 2 — Differential diagnosis

Think:

Step 4 — Massive PE

Massive PE with:

Shock

Severe hypoxaemia

RV dysfunction

is a medical emergency. RCOG recommends multidisciplinary resuscitation management, with treatment decisions including anticoagulation/thrombolysis or surgical approaches according to individual circumstances.

SECTION 10

9. 🔴 MASSIVE/SEVERE SEPSIS

RCOG's current Green-top Guideline No. 64 covers maternal sepsis during the antenatal, intrapartum and postpartum periods.

Recognise

Do not wait for hypotension.

Red flags:

Altered mental state

Tachypnoea

Hypoxaemia

Hypotension

Tachycardia

Fever OR hypothermia

Reduced urine output

High lactate

Severe abdominal/uterine tenderness

Foul liquor

Systemic deterioration

Step 1 — Call senior help

Obstetrician

Anaesthetist/critical care

Microbiology/infectious disease when appropriate

Neonatal team if fetal concerns

Step 2 — Sepsis assessment

ABCDE +:

Lactate

Blood cultures

FBC

U&E

LFT

CRP according to local protocol

Urine

Relevant cultures

Step 3 — Antibiotics

Give prompt broad-spectrum IV antibiotics according to the local pregnancy-safe sepsis protocol.

Do not delay antibiotics while waiting for cultures if the patient is clinically septic.

Step 4 — Source control

Look for:

Chorioamnionitis

Pyelonephritis

Pneumonia

UTI

Wound infection

Intra-abdominal infection

Retained products/postpartum infection

Step 5 — Fluids

Give IV crystalloid if indicated, but reassess frequently, particularly if:

Pre-eclampsia

Cardiac disease

Pulmonary oedema risk

Step 6 — Fetus

If viable:

CTG

Assess fetal condition

Important

Maternal stabilisation remains the priority.

10. 🔴 ACUTE CHEST PAIN / SUSPECTED MASSIVE PE

This deserves its own RED pathway.

Step 1 — ABCDE

Immediately assess:

SpO₂

RR

BP

Pulse

Consciousness

Step 2 — PE clues

Sudden dyspnoea

Pleuritic chest pain

Haemoptysis

Tachycardia

Syncope

Hypoxia

Collapse

Check legs for DVT.

Step 3 — If unstable

Call:

Consultant obstetrician

Anaesthetist

Physician/critical care

Haematology

Appropriate multidisciplinary team

RCOG identifies massive PE with shock/refractory hypoxaemia/RV dysfunction as a medical emergency.

Step 4 — Investigate urgently

Depending on stability:

ECG

CXR

ABG

Echocardiography

Compression ultrasound if DVT symptoms

CTPA/VQ according to the diagnostic pathway

Step 5 — Treatment

If PE is strongly suspected/confirmed, anticoagulation should not be unnecessarily delayed.

For massive PE with haemodynamic collapse, management may require:

IV unfractionated heparin

Thrombolysis

Surgical/embolectomy strategies

depending on the multidisciplinary decision.

SECTION 12

12. 🔴 SHOULDER DYSTOCIA

Although technically a delivery-room emergency rather than a triage presentation, it belongs in your RED emergency list.

Recognise

After delivery of the head:

Head retracts against perineum = turtle sign

and:

Failure of restitution

Failure of shoulders to deliver with normal traction

Step 1 — Announce

“Shoulder dystocia.”

Call:

Obstetrician

Senior midwife

Anaesthetist

Neonatal team

Step 2

Stop maternal pushing temporarily if instructed by team.

Avoid excessive traction on fetal head.

Step 3 — McRoberts manoeuvre

Hyperflex maternal hips.

Step 4 — Suprapubic pressure

Apply appropriate suprapubic pressure.

NOT fundal pressure.

Step 5 — Internal manoeuvres if unsuccessful

Sequentially consider:

Rubin

Woods screw

Delivery of posterior arm

Step 6 — Change maternal position

All-fours/Gaskin manoeuvre where appropriate.

Step 7 — Last-resort manoeuvres

If unresolved:

Intentional clavicular fracture

Cephalic replacement/Zavanelli

Emergency caesarean/laparotomy as appropriate

Step 8 — After delivery

Maternal

PPH

Genital tract trauma

Uterine injury

Baby:

Neonatal resuscitation

Cord gases

Brachial plexus injury

Fractures

Hypoxic injury

🔴 13. RAPIDLY PROGRESSING PRETERM DELIVERY

A woman at very preterm gestation with:

Advanced cervical dilatation

Strong contractions

Bulging membranes

Urge to push

Fetal compromise

should be RED.

Immediate

Call:

Senior obstetrician

Neonatologist

Anaesthetist

Assess:

Gestational age

Presentation

Cervical dilatation

Membranes

Fetal heart

Prepare:

Neonatal resuscitation

Delivery

Antenatal corticosteroids if time and gestational age appropriate

Magnesium sulfate for neuroprotection where indicated

RCOG currently recommends antenatal corticosteroids for women at 24+0–34+6 weeks when imminent preterm birth is anticipated; importantly, birth should not be delayed when maternal or fetal health requires delivery.

🔴 14. VASA PRAEVIA WITH FETAL COMPROMISE

Classic scenario
➔ ROM → painless vaginal bleeding → fetal bradycardia

The mother may appear surprisingly well because the blood loss is primarily fetal.

Immediate actions

Call obstetric emergency

Call neonatal team

Continuous CTG

Maternal IV access

Blood available

Do not delay birth attempting to confirm diagnosis

Emergency caesarean if vaginal birth is not immediately achievable

RCOG material emphasises that fetal exsanguination can occur rapidly after rupture of vasa praevia and that delivery should not be delayed trying to confirm the diagnosis.

MRCOG approach

“It is probably labour.”

Instead:

“I would first exclude life-threatening obstetric and medical causes.”

Then:

➔ ABCDE → maternal observations → fetal assessment → targeted examination → investigations → senior review → definitive treatment.
