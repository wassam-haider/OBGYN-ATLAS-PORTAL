WHO/UKMEC-style eligibility classification

For MRCOG, understand the principle:

Category Meaning
1 No restriction
2 Advantages generally outweigh risks
3 Risks generally outweigh advantages
4 Unacceptable health risk

OSCE phrase:

“I would assess her eligibility using the UKMEC criteria, considering both medical eligibility and her individual preferences.”

4

Contraceptive effectiveness

Typical-use effectiveness is important for counselling.

Long-acting reversible contraception — highest effectiveness

Implant.

LNG-IUS.

Copper IUD.

Failure is generally <1% per year with typical use.

Injection

More user-dependent.

Typical-use failure approximately 4%/year.

Combined hormonal contraception

Typical-use failure approximately 7%/year.

Progestogen-only pill

Typical-use failure approximately 7%/year.

Condoms

Typical-use failure approximately 13%/year.

Fertility awareness methods

Effectiveness varies greatly with method and adherence.

OSCE principle:

“The most effective reversible methods are LARC methods because they remove adherence problems.”

Combined hormonal contraception — CHC

Includes:

Combined oral contraceptive pill.

Combined transdermal patch.

Combined vaginal ring.

Contains:

Oestrogen + progestogen

Mechanism

Main mechanism:

suppresses ovulation.

Also:

thickens cervical mucus.

changes endometrium.

6

Combined pill OSCE counselling

Advantages

Highly effective when used correctly.

Regular, lighter periods.

Reduced dysmenorrhoea.

Reduced menstrual blood loss.

Can improve acne.

Reduced risk of:

May reduce functional ovarian cysts.

Disadvantages

Daily adherence.

Nausea.

Breast tenderness.

Headache.

Breakthrough bleeding.

Mood symptoms may occur.

Increased VTE risk.

8

Blood pressure before CHC

Always demonstrate this in an OSCE.

Technique

Patient seated.

Rest before measurement.

Correct cuff size.

Arm supported.

Repeat elevated reading.

A persistent BP around:

≥160/100 mmHg

is generally incompatible with CHC.

Even lower hypertension may alter eligibility depending on severity and cardiovascular risk.

OSCE phrase:

“I would check blood pressure before prescribing combined hormonal contraception.”

PE

sudden breathlessness

11

Progestogen-only pill — POP

Mechanism

Depending on formulation:

ovulation suppression.

cervical mucus thickening.

endometrial effects.

Advantages

No oestrogen.

Suitable for many women unable to take CHC.

Can be used postpartum.

Suitable during breastfeeding.

Disadvantage

Bleeding pattern unpredictability.

Possible:

amenorrhoea.

irregular bleeding.

prolonged bleeding.

spotting.

12

This is a classic OSCE station.

Ask:

Which POP?

How many pills were missed?

Sexual intercourse since the missed pill?

Any vomiting/diarrhoea?

Enzyme-inducing medication?

The missed-pill window depends on the formulation, so do not give a single number without identifying the POP.

For traditional POP:

approximately 3-hour window.

Desogestrel POP:

approximately 12-hour window.

Contraceptive implant

Common implant:

etonogestrel implant.

Usually effective for 3 years according to licensed duration in many settings.

Mechanism
inhibits ovulation.

thickens cervical mucus.

alters endometrium.

Effectiveness

>99% with correct insertion and continued use.

Two major types:

Copper IUD

Non-hormonal.

18

Copper IUD

Mechanism

Copper produces a local inflammatory reaction toxic to sperm and prevents fertilisation.

Advantages

Highly effective.

Long acting.

Hormone-free.

Rapid return to fertility.

Can be used as emergency contraception.

Disadvantages

May cause:

heavier periods.

longer periods.

increased dysmenorrhoea.

Suspected uterine perforation

OSCE scenario:

Patient develops severe pain during insertion and the device cannot be located.

Management

Stop procedure.

Assess haemodynamic status.

Examine.

Urgent senior review.

Ultrasound/localisation as appropriate.

Further imaging if required.

Gynaecology management.

Do not simply continue manipulating the device.

26

28

Emergency contraception

Two principal options:

Copper IUD

Most effective emergency contraception.

Can be inserted within:

5 days (120 hours)

of unprotected intercourse, or within 5 days of the earliest estimated date of ovulation according to applicable guidance.

It provides ongoing contraception.

Oral emergency contraception

Levonorgestrel

Typically within:

72 hours

of unprotected intercourse.

Effectiveness decreases with increasing interval.

Ulipristal acetate

Within:

120 hours / 5 days.

30

Important ulipristal interaction

After ulipristal acetate, starting/restarting a progestogen-containing contraceptive immediately can reduce ulipristal effectiveness.

Therefore, follow current guidance regarding the interval before starting hormonal contraception and use condoms/abstinence during that interval and until effective contraception is established.

This is a high-yield MRCOG counselling point.

31

Maintain patient autonomy and avoid judgement.

Vasectomy

Important distinction

Postpartum contraception

This is a common MRCOG station.

Ask:

Key principle

Combined hormonal contraception is restricted early postpartum because of VTE risk, particularly during the first weeks.

Progestogen-only methods and IUD/IUS have different postpartum eligibility criteria.

35

Postpartum IUD/IUS

37

Contraception after abortion/miscarriage

Most contraceptive methods can be started immediately after abortion if pregnancy is excluded and there are no contraindications.

IUD/IUS can often be inserted immediately following an uncomplicated abortion.

Avoid insertion when there is:

septic abortion.

significant infection.

Obesity and contraception

Assess:

LARC methods generally retain excellent efficacy in people with obesity.

41

Contraception after bariatric surgery

High-yield.

Consider:

malabsorption.

altered GI anatomy.

VTE risk.

nutritional status.

LARC is often preferable because efficacy does not depend on GI absorption.

Oral contraceptive effectiveness may be affected after certain malabsorptive procedures.

42

Enzyme-inducing drugs

Important examples

carbamazepine.

phenytoin.

phenobarbital.

primidone.

topiramate at relevant doses.

rifampicin.

rifabutin.

St John's wort.

These can reduce effectiveness of some hormonal methods.

Copper IUD and some non-hormonal strategies are not affected by enzyme induction.

43

Contraception + migraine

Migraine without aura

CHC may be possible depending on individual risk assessment.

Migraine with aura

Avoid CHC.

Reason:

increased ischaemic stroke risk.

Progestogen-only methods and IUD/IUS are usually preferable.

44

Contraception + hypertension

Measure BP before CHC.

Severe hypertension

Approximately:

≥160 systolic or ≥100 diastolic

➔ → CHC generally contraindicated.

For moderately elevated BP, eligibility depends on exact readings and overall cardiovascular risk.

45

Contraception + previous VTE

Avoid oestrogen-containing contraception in many women with significant VTE history.

Consider:

LNG-IUS.

copper IUD.

implant.

POP.

Exact UKMEC category depends on:

current vs past VTE.

anticoagulation.

thrombophilia.

recurrence risk.

provoking factors.

46

Contraception + breast cancer

Current breast cancer

Avoid hormonal contraception where possible.

Consider:

copper IUD.

Past breast cancer

Eligibility depends on:

years since diagnosis.

recurrence status.

treatment.

47

Contraception + liver disease

Be particularly cautious with hormonal methods in:

severe/decompensated liver disease.

hepatic adenoma.

hepatocellular carcinoma.

Copper IUD may be an attractive non-hormonal option where appropriate.

Every ~13 weeks.

Useful when oestrogen is unsuitable.

Disadvantages

Delayed return to fertility.

52

DMPA and bone density

Important MRCOG question

DMPA is associated with a reversible reduction in bone mineral density during use.

Don't tell every woman:

“You will develop osteoporosis.”

Instead:

“There can be a temporary reduction in bone mineral density, which generally improves after stopping. I would consider your individual osteoporosis risk factors.”

53

Contraception and STIs

Critical distinction:

Hormonal contraception does NOT protect against STIs.

Condoms reduce transmission of:

HIV.

gonorrhoea.

chlamydia.

some other STIs.

For patients at STI risk:

“I would recommend condoms even if you choose another highly effective contraceptive method.”

54

Pregnancy while using contraception

Always consider:

Ectopic pregnancy

Especially:

pregnancy with IUD/IUS in situ.

However, remember:

An IUD/IUS reduces the overall risk of pregnancy, but if pregnancy occurs with an IUD in situ, the proportion of pregnancies that are ectopic is higher.

63

MRCOG communication phrases to score marks

Shared decision-making

“I can explain the options, but ultimately the choice should be based on your medical eligibility and what fits your priorities.”

Risk communication

“I want to put this risk into perspective. The relative risk may increase, but the absolute risk in a healthy woman is still low.”

If patient refuses

“That's completely your choice. Can I check what concerns you most so I can address them?”

If patient is undecided

“You don't have to decide immediately. I can give you information about the options and we can make a plan that you're comfortable with.”

Safeguarding

“I ask all patients about whether their sexual relationships are consensual and whether anyone is pressuring them, because this is an important part of safe care.”

Safety-netting

“If you develop severe abdominal pain, heavy bleeding, fever, collapse or suspect pregnancy, you should seek urgent medical assessment.”
