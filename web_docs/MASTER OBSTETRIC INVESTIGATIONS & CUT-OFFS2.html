SECTION 1

Overview & Core Guidance

Physiological + pathological + labour + fetal examination + third stage + puerperium + diagnostic criteria

Important
Traditional eponyms are retained because they are commonly examined, but the modern diagnostic criteria are given wherever a formal RCOG/NICE-compatible definition exists. A clinical sign alone should not be treated as a definitive diagnosis when modern ultrasound, laboratory or clinical criteria are required.

NICE's current guidance is particularly important for early pregnancy, labour and hypertensive disorders; RCOG Green-top guidance should be used alongside it for specialist obstetric conditions. For example, NICE defines established labour as regular contractions with progressive cervical dilatation from 4 cm, and modern pre-eclampsia is not diagnosed by the old triad of hypertension + proteinuria + oedema.

SECTION 16

60. ECTOPIC PREGNANCY

Classical triad

Amenorrhoea + abdominal/pelvic pain + vaginal bleeding

But this is not required for diagnosis.

Red flags

Unilateral pain

Shoulder-tip pain

Syncope

Tachycardia

Hypotension

Peritoneal irritation

Adnexal tenderness

Cervical motion tenderness

Shock

Modern diagnostic approach

NICE recommends transvaginal ultrasound to establish pregnancy location and viability.

Definite/high-probability ultrasound features

A tubal ectopic is strongly indicated by an adnexal mass separate from the ovary containing:

Gestational sac + yolk sac, or

Gestational sac + fetal pole ± heartbeat.

High-probability signs include:

Tubal ring/bagel sign

Empty gestational sac in separate adnexal mass

Complex inhomogeneous adnexal mass separate from ovary

These latter findings require correlation with symptoms and hCG rather than being treated as definitive in isolation.

Pregnancy of unknown location

Do not use a single hCG value to determine location.

NICE recommends serial hCG approximately 48 hours apart when appropriate.

62

63

64

Fox's sign

For transvaginal ultrasound:

➔ CRL <7 mm + no heartbeat → repeat scan after ≥7 days before diagnosis
➔ CRL ≥7 mm + no heartbeat → second opinion and/or repeat scan ≥7 days
➔ Mean gestational sac diameter <25 mm + no fetal pole → repeat scan ≥7 days
➔ Mean gestational sac diameter ≥25 mm + no fetal pole → second opinion and/or repeat scan ≥7 days

These thresholds are deliberately conservative to avoid false diagnosis.

SECTION 28

89. PRE-ECLAMPSIA — MODERN DIAGNOSTIC CRITERIA

This is one of the most important corrections to the traditional textbook list.

Diagnosis requires:

A

New hypertension after 20 weeks

≥140 systolic OR ≥90 diastolic

PLUS

B

≥1 new maternal/uteroplacental complication

Proteinuria

PCR ≥30 mg/mmol, OR

ACR ≥8 mg/mmol, OR

Dipstick ≥2+ if quantitative testing unavailable/appropriate.

NICE does not recommend routine 24-hour urine collection for quantification.

OR renal dysfunction

Creatinine ≥90 µmol/L

OR hepatic dysfunction

ALT/AST >40 IU/L

With or without RUQ/epigastric pain

OR neurological complications

Eclampsia

Altered mental status

Blindness

Stroke

Clonus

Severe headache

Persistent visual scotomata

OR haematological complications

Platelets <150 ×10⁹/L

Haemolysis

DIC

OR uteroplacental dysfunction

Fetal growth restriction

Abnormal umbilical artery Doppler

Stillbirth

These are the current NICE diagnostic concepts.

Therefore:

❌ Oedema is NOT diagnostic.

❌ Proteinuria is NOT mandatory.

90

Severe hypertension

≥160 systolic OR ≥110 diastolic

Clinical emergency requiring prompt assessment/treatment.

NICE recommends antihypertensive treatment for severe hypertension and close monitoring.

91

Clinical signs of severe pre-eclampsia

Severe headache

Epigastric/RUQ pain

Hyperreflexia

Pulmonary oedema

Altered mental state

Severe hypertension

92

93

Important

Eclampsia can occur without obvious severe hypertension or significant proteinuria.

SECTION 47

PART XLV — MODERN RCOG/NICE CORRECTIONS TO MEMORISE

1

Pre-eclampsia

❌ Old: hypertension + proteinuria + oedema

✅ Modern: new hypertension after 20 weeks + proteinuria OR maternal/uteroplacental dysfunction.

2

Proteinuria

PCR ≥30 mg/mmol or ACR ≥8 mg/mmol are important NICE thresholds.

3

Severe hypertension

≥160/110 mmHg is a major clinical threshold requiring urgent management.

4

Eclampsia

Not simply "severe pre-eclampsia + seizure."

It is a new-onset seizure associated with a hypertensive pregnancy disorder after alternative causes have been excluded.

5

Established labour

NICE

Regular contractions + progressive cervical dilatation from 4 cm.

6

Ectopic pregnancy

Do not diagnose from the classical triad alone.

TVUS + clinical assessment ± serial hCG are central.

7

Pregnancy of unknown location

A single hCG value does not establish pregnancy location.

8

Miscarriage

Do not diagnose nonviability prematurely from a single borderline ultrasound measurement.

NICE uses conservative repeat-scan criteria around CRL 7 mm and mean sac diameter 25 mm.

9

Shoulder dystocia

Turtle sign is not the diagnostic criterion.

The diagnosis is failure of the shoulders to deliver with normal gentle traction after the head.

SECTION 48

10. DVT

❌ Homan's sign is not a reliable diagnostic/exclusion test.

SECTION 49

11. AFE

Hypoxia + hypotension + DIC is a classic clinical pattern, not a laboratory diagnostic triad.

12

Cullen / Grey Turner / Kehr

Supportive historical signs; low sensitivity and nonspecific.

13

Crede

Do not confuse the historical Credé manoeuvre with modern active management of the third stage.

SECTION 50

14. PPH

Modern NICE terminology defines primary PPH as ≥500 mL within 24 hours, with 500–1000 mL minor and >1000 mL major.

15

Clinical signs ≠ diagnostic criteria

This is the most important exam principle:

An eponym may suggest a diagnosis; a modern diagnostic criterion establishes it.

For MRCOG/FCPS revision, therefore, learn each condition in this sequence:

➔ Clinical presentation → named sign → diagnostic criterion → investigation of choice → severity criteria → differential diagnosis → immediate management.
