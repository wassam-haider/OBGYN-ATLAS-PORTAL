SECTION 1

Overview & Core Guidance

This is structured as an MRCOG Part 3 OSCE answer framework: what to say, what to examine, investigations, numerical thresholds, interpretation, immediate management, counselling and examiner traps.

Guideline note: RCOG's ectopic guideline is currently being updated; the current RCOG page lists the existing Green-top Guideline No. 21, while the update is in development. For practical UK management, NICE NG126 is also important.

SECTION 2

1. UNIVERSAL EARLY-PREGNANCY OSCE APPROACH

Any station involving pain, bleeding, vomiting or a positive pregnancy test:

Step 1 — Safety first

Introduce yourself.

Confirm identity.

Confirm gestational age/LMP.

Explain the purpose of assessment.

Gain consent.

Offer chaperone for intimate examination.

Assess ABCDE if acutely unwell.

Determine whether this is:

haemorrhagic shock

ruptured ectopic

septic miscarriage

severe hyperemesis/dehydration

molar pregnancy with complications.

Immediate observations

Parameter Normal adult reference Concerning
HR ~60–100 bpm >100–120
BP approximately 90–140/60–90 hypotension
RR 12–20/min ≥22 concerning in acute illness
SpO₂ ≥95% generally <94%
Temperature ~36–37.5°C ≥38°C infection
CRT <2 sec ≥2–3 sec
GCS 15 reduced = emergency
Urine output ≥0.5 mL/kg/h oliguria

Do not wait for investigations in an unstable patient.

6

β-hCG — EXAMINER FAVOURITE

Serial β-hCG

Usually measured 48 hours apart when pregnancy location is uncertain.

A normally developing intrauterine pregnancy generally demonstrates a substantial rise; RCOG patient information describes approximately 63% rise over 48 hours as typical. Ectopic pregnancy may show a slower rise, plateau or fall.

Important MRCOG warning

β-hCG alone does NOT diagnose ectopic pregnancy.

Think:

Location = ultrasound

Trend = β-hCG

Do not say:

"The β-hCG is abnormal, therefore it is ectopic."

SECTION 7

7. PREGNANCY OF UNKNOWN LOCATION — PUL

Definition

Positive pregnancy test but no intrauterine or extrauterine pregnancy identified on TVUS.

Possibilities:

Very early IUP

Failed intrauterine pregnancy

Ectopic pregnancy.

Management principle

haemodynamic status first

symptoms take priority over β-hCG

serial β-hCG

repeat TVUS

safety-net.

RCOG notes that early ectopic diagnosis can sometimes take a week or more and that repeat ultrasound may be required.

Definition

SECTION 16

17. ULTRASOUND DIAGNOSIS OF MISSED MISCARRIAGE

Use TVUS.

Important cut-offs

A key modern principle:

Do not diagnose miscarriage from an isolated borderline scan.

For a pregnancy with:

CRL ≥7 mm

and

No visible cardiac activity

➔ → diagnostic of pregnancy failure when confirmed according to appropriate ultrasound criteria.

Mean gestational sac diameter ≥25 mm

with

No embryo

➔ → diagnostic of pregnancy failure.

But in OSCE:

If measurements are close to diagnostic thresholds or dates are uncertain, repeat TVUS rather than making a potentially irreversible diagnosis.

NICE/RCOG practice emphasises cautious ultrasound diagnosis and appropriate repeat scanning.

25. MOLAR PREGNANCY

5

Definition

Complete mole

29

β-hCG IN MOLAR PREGNANCY

β-hCG is often:

markedly elevated

There is no single universal diagnostic number.

Very high levels, particularly >100,000 IU/L, strongly support the diagnosis in the appropriate clinical/ultrasound context.

Why?

Trophoblastic tissue produces hCG.

Very high hCG can cause:

hyperemesis

hyperthyroidism

ovarian theca-lutein cysts.

34

POST-MOLAR hCG SURVEILLANCE

The key principle:

Serial quantitative β-hCG surveillance

Purpose:

detect persistent gestational trophoblastic disease

detect gestational trophoblastic neoplasia.

Plateau/rise

A plateau or rise in β-hCG following evacuation raises concern for persistent disease.

MRCOG phrase

"I would refer to the specialist gestational trophoblastic disease centre and follow their established hCG surveillance protocol."

This is safer than memorising one surveillance schedule because protocols can differ according to complete versus partial mole and current national guidance.

SECTION 36

39. NUMERICAL ASSESSMENT

SECTION 37

40. WEIGHT LOSS

A commonly used marker of significant hyperemesis:

≥5% pre-pregnancy weight loss

particularly when associated with dehydration and inability to eat/drink.

Example:

Pre-pregnancy weight = 80 kg

5% = 4 kg

Therefore:

76 kg or less = ≥5% loss.

SECTION 44

47. CRITICAL THIAMINE RULE

NEVER give IV dextrose to a severely vomiting/depleted patient before thiamine.

Why?

Glucose increases thiamine demand and may precipitate:

Wernicke encephalopathy

Therefore:

Thiamine before dextrose.

Give thiamine to women with prolonged vomiting/poor intake, especially before carbohydrate administration.

SECTION 54

59. HIGH-YIELD NUMBERS TO MEMORISE FOR MRCOG

Ectopic

~6–10 weeks: common period when ectopic is diagnosed

β-hCG commonly assessed 48 hours apart

normal IUP: substantial rise; RCOG patient information gives ~63%/48 h

➔ MTX follow-up: Day 4 → Day 7

expected MTX fall: ≥15%

recurrent ectopic: approximately 7–10%

Miscarriage

➔ CRL ≥7 mm + no heartbeat → pregnancy failure criterion
➔ MSD ≥25 mm + no embryo → pregnancy failure criterion

Do not diagnose borderline cases from a single scan

Hyperemesis

≥5% weight loss = significant

PUQE:

≤6 mild

7–12 moderate

≥13 severe

Na <135

K <3.5

thiamine before dextrose

Molar

β-hCG often >100,000 IU/L

➔ early pre-eclampsia <20 weeks → consider molar pregnancy
➔ previous mole → recurrence approximately 1–2%

60. MRCOG EXAMINER "RED FLAGS"

If you hear:

"Pregnant + collapse + abdominal pain"

➔ → ruptured ectopic

"Pregnant + unilateral pain + shoulder-tip pain"

➔ → haemoperitoneum/ectopic

"Positive pregnancy test + no pregnancy on scan"

➔ → PUL — ectopic remains possible

"Bleeding + fever + offensive discharge"

➔ → septic miscarriage

"Very high β-hCG + snowstorm scan"

➔ → complete molar pregnancy

"Hyperemesis + hyperthyroidism"

➔ → consider hCG-mediated gestational transient thyrotoxicosis/molar pregnancy

"Vomiting + confusion + ataxia"

➔ → Wernicke encephalopathy

"Dextrose before thiamine"

➔ → major OSCE error

"Unstable ectopic + waiting for β-hCG"

➔ → major OSCE error

➔ → major OSCE error
