SECTION 1

1. NORMAL / LOW-RISK PREGNANCY

Condition Preferred timing Preferred mode
Uncomplicated singleton, cephalic Spontaneous labour at term Vaginal birth
Elective caesarean without another indication ≥39+0 weeks Caesarean
Previous uncomplicated 1 LSCS – planned VBAC Await spontaneous labour VBAC
Previous CS – ERCS ≥39+0 weeks Planned CS
Previous vaginal birth Usually await spontaneous labour Vaginal birth
Post-term pregnancy IOL generally offered by 41+0 weeks Induction → vaginal birth if successful

RCOG specifically recommends that elective repeat caesarean is generally performed from 39+0 weeks, because earlier planned CS increases neonatal respiratory morbidity.

SECTION 2

2. HYPERTENSIVE DISORDERS

Gestational hypertension

Severity Timing Mode
BP <160/110, no complications Do not routinely plan birth <37 wk IOL/Vaginal if suitable
≥37 wk Individualised with senior obstetrician IOL usually preferred if suitable
Severe hypertension ≥160/110 or maternal/fetal deterioration Any gestation if indicated IOL or CS depending on urgency

NICE specifically advises against planned early birth before 37 weeks for gestational hypertension when BP is <160/110 and there are no other indications.

Pre-eclampsia

Condition Timing Mode
Pre-eclampsia <34 wk, stable Expectant management if safe Later IOL/CS
34–36+6 wk, stable Individualised IOL if feasible
≥37 wk Birth within 24–48 h IOL or CS
Severe PE/eclampsia/HELLP with maternal compromise Immediate/urgent birth regardless of gestation Often CS if urgent; IOL if safe

RCOG states that pre-eclampsia is ultimately cured by birth, but timing and route depend on maternal and fetal circumstances. NICE recommends initiating birth within 24–48 hours from 37 weeks.

SECTION 3

3. DIABETES IN PREGNANCY

Pre-existing type 1 / type 2 diabetes

Situation Timing Mode
Uncomplicated T1DM/T2DM 37+0–38+6 wk IOL or CS if indicated
Maternal/fetal complications Before 37 wk may be required IOL/CS depending on circumstances
Suspected macrosomia Individualised Discuss vaginal birth vs IOL vs CS

NICE recommends elective birth between 37+0 and 38+6 weeks for uncomplicated pre-existing diabetes.

Gestational diabetes

GDM Timing Mode
Diet-controlled, uncomplicated Await labour, but birth by 40+6 Vaginal/IOL
Medication/insulin-treated Individualised according to control and complications IOL usually possible
Poor control / pre-eclampsia / FGR / other complications Earlier IOL/CS according to indication
Macrosomia Individualised counselling IOL/vaginal/CS

NICE recommends birth no later than 40+6 weeks in uncomplicated GDM and earlier birth when maternal/fetal complications exist.

SECTION 4

4. FETAL GROWTH RESTRICTION / SGA

This is particularly important for MRCOG.

Fetal condition Timing Mode
SGA but normal surveillance Usually 39 wk–40 wk latest Vaginal birth
FGR with abnormal surveillance Earlier Depends on CTG/Doppler
Abnormal umbilical artery Doppler Earlier delivery Often CS depending on severity/gestation
Absent/reversed end-diastolic flow Preterm planned birth often required Frequently CS
Severe fetal compromise Immediate delivery Usually CS if vaginal delivery not imminent

RCOG emphasises that timing is based on growth, Doppler, CTG and overall fetal condition rather than fetal size alone. Its July 2026 update also confirms that RCOG no longer specifically endorses a single fetal growth chart.

Exam pearl

SGA ≠ FGR.

A constitutionally small fetus with reassuring surveillance may safely reach term, whereas true placental FGR with abnormal Doppler may require substantially earlier delivery.

SECTION 5

5. PLACENTAL CONDITIONS

Placenta praevia

Placental location Mode Timing
Placenta ≥20 mm from internal os Vaginal birth may be possible Term if uncomplicated
Placenta <20 mm from internal os at 36 wk CS safest Usually 36–37 wk
Major placenta praevia CS Usually 36–37 wk
Recurrent/heavy APH Earlier delivery CS
Maternal/fetal instability Emergency Emergency CS

RCOG's current patient guidance states that if the placental edge is <20 mm from the cervix at 36 weeks, caesarean is safest; uncomplicated praevia generally has CS around 36–37 weeks, earlier if significant bleeding occurs.

SECTION 6

6. PLACENTA ACCRETA SPECTRUM

Condition Timing Mode
Suspected PAS 35–37 wk, individualised Planned CS in specialist centre
Placenta accreta/increta/percreta with previous CS + praevia Usually planned preterm CS Often CS + planned hysterectomy
Major haemorrhage/instability Immediate Emergency CS ± hysterectomy

RCOG's updated 2026 guidance recommends specialist planning; the timing is generally 35–37 weeks, depending on individual circumstances.

SECTION 7

7. VASA PRAEVIA

This is a classic CS condition.

Condition Timing Mode
Diagnosed antenatally, stable ~34–36 wk Planned CS
Labour/ROM with vasa praevia Immediate Emergency CS
Fetal compromise after vessel rupture Immediate Emergency CS

RCOG currently advises planned CS around 34–36 weeks for antenatally diagnosed vasa praevia.

SECTION 8

8. PLACENTAL ABRUPTION

Situation Timing Mode
Stable mother + live fetus + labour progressing Immediate/appropriate Vaginal birth often preferred
Stable mother + live fetus + vaginal delivery not imminent Urgent CS
Maternal instability/major haemorrhage Immediate CS if fetus alive and vaginal birth not imminent
IUFD Birth required, but vaginal birth preferred unless maternal indication for CS Vaginal
Severe DIC Correct coagulopathy while expediting birth Usually vaginal if fetus dead

Exam pearl

➔ Abruption + dead fetus → vaginal delivery is generally preferred.
SECTION 9

9. MULTIPLE PREGNANCY

DCDA twins

~37 weeks

Vaginal birth possible if Twin 1 cephalic and no contraindication.

CS if obstetric indication.

MCDA twins

~36 weeks

Vaginal birth possible in suitable cases.

CS if indication.

MCMA twins

32–34 weeks

Planned CS generally recommended.

Triplets

~35 weeks

Usually planned CS.

RCOG gives approximately 37 weeks for uncomplicated DCDA twins, 36 weeks for MCDA twins, 32–34 weeks for MCMA twins and 35 weeks for triplets.

10. BREECH PRESENTATION

At 36 weeks, discuss:

ECV

Planned CS

Planned vaginal breech birth in carefully selected circumstances

RCOG notes that ECV is usually performed after 36–37 weeks.
Situation Mode
Breech → successful ECV Vaginal birth
Persistent breech, suitable for vaginal breech birth Planned vaginal breech possible
Persistent breech, unsuitable for vaginal breech birth Planned CS
Footling breech CS generally
Hyperextended fetal neck CS generally
Placenta praevia + breech CS
Fetal anomaly incompatible with vaginal breech CS/individualised
SECTION 11

11. TRANSVERSE / OBLIQUE LIE

Situation Management
Before term Observe
Persistent transverse lie near term ECV may be considered
Persistent transverse lie at labour CS
Transverse lie + ROM Emergency due to cord prolapse risk → usually CS

Persistent transverse lie = caesarean delivery.

SECTION 12

12. PREVIOUS CAESAREAN

One previous lower-segment CS

Two major options:

VBAC

Await spontaneous labour if appropriate.

Continuous intrapartum fetal monitoring.

Immediate access to emergency CS.

ERCS

From 39+0 weeks.

Planned CS.

RCOG explicitly identifies 39+0 weeks as the preferred timing for ERCS.
SECTION 13

13. PRETERM PRELABOUR RUPTURE OF MEMBRANES

There is no single delivery week for all PPROM.

Situation Approach
PPROM without infection/fetal compromise Expectant management when appropriate
Chorioamnionitis Deliver
Fetal compromise Deliver
Placental abruption Deliver
Established labour Deliver
Severe maternal disease Deliver

Mode

PPROM itself is NOT an indication for CS.

If fetus is cephalic and no obstetric contraindication:

Vaginal birth is preferred.

SECTION 14

14. PRETERM LABOUR

Preterm labour itself does not automatically mean CS.

Presentation Mode
Cephalic fetus Vaginal birth generally
Breech/other malpresentation Individualised
Fetal compromise CS if appropriate
Very preterm breech CS may be considered depending on gestation and circumstances
SECTION 15

15. INTRAHEPATIC CHOLESTASIS OF PREGNANCY

This is a very high-yield numerical question.

Bile acids Timing of planned birth
19–39 µmol/L By 40 wk may be considered; expectant management also reasonable if no other risk
40–99 µmol/L 38–39 wk
≥100 µmol/L 35–36 wk

Mode

ICP does NOT itself require CS.

➔ IOL → vaginal birth is appropriate.

Planned CS only for obstetric indication.

RCOG explicitly states that planned CS is not required merely because of ICP.
SECTION 16

16. MATERNAL OBESITY

Obesity alone is NOT an indication for CS.

Situation Timing/mode
Obesity alone Await spontaneous labour
Obesity + diabetes Follow diabetes recommendations
Obesity + pre-eclampsia Follow hypertensive indication
Obesity + macrosomia Individualised
Previous CS VBAC/ERCS based on standard criteria
SECTION 17

17. ANAEMIA

Mild/moderate anaemia

No indication for early delivery or CS.

Treat the cause and correct iron deficiency.

Severe anaemia

Delivery timing is individualised according to maternal condition.

Anaemia alone ≠ CS.

SECTION 18

18. THROMBOCYTOPENIA

Condition Delivery
Gestational thrombocytopenia Usually normal timing/mode
ITP Usually vaginal birth
Severe thrombocytopenia Individualised
HELLP Deliver according to maternal/fetal condition

Thrombocytopenia alone is not an indication for CS.

The major issue is often anaesthetic planning, particularly neuraxial anaesthesia.

SECTION 19

19. THYROID DISEASE

Well-controlled hypothyroidism/hyperthyroidism

No special early delivery solely because of thyroid disease.

Vaginal birth normally appropriate.

Uncontrolled severe thyrotoxicosis

Maternal stabilisation is essential; timing/mode depends on maternal/fetal condition.

SECTION 20

20. RENAL DISEASE

Stable chronic kidney disease:

No automatic CS.

Timing individualised according to renal function, BP, fetal growth and maternal condition.

If severe maternal deterioration or superimposed pre-eclampsia:

Deliver according to maternal/fetal indication.

SECTION 21

21. SYSTEMIC LUPUS / AUTOIMMUNE DISEASE

Stable disease:

No automatic early birth or CS.

Delivery depends on:

disease activity

hypertension/pre-eclampsia

FGR

fetal surveillance

maternal organ involvement.

SECTION 22

22. ANTIBODY-RELATED PREGNANCY

Examples:

RhD alloimmunisation

anti-K

anti-c

other clinically significant antibodies

If fetus unaffected:

Term birth / normal mode.

If severe fetal anaemia:

fetal therapy may be required

delivery based on fetal condition and gestation

CS only if obstetrically indicated.

SECTION 23

23. FETAL CONGENITAL ANOMALIES

The anomaly itself does not automatically mean CS.

Fetal anomaly General principle
Neural tube defect Usually vaginal unless obstetric indication
Down syndrome Vaginal possible
Cardiac anomaly Vaginal often appropriate
Lethal anomaly Vaginal birth generally preferred

Sacrococcygeal teratoma Specialist MDT plan
Severe skeletal dysplasia Specialist fetal medicine plan
SECTION 24

24. POLYHYDRAMNIOS

Polyhydramnios alone:

No automatic CS.

Important risks

malpresentation

cord prolapse

uterine atony

PPH

Delivery timing depends on cause/severity and fetal/maternal complications.

SECTION 25

25. OLIGOHYDRAMNIOS

Depends on cause and fetal surveillance.

Situation Management
Isolated mild oligohydramnios Increased surveillance
Term oligohydramnios Consider delivery
Oligohydramnios + FGR Earlier delivery
Abnormal CTG Immediate delivery
Severe placental insufficiency Earlier delivery

Mode depends on fetal condition, presentation and CTG.

SECTION 26

26. REDUCED FETAL MOVEMENTS

Reduced fetal movements alone does not specify a CS.

If term and concerning assessment:

Delivery may be indicated.

Mode:

vaginal if safe

CS if fetal compromise/obstetric indication.

SECTION 27

27. IUFD / STILLBIRTH

Unless maternal circumstances demand otherwise:

Preferred mode:

Vaginal birth.

CS is generally avoided solely for fetal indication because the fetus has died.

Exceptions may include:

maternal haemorrhage

uterine rupture

placenta praevia with major bleeding

other major maternal indication.

SECTION 28

28. HIV

Modern antiretroviral therapy has dramatically changed delivery planning.

Well-controlled/low viral load

Vaginal birth generally appropriate.

High/unknown viral load

Mode may be planned according to viral load and treatment history; planned CS may reduce transmission in selected circumstances.

HIV alone does not automatically mean CS.

SECTION 29

29. HEPATITIS B

Hepatitis B alone is NOT an indication for CS.

Vaginal birth usually.

Neonatal immunoprophylaxis is crucial.

SECTION 30

30. HEPATITIS C

Vaginal birth usually.

CS is not routinely performed solely to prevent HCV transmission.

SECTION 31

31. GENITAL HERPES

Active genital lesions/prodrome at labour

CS recommended to reduce neonatal HSV transmission.

No active lesions

Vaginal birth usually appropriate.

SECTION 32

32. GROUP B STREPTOCOCCUS

GBS colonisation is NOT an indication for CS.

Vaginal birth appropriate.

Intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis according to indication.

SECTION 33

33. CHORIOAMNIONITIS

Do not perform CS simply because chorioamnionitis is present.

Principle:

Antibiotics + expedite birth.

If cephalic and vaginal delivery feasible:

Vaginal birth.

CS only for standard obstetric indications.

SECTION 34

34. MATERNAL SEPSIS

Maternal sepsis:

resuscitate

antibiotics

source control

assess fetal/maternal status

Sepsis alone is not automatically a CS indication.

SECTION 35

35. UTERINE RUPTURE

Suspected uterine rupture during VBAC:

Immediate emergency laparotomy + CS if fetus is undelivered.

This is one of the clearest emergency CS indications.

SECTION 36

36. CORD PROLAPSE

Situation Mode
Live fetus + cord prolapse + vaginal birth not imminent Emergency CS
Fully dilated + birth imminent Operative vaginal birth may be appropriate
IUFD Vaginal birth generally
SECTION 37

37. SHOULDER DYSTOCIA

Shoulder dystocia is an intrapartum emergency, not a condition for planned CS after it has occurred.

If it occurs:

➔ HELPERR → manoeuvres → deliver baby.

After a previous shoulder dystocia, future mode is individualised based on:

estimated fetal weight

diabetes

previous neonatal injury

severity of previous dystocia

maternal preference.

SECTION 38

38. MACROSOMIA

Macrosomia alone does not automatically require CS.

Particularly with diabetes:

Discuss:

expectant management

induction

vaginal birth

elective CS

according to estimated fetal weight and individual risk. NICE specifically recommends counselling rather than automatically selecting CS for diabetic pregnancies with suspected macrosomia.

SECTION 40

⭐ THE EXAM RULE FOR MODE OF DELIVERY

A very useful MRCOG framework is:

CS because the route is obstructed

➔ → placenta praevia, vasa praevia, persistent transverse lie.

CS because the fetus/mother cannot safely tolerate labour

➔ → severe fetal compromise, some severe FGR, uterine rupture, certain severe maternal conditions.

Vaginal birth preferred despite pathology

➔ → abruption with IUFD, chorioamnionitis, maternal sepsis, GBS, HIV with appropriate viral suppression, hepatitis B/C, ICP, obesity, anaemia.

Timing is pathology-specific

➔ → diabetes, pre-eclampsia, twins, ICP, FGR, placenta praevia/PAS/vasa praevia.

Gestational Timing Matrix

Core associations, key diagnostic parameters, and rapid exam differentiation:

📌 1. NORMAL / LOW-RISK PREGNANCY
  • ● Uncomplicated singleton, cephalic: Spontaneous labour at term
  • ● Elective caesarean without another indication: ≥39+0 weeks
  • ● Previous uncomplicated 1 LSCS – planned VBAC: Await spontaneous labour
  • ● Previous CS – ERCS: ≥39+0 weeks
  • ● Previous vaginal birth: Usually await spontaneous labour
  • ● Post-term pregnancy: IOL generally offered by 41+0 weeks
📌 2. HYPERTENSIVE DISORDERS
  • ● Severity: Timing
  • ● BP <160/110, no complications: Do not routinely plan birth <37 wk
  • ● ≥37 wk: Individualised with senior obstetrician
  • ● Severe hypertension ≥160/110 or maternal/fetal deterioration: Any gestation if indicated
  • ● Pre-eclampsia <34 wk, stable: Expectant management if safe
  • ● 34–36+6 wk, stable: Individualised
📌 3. DIABETES IN PREGNANCY
  • ● Situation: Timing
  • ● Uncomplicated T1DM/T2DM: 37+0–38+6 wk
  • ● Maternal/fetal complications: Before 37 wk may be required
  • ● Suspected macrosomia: Individualised
  • ● GDM: Timing
  • ● Diet-controlled, uncomplicated: Await labour, but birth by 40+6
📌 4. FETAL GROWTH RESTRICTION / SGA
  • ● Fetal condition: Timing
  • ● SGA but normal surveillance: Usually 39 wk–40 wk latest
  • ● FGR with abnormal surveillance: Earlier
  • ● Abnormal umbilical artery Doppler: Earlier delivery
  • ● Absent/reversed end-diastolic flow: Preterm planned birth often required
  • ● Severe fetal compromise: Immediate delivery
📌 5. PLACENTAL CONDITIONS
  • ● Placental location: Mode
  • ● Placenta ≥20 mm from internal os: Vaginal birth may be possible
  • ● Placenta <20 mm from internal os at 36 wk: CS safest
  • ● Major placenta praevia: CS
  • ● Recurrent/heavy APH: Earlier delivery
  • ● Maternal/fetal instability: Emergency
📌 6. PLACENTA ACCRETA SPECTRUM
  • ● Suspected PAS: 35–37 wk, individualised
  • ● Placenta accreta/increta/percreta with previous CS + praevia: Usually planned preterm CS
  • ● Major haemorrhage/instability: Immediate
📌 7. VASA PRAEVIA
  • ● Diagnosed antenatally, stable: ~34–36 wk
  • ● Labour/ROM with vasa praevia: Immediate
  • ● Fetal compromise after vessel rupture: Immediate
📌 8. PLACENTAL ABRUPTION
  • ● Situation: Timing
  • ● Stable mother + live fetus + labour progressing: Immediate/appropriate
  • ● Stable mother + live fetus + vaginal delivery not imminent: Urgent
  • ● Maternal instability/major haemorrhage: Immediate
  • ● IUFD: Birth required, but vaginal birth preferred unless maternal indication for CS
  • ● Severe DIC: Correct coagulopathy while expediting birth
📌 10. BREECH PRESENTATION
  • ● Situation: Mode
  • ● Breech → successful ECV: Vaginal birth
  • ● Persistent breech, suitable for vaginal breech birth: Planned vaginal breech possible
  • ● Persistent breech, unsuitable for vaginal breech birth: Planned CS
  • ● Footling breech: CS generally
  • ● Hyperextended fetal neck: CS generally
📌 11. TRANSVERSE / OBLIQUE LIE
  • ● Situation: Management
  • ● Before term: Observe
  • ● Persistent transverse lie near term: ECV may be considered
  • ● Persistent transverse lie at labour: CS
  • ● Transverse lie + ROM: Emergency due to cord prolapse risk → usually CS
