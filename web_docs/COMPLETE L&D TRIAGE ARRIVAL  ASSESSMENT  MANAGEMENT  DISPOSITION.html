SECTION 1

1. PATIENT ARRIVAL

Step 1 — Reception

Immediately establish:

Name/identity

Age

Gestational age

Gravida/para

Reason for attendance

Whether she is booked at the hospital

Any obvious emergency

Receptionist must immediately identify obvious danger signs

Do not leave a visibly unstable patient waiting.

Examples:

Heavy vaginal bleeding

Convulsion

Collapse/unconsciousness

Severe respiratory distress

Severe abdominal pain

Cord prolapse

Imminent delivery

Severe hypertension symptoms

Major trauma

Severe maternal deterioration

➡️ Call triage/labour ward team immediately.

SECTION 2

2. INITIAL TRIAGE — TARGET WITHIN 15 MINUTES

RCOG recommends that women attending maternity triage receive an initial midwife assessment within 15 minutes and then be prioritised using symptom-specific algorithms.

The initial triage is not the complete medical assessment.

Its purpose is:

Identify who cannot safely wait.

SECTION 6

6. ASSIGN TRIAGE CATEGORY

🔴 LEVEL 1 — RED / IMMEDIATE

Principle

The patient cannot safely wait in triage.

Move directly to Labour Ward/Labour Room Operating Room/resuscitation area according to the emergency.

Senior obstetric review immediately.

RCOG similarly recommends that women classified as the highest urgency be transferred to the labour ward for assessment and that urgent cases receive senior obstetric review.

RED CONDITIONS

1

Imminent delivery

Examples:

Crowning

Fully dilated with pushing

Head on perineum

Delivery occurring

Severe urge to push with advanced labour

Immediate actions

Labour ward

Prepare delivery equipment

Senior obstetrician

Midwife

Neonatal team if indicated

IV access

Maternal observations

Fetal assessment

Prepare for neonatal resuscitation

SECTION 9

4. PATHOLOGICAL CTG

If CTG shows severe fetal compromise:

➡️ Do not wait for routine triage assessment.

Immediate steps

Call senior obstetrician

Review CTG

Identify reversible causes

Maternal observations

Left lateral positioning where appropriate

Stop oxytocin if running

Treat hypotension

Assess contractions

Consider expedited birth

SECTION 10

5. CORD PROLAPSE

Immediate actions

Call emergency team

Relieve cord compression

Knee-chest/left lateral with elevation of presenting part

Avoid unnecessary handling of cord

Prepare immediate birth

Usually emergency CS unless vaginal birth is immediately achievable

SECTION 11

6. MATERNAL COLLAPSE

Potential causes include:

Haemorrhage

PE

Eclampsia

Sepsis

Arrhythmia

Hypoglycaemia

Anaesthetic complication

Amniotic fluid embolism

Cardiac disease

Management follows maternal resuscitation principles with simultaneous fetal considerations. RCOG emphasises that outcome depends on prompt and effective resuscitation.

SECTION 12

7. SEVERE RESPIRATORY DISTRESS

Examples:

Cannot speak full sentences

Severe hypoxaemia

Severe asthma

Suspected PE

Pulmonary oedema

Pneumonia/sepsis

➡️ Immediate ABC assessment and senior/anaesthetic/medical involvement.

🔶 LEVEL 2 — AMBER / EMERGENT

Principle

Potentially serious condition requiring assessment within 15 minutes.

Move to triage assessment area, but do not allow prolonged waiting.

Reassess approximately every 15 minutes or sooner if deteriorating.

<37 weeks

Diagnosis

Usually:

Sterile speculum examination

Look for:

RCOG recommends history followed by sterile speculum examination; if fluid is not seen, biochemical testing such as IGFBP-1/PAMG-1 may assist diagnosis.

Investigations

Fetal assessment

Treatment

Depending on gestation and clinical condition:

Antibiotics

Corticosteroids

Expectant management where appropriate

Delivery if infection, fetal compromise or another indication

RCOG notes that PPROM increases risks including infection, cord prolapse and placental abruption and recommends antibiotic therapy following diagnosis.

SECTION 30

12. REASSESSMENT

This is a crucial part of triage.

RED

Continuous/high-frequency observation.

Your local protocol specifies approximately:

5-minute observations

for severe emergencies.

AMBER

Approximately:

15-minute reassessment

or sooner if clinically indicated.

GREEN

Approximately:

30–60 minutes

depending on symptoms.

WHITE

No routine continuous reassessment if completely stable, but reassess immediately if:

Pain increases

Bleeding occurs

Liquor changes

Fetal movements reduce

CTG changes

Vitals deteriorate

New symptoms appear

SECTION 32

14. FINAL DISPOSITION

Every triage patient should finish with a clear documented disposition.

There are essentially four possibilities:

A

ADMIT LABOUR WARD

Examples:

Active labour

Preterm labour

Significant PPROM

Chorioamnionitis

Abnormal CTG

Significant APH

Severe hypertension

High-risk labour

Previous uterine scar with labour

Need for induction/augmentation

B

ADMIT ANTENATAL/WARD

Examples:

Stable PPROM

Pre-eclampsia requiring monitoring

Hyperemesis/dehydration

Pyelonephritis

DVT requiring treatment

Reduced fetal movements requiring further surveillance

C

OBSERVATION → REASSESS

Early labour

Borderline BP

Uncertain membrane rupture

Mild symptoms

Awaiting CTG

Awaiting blood tests

Awaiting ultrasound

Awaiting senior review

SECTION 35

16. SAFETY-NETTING — ESSENTIAL

Every discharged woman should know:

Return immediately for:

🔴 Vaginal bleeding

🔴 Reduced/absent fetal movements

🔴 Waters breaking

🔴 Green/foul-smelling liquor

🔴 Regular painful contractions

🔴 Severe abdominal pain

🔴 Severe headache

🔴 Visual disturbance

🔴 RUQ/epigastric pain

🔴 Chest pain

🔴 Severe breathlessness

🔴 Fever/rigors

🔴 Collapse

🔴 Seizure

🔴 Any significant deterioration or feeling seriously unwell

For PPROM specifically, NHS advice includes urgent return for fever, offensive/green fluid, bleeding, abdominal/back pain, contractions or reduced fetal movements.
