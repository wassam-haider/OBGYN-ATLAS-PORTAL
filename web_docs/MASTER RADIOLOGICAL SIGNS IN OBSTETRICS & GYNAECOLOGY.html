PART A — OBSTETRIC RADIOLOGY

I

EARLY PREGNANCY & FIRST-TRIMESTER ULTRASOUND

A

Signs indicating a true intrauterine pregnancy

1

Intradecidual sign

Modality
TVUS
Appearance

Small eccentric gestational sac

Located within thickened decidua

Sac is not centrally located within the endometrial cavity.

Represents

Early implantation into decidualized endometrium.

Diagnostic significance

Supports an early IUP before a yolk sac/embryo is visible.

Exam pearl

Not equivalent to definitive visualization of a yolk sac or embryo.

2

Double decidual sac sign

Appearance
Two concentric echogenic rings surrounding the gestational sac.

Anatomy

Decidua parietalis

Decidua capsularis

Suggests

True gestational sac.

Differential

Pseudogestational sac

The DDSS is supportive rather than an absolute guarantee of IUP.

3

Yolk-sac sign

Appearance
Round anechoic centre

Echogenic wall

Located within gestational sac.

Significance

Visualization of a yolk sac within an intrauterine gestational sac is strong evidence of an IUP.

4

Double-bleb sign

Appearance

Two adjacent structures:

Yolk sac

Amnion/embryonic structure

Significance

Very early pregnancy development.

SECTION 2

II. PSEUDOGESTATIONAL SAC

Appearance
Central fluid collection

Irregular/elongated

Within endometrial cavity

No yolk sac

No embryo

Usually no characteristic eccentric implantation appearance.

Associated condition

Ectopic pregnancy

Exam trap

A pseudosac does not prove ectopic pregnancy.

It means:

➔ Pregnancy of unknown location → evaluate serial β-hCG + repeat TVUS + clinical status.
SECTION 3

III. EARLY PREGNANCY FAILURE

1

Empty sac sign

Appearance

Gestational sac without embryo.

Important criterion

An MSD ≥25 mm without an embryo on appropriate TVUS is diagnostic of pregnancy failure under commonly used consensus criteria.

2

Expanded amnion sign

Appearance

Amnion visualized but embryo not visualized.

Significance

Strongly associated with early pregnancy failure.

3

Abnormal yolk sac

Suspicious features include:

Enlarged yolk sac

Irregular yolk sac

Abnormal morphology

Calcification.

These are suspicious, not automatically diagnostic of pregnancy failure.

SECTION 4

IV. ECTOPIC PREGNANCY

A

Tubal ectopic

1

Blob sign

Appearance

Non-cystic/heterogeneous adnexal mass

Separate from ovary

Often surrounded by blood.

Represents

Tubal ectopic pregnancy.

Exam pearl

A tubal mass separate from the ovary in a woman with a positive pregnancy test is highly suspicious.

2

Tubal ring / bagel sign

Appearance

Echogenic ring surrounding an extrauterine gestational sac.

Represents

Trophoblastic tissue surrounding gestational sac.

Diagnostic value

Highly suspicious for tubal ectopic.

3

Ring-of-fire sign

Doppler appearance

Circumferential peripheral vascularity.

Mechanism

Low-resistance trophoblastic flow.

Critical exam trap

It is NOT specific for ectopic pregnancy.

It can also occur in:

Corpus luteum

Therefore:

Ring of fire ≠ automatic ectopic pregnancy.

4

Extrauterine yolk sac

Diagnostic significance

Strong/direct evidence of ectopic pregnancy.

5

Extrauterine embryo

Diagnostic significance

Diagnostic of ectopic pregnancy.

6

Extrauterine fetal cardiac activity

Diagnostic significance

Definitive ectopic pregnancy.

VIII. TWIN COMPLICATIONS

Twin-to-twin transfusion syndrome

Donor twin

Oligohydramnios

Small/absent bladder

"Stuck twin"

Recipient twin

Polyhydramnios

Enlarged bladder

Cardiomegaly

Hydrops in severe disease.

TAPS

Donor

Increased MCA-PSV

➔ → Anaemia.

Recipient

Reduced MCA-PSV

➔ → Polycythaemia.

TRAP sequence

Acardiac twin

Absent functional heart

Severe structural abnormalities.

Pump twin

Normal heart

Provides circulation to acardiac twin.

Key mechanism

Reversed arterial perfusion.

SECTION 9

IX. PLACENTA PREVIA & VASA PREVIA

Placenta previa

Ultrasound

Placental tissue reaches or covers internal cervical os.

Low-lying placenta

Placental edge is close to internal os but does not cover it.

Vasa previa

Color Doppler

Fetal vessels traverse the membranes over/near the internal os.

Critical distinction

➔ Placenta previa → placental tissue
➔ Vasa previa → fetal vessels
