SECTION 1

1. 🧪 IMPORTANT BIOCHEMISTRY IN OBSTETRICS

A

β-hCG — the key pregnancy hormone

Feature Important point
Source Syncytiotrophoblast
Detectable ~8–10 days after conception
Peaks ~8–10 weeks
Then Falls and plateaus
Structure α + β subunits
Main role Maintains corpus luteum → progesterone

hCG patterns

Normal early pregnancy

Rapidly rises.

Approximate doubling every 48–72 h in early pregnancy, although the rate becomes slower as the level rises.

A single hCG value cannot reliably diagnose viability.

Abnormal patterns

➔ Slow rise → ectopic pregnancy, failing pregnancy or pregnancy of uncertain viability.
➔ Falling → miscarriage is likely, but interpret with clinical/US findings.

Very high:

Multiple pregnancy

Molar pregnancy

Occasionally chromosomal abnormalities

OSCE point

Never say “hCG must double every 48 hours”. Say:

“I would interpret serial quantitative β-hCG according to the starting concentration, the interval between measurements and ultrasound findings.”

2

Progesterone

Source

Corpus luteum initially.

Placenta becomes major source later.

Functions

Maintains endometrium.

Reduces uterine contractility.

Supports pregnancy.

Promotes breast development.

Contributes to respiratory and GI smooth-muscle effects.

Low progesterone

May be associated with:

Failing pregnancy

Ectopic pregnancy

But:

Serum progesterone alone does NOT reliably distinguish ectopic from miscarriage.

3

Oestrogen

Main pregnancy oestrogen:

Estriol (E3)

Sources

Fetoplacental unit:

➔ Fetal adrenal → DHEA-S
➔ Fetal liver → metabolites
➔ Placenta → estriol

Functions

Uterine growth.

Breast development.

Increased uteroplacental blood flow.

Increases hepatic production of several proteins.

Increases clotting tendency.

Important exam concept

Estriol is a marker of fetoplacental function.

Reduced unconjugated estriol can occur in:

Trisomy 21

Trisomy 18

Fetal adrenal dysfunction

Placental dysfunction

Incorrect gestational dating

4

Human placental lactogen — hPL

Also called:

Human chorionic somatomammotropin

Source

Placenta.

Effects

Maternal insulin resistance ↑

Lipolysis ↑

Maternal glucose availability to fetus ↑

Supports lactation preparation.

Clinical significance

As pregnancy progresses:

➔ hPL ↑ → insulin resistance ↑

This contributes to:

Gestational diabetes

Increased maternal insulin requirements

Other contributors to insulin resistance

Placental growth hormone

Progesterone

Cortisol

Prolactin

TNF-α

5

Placental growth hormone

Replaces maternal pituitary GH progressively during pregnancy.

Effects

↑ insulin resistance

↑ maternal IGF-1

Promotes maternal metabolic adaptation.

6

Relaxin

Source:

Placenta

Effects:

7

Prolactin

Source

Anterior pituitary.

Pregnancy

Prolactin progressively increases.

Stimulates mammary gland development.

Why doesn't milk secretion occur fully during pregnancy?

Because high:

Progesterone

inhibit full lactogenesis despite high prolactin.

After delivery:

➔ Placenta expelled → oestrogen/progesterone fall → prolactin can stimulate milk production.

➔ ↓

➔ ↓

11. Thyroid biochemistry in pregnancy

TSH

Early pregnancy:

hCG has thyrotropic activity

Therefore:

➔ hCG ↑ → TSH ↓

particularly during the first trimester.

Later:

TSH generally rises toward the non-pregnant range.

Important

Do not use non-pregnant reference ranges blindly.

Use:

Laboratory pregnancy-specific ranges, preferably trimester-specific, or

Accepted pregnancy-specific guidance when laboratory ranges are unavailable.

12

Free T4 / total T4

Pregnancy:

TBG ↑

Total T4 ↑

Total T3 ↑

Free hormone changes are smaller and assay-dependent.

Mechanism

Oestrogen ↑

➔ ↓

TBG ↑

➔ ↓

Total thyroid hormones ↑

13

Renal biochemical changes in pregnancy

GFR

↑ approximately 40–50%

Therefore:

Creatinine

Normally:

➔ ↓

A creatinine that looks “normal” by non-pregnant standards may actually be concerning in pregnancy.

Urea

Usually:

➔ ↓

Uric acid

Falls early.

Then rises later in pregnancy.

Clinical importance

Elevated uric acid may accompany:

Preeclampsia

Placental dysfunction

But:

Uric acid is not diagnostic of preeclampsia.

14

Sodium and osmolality

Pregnancy causes:

Plasma volume ↑

Total body water ↑

➔ Plasma osmolality ↓ slightly
➔ Serum sodium ↓ slightly

This is due to resetting of osmoregulation.

15

Haematological biochemistry

Plasma volume

↑ approximately 40–50%

Red-cell mass

↑ approximately 20–30% without adequate iron supplementation.

Therefore:

Physiological anaemia

Plasma volume expansion

RBC mass expansion

➔ → haemodilution
➔ → Hb concentration falls.
Important distinction

Physiological haemodilution ≠ iron-deficiency anaemia.

16

Iron metabolism

Pregnancy increases:

Iron requirement

Maternal erythropoiesis

Fetal/placental iron transfer.

Ferritin

Low ferritin:

➔ → iron deficiency.

Ferritin is also an acute-phase reactant, therefore:

Inflammation can make ferritin falsely reassuring.

17

Calcium

Total calcium may fall because:

➔ Albumin ↓ → protein-bound calcium ↓

Ionised calcium generally remains much more stable.

Exam trap

Low total calcium does not automatically mean true hypocalcaemia.

18

Albumin

Pregnancy:

➔ Albumin ↓

because of haemodilution.

Consequences:

➔ Total calcium ↓

Total drug concentrations may change

Reduced oncotic pressure contributes to oedema.

Normal or slightly lower.

Bile acids

24

Glucose metabolism

Pregnancy is a state of:

Early pregnancy

Relative insulin sensitivity.

Later pregnancy

Progressive insulin resistance

Major drivers:

hPL

Placental GH

Cortisol

Progesterone

Oestrogen

Pro-inflammatory cytokines

Result

Maternal glucose availability increases.

If pancreatic β-cells cannot compensate:

➔ → gestational diabetes.
25

Lipid metabolism

Pregnancy:

Triglycerides ↑

Total cholesterol ↑

LDL ↑

HDL usually ↑ modestly, then may decline late.

Purpose:

Increased energy availability.

Fetal development.

Severe hypertriglyceridaemia

Can increase risk of:

Acute pancreatitis.

26

Fibrinogen

Pregnancy:

Fibrinogen increases

Typical non-pregnant:

~2–4 g/L

Late pregnancy:

often approximately 4–6 g/L

Major obstetric point

A fibrinogen of 2 g/L may appear “normal” outside pregnancy but is concerning in major obstetric haemorrhage.

Fibrinogen can fall rapidly in:

Massive PPH

Placental abruption

DIC

27

Coagulation

Pregnancy = physiological hypercoagulable state

Changes:

Fibrinogen ↑

Factors VII, VIII, IX, X, XII ↑

vWF ↑

➔ Protein S ↓

Antithrombin relatively preserved

Fibrinolysis suppressed

Purpose:

➔ → protect against haemorrhage at delivery.

Downside:

➔ → VTE risk increases.
28

ABG in pregnancy

Important OSCE value.

Normal pregnancy

Progesterone stimulates ventilation.

Therefore:

➔ PaCO₂ ↓

Typical:

PaCO₂ ~28–32 mmHg

PaO₂ usually >100 mmHg

HCO₃⁻ ~18–22 mmol/L

Mild respiratory alkalosis

Renal bicarbonate compensation.

Key exam point

A PaCO₂ of 40 mmHg may be abnormal in pregnancy.

29

Lactate

Normal pregnancy:

Lactate generally remains normal.

Raised lactate may indicate:

Tissue hypoperfusion

Sepsis

Severe hypoxia

Shock

Seizures

Severe metabolic stress

30. IMMUNOPATHOLOGY OF PREGNANCY

The classic statement:

Pregnancy is not simply an immunosuppressed state; it involves complex immune modulation.

31

Maternal–fetal immune tolerance

The fetus expresses paternal antigens.

Yet maternal immune system normally tolerates pregnancy.

Major mechanisms:

1

T-regulatory cells ↑

T-reg cells promote tolerance.

2

Decidual immune environment

Contains:

Decidual NK cells

Macrophages

T cells

Dendritic cells

3

Trophoblast immune modulation

Trophoblast expresses unusual HLA patterns.

Important

HLA-G

HLA-E

HLA-C

32

Decidual NK cells

Very important.

They are abundant in early pregnancy.

Functions:

Spiral artery remodelling.

Placental development.

Trophoblast interaction.

Important trap

They are not primarily cytotoxic killer cells in normal pregnancy.

33

Spiral artery remodelling

Normal:

Extravillous trophoblast

➔ ↓

invades decidua/myometrium

➔ ↓

spiral arteries remodel

➔ ↓

low-resistance, high-capacitance circulation

➔ ↓

adequate placental perfusion.

34

Preeclampsia immunopathology

Abnormal:

Poor trophoblast invasion

➔ ↓

inadequate spiral artery remodelling

➔ ↓

high-resistance placental circulation

➔ ↓

placental hypoxia/ischemia and oxidative stress

➔ ↓

antiangiogenic factors released

➔ ↓

maternal endothelial dysfunction

➔ ↓

hypertension + proteinuria/systemic organ dysfunction.

35

Important angiogenic factors

VEGF

Vascular endothelial growth factor.

Promotes:

Angiogenesis

Endothelial function.

PlGF

Placental growth factor.

Promotes placental angiogenesis.

sFlt-1

Soluble fms-like tyrosine kinase-1.

Acts as an antiangiogenic factor by binding:

VEGF

PlGF

➔ → reduces their bioavailability.
36

sFlt-1 : PlGF ratio

Used in some settings to help assess risk of preterm pre-eclampsia, depending on the assay and local protocol.

Important OSCE point

Do not quote one universal cut-off without specifying the assay/test pathway.

37

38

RhD alloimmunisation

Classic immunopathology.

RhD-negative mother

+

➔ ↓

➔ ↓

➔ ↓

Potential sensitising events

Miscarriage

Ectopic pregnancy

Abdominal trauma

Antepartum haemorrhage

Invasive procedures

External cephalic version
