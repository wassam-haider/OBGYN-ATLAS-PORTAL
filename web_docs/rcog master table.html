SECTION 1

Overview & Core Guidance

Key
Expectant = continue pregnancy with surveillance

Planned birth = induction or planned CS depending on indication

Urgent birth = do not wait for the target gestation if maternal/fetal compromise occurs

SECTION 2

1. NORMAL / GENERAL PREGNANCY

Condition Ideal timing of birth
Uncomplicated singleton pregnancy 40+0 weeks; spontaneous labour acceptable
Uncomplicated pregnancy approaching 41 weeks Offer induction by 41+0 weeks under NICE
Previous uncomplicated LSCS — planned ERCS ≥39+0 weeks
Previous CS + planned VBAC Await spontaneous labour unless another indication
Maternal request for elective CS without medical indication ≥39+0 weeks
Obesity alone No routine early delivery solely for obesity
Well-controlled hypothyroidism No alteration in timing
Well-controlled hyperthyroidism No routine early birth solely for thyroid disease
Well-controlled epilepsy No routine early birth solely because of epilepsy

RCOG specifically states that well-controlled thyroid disease should not alter when/how birth occurs, and epilepsy alone is not an indication for early birth or planned CS.

SECTION 3

2. HYPERTENSIVE DISORDERS

Chronic hypertension

Situation Timing
Chronic HTN, BP <160/110, no complications Do not routinely deliver <37 weeks
≥37 weeks Timing individualized with senior obstetrician
BP ≥160/110 despite treatment / complications Earlier birth may be required
Superimposed pre-eclampsia Follow pre-eclampsia timing

NICE states that planned early birth should not routinely be offered before 37 weeks when BP is below 160/110 mmHg.

Gestational hypertension

Situation Timing
BP <160/110 and uncomplicated No planned birth <37 weeks
≥37 weeks Plan birth according to maternal/fetal circumstances
Severe hypertension / maternal or fetal compromise Earlier delivery
SECTION 4

3. PRE-ECLAMPSIA

Pre-eclampsia Timing
<34 weeks, stable mother + fetus Expectant management if safe
34–36+6 weeks Individualized; consider planned birth

Gestational Timing Matrix

Core associations, key diagnostic parameters, and rapid exam differentiation:

📌 1. NORMAL / GENERAL PREGNANCY
  • ● Uncomplicated singleton pregnancy: 40+0 weeks; spontaneous labour acceptable
  • ● Uncomplicated pregnancy approaching 41 weeks: Offer induction by 41+0 weeks under NICE
  • ● Previous uncomplicated LSCS — planned ERCS: ≥39+0 weeks
  • ● Previous CS + planned VBAC: Await spontaneous labour unless another indication
  • ● Maternal request for elective CS without medical indication: ≥39+0 weeks
  • ● Obesity alone: No routine early delivery solely for obesity
📌 2. HYPERTENSIVE DISORDERS
  • ● Situation: Timing
  • ● Chronic HTN, BP <160/110, no complications: Do not routinely deliver <37 weeks
  • ● ≥37 weeks: Timing individualized with senior obstetrician
  • ● BP ≥160/110 despite treatment / complications: Earlier birth may be required
  • ● Superimposed pre-eclampsia: Follow pre-eclampsia timing
  • ● Situation: Timing
📌 3. PRE-ECLAMPSIA
  • ● Pre-eclampsia: Timing
  • ● <34 weeks, stable mother + fetus: Expectant management if safe
  • ● 34–36+6 weeks: Individualized; consider planned birth
  • ● ≥37+0 weeks: Initiate birth within 24–48 h
  • ● Severe pre-eclampsia + maternal/fetal deterioration: Deliver irrespective of gestation
📌 4. DIABETES
  • ● Uncomplicated type 1/type 2 diabetes: 37+0–38+6 weeks
  • ● Poor glycaemic control: Consider earlier
  • ● Maternal vascular disease: Earlier according to severity
  • ● Fetal compromise/FGR: Earlier
  • ● Severe complications: Individualized urgent delivery
  • ● GDM: Timing
📌 5. INTRAHEPATIC CHOLESTASIS OF PREGNANCY
  • ● Bile acids: Severity
  • ● 19–39 µmol/L: Mild
  • ● 40–99 µmol/L: Moderate
  • ● ≥100 µmol/L: Severe
📌 6. FETAL GROWTH RESTRICTION / SGA
  • ● Fetal condition: Typical timing
  • ● SGA, normal UA Doppler, no other concern: Around 39 weeks / by 40 weeks
  • ● FGR with abnormal UA Doppler: Earlier, commonly 37 weeks range depending on severity
  • ● Absent/reversed end-diastolic flow: Much earlier delivery according to Doppler/CTG/DV and gestation
  • ● Abnormal DV / severe fetal compromise: Urgent delivery depending on gestation
  • ● Non-reassuring CTG: Immediate delivery
📌 7. PRETERM PRELABOUR RUPTURE OF MEMBRANE
  • ● PPROM situation: Timing
  • ● PPROM + stable mother/fetus + no infection: Expectant management to 37 weeks
  • ● PPROM + chorioamnionitis: Deliver immediately after maternal stabilization
  • ● PPROM + fetal compromise: Deliver
  • ● PPROM + significant abruption: Deliver
  • ● PPROM + established labour: Manage labour
📌 8. PLACENTA PRAEVIA
  • ● Situation: Timing
  • ● Uncomplicated placenta praevia: Usually 36–37 weeks
  • ● Heavy/recurrent bleeding: Earlier
  • ● Active major APH / maternal instability: Immediate delivery
  • ● Labour with placenta praevia: Usually CS
📌 9. PLACENTA ACCRETA SPECTRUM — PAS
  • ● PAS: Timing
  • ● Suspected placenta accreta: Planned birth around 35–37 weeks, individualized
  • ● Significant bleeding / instability: Earlier
  • ● Uncontrolled haemorrhage: Emergency delivery
📌 10. VASA PRAEVIA
  • ● Antenatally diagnosed vasa praevia: Planned CS around 34–36 weeks
  • ● Labour / ROM with vasa praevia: Emergency CS
  • ● Fetal vessel rupture: Immediate emergency CS
📌 11. MULTIPLE PREGNANCY
  • ● Pregnancy: Planned birth
  • ● DCDA twins: 37 weeks
  • ● MCDA twins: 36 weeks
  • ● MCMA twins: 32–34 weeks
  • ● Triplets: 35 weeks
