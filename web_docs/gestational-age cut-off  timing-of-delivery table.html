1. First memorize the universal gestational-age definitions

SECTION 1

Overview & Core Guidance

<28+0 Extremely preterm
28+0–31+6 Very preterm
32+0–33+6 Moderate preterm
34+0–36+6 Late preterm
37+0–38+6 Early term
39+0–40+6 Full term
41+0–41+6 Late term
≥42+0 Post-term

ACOG specifically defines late-preterm as 34+0–36+6, early-term as 37+0–38+6, full-term as 39+0–40+6, late-term as 41+0–41+6, and post-term as ≥42+0.

The single most important rule

No elective/non-medically indicated delivery <39+0 weeks.

This includes elective induction, elective CS and cervical ripening. A medical indication overrides this rule.

SECTION 2

2. NORMAL PREGNANCY

Uncomplicated singleton

ACOG

Elective delivery: ≥39+0

Expectant management acceptable through 40 weeks

At 41+0, surveillance is generally started

Delivery should occur by 42+0

RCOG/NICE approach

Induction generally offered at 41+0 weeks.

Exam memory

39 = elective delivery

41 = induction/surveillance

42 = post-term — don't continue routinely

ACOG notes that for uncomplicated pregnancies, surveillance is recommended from 41+0 and delivery is indicated by 42+0.

SECTION 3

3. PRETERM LABOUR

Situation Important cut-off
Preterm birth <37+0
Late preterm 34+0–36+6
Term ≥37+0
Antenatal corticosteroid major window before 34–35 weeks, depending on guideline/clinical scenario
Magnesium sulfate for fetal neuroprotection Usually <32 weeks; local guideline may extend to <33–34

RCOG's current corticosteroid guideline covers women at risk of preterm birth and has updated evidence for steroid use; the exact threshold depends on gestational age and circumstances.

SECTION 4

4. PPROM

RCOG

PPROM = rupture before 37+0 weeks.

For uncomplicated PPROM:

Expectant management until 37+0 weeks

provided:

no chorioamnionitis

no fetal compromise

no significant abruption

no other indication for delivery.

RCOG specifically recommends expectant management until 37+0 when mother and fetus remain well.

Deliver earlier if:

chorioamnionitis

fetal compromise

significant bleeding/abruption

cord prolapse

established labour

other maternal/fetal indication.

ACOG

Timing is individualized according to:

gestational age

infection

fetal status

GBS

neonatal considerations.

SECTION 5

5. PRE-ECLAMPSIA / HYPERTENSION

Gestational hypertension

No severe features

Delivery: 37+0 weeks

Pre-eclampsia without severe features

Delivery: 37+0 weeks

Severe pre-eclampsia

➔ ≥34+0 weeks → delivery after maternal stabilization
➔ <34 weeks → expectant management may be considered in a specialist centre if:

mother stable

fetus stable

no contraindication to expectant management.

Deliver immediately regardless of gestation for:

uncontrolled severe hypertension

eclampsia

pulmonary oedema

HELLP/severe deterioration

significant abruption

persistent fetal compromise.

ACOG states that pre-eclampsia without severe features is generally delivered at 37 weeks, while severe features at ≥34 weeks generally warrant delivery once stable; earlier delivery is required if maternal/fetal status deteriorates.

RCOG patient guidance similarly states that women with pre-eclampsia should generally be offered birth at 37 weeks, or earlier if mother/baby becomes unwell.

Memorize

PET = 37

Severe PET = 34

Unstable = NOW

6. CHRONIC HYPERTENSION

Timing depends on:

controlled vs poorly controlled

medication requirement

superimposed PET

FGR

maternal complications.

ACOG high-yield

Generally:

➔ Well controlled chronic HTN without complications → 37–39 weeks

Earlier if:

poor control

superimposed pre-eclampsia

FGR

abruption

maternal deterioration.

SECTION 7

7. DIABETES

Gestational diabetes — diet controlled

ACOG:

Do not routinely deliver before 39+0

➔ If well controlled and uncomplicated → 39–40+6 is typical.

GDM on medication

Generally:

39+0–39+6 if well controlled.

Poorly controlled GDM

Consider earlier delivery, commonly:

37+0–38+6

depending on:

glucose control

fetal growth

antenatal testing

complications.

Pregestational diabetes

Well controlled, no vascular disease

Usually:

39+0–39+6

Vascular disease / poor control / complications

Earlier delivery may be indicated, commonly:

36+0–39+0, individualized.

ACOG specifically lists poorly controlled gestational/pregestational diabetes and pregestational diabetes with vascular disease among conditions that can justify delivery before 39 weeks.

SECTION 8

8. FETAL GROWTH RESTRICTION — VERY IMPORTANT

This is one of the most important MRCOG questions.

Timing depends on:

EFW percentile

umbilical artery Doppler

MCA/CPR

ductus venosus

oligohydramnios

maternal disease.

Simplified ACOG/SMFM-style framework

FGR situation Typical delivery
EFW 3rd–10th percentile, normal UA Doppler 38–39 weeks
EFW <3rd percentile 37 weeks
Abnormal UA Doppler, decreased diastolic flow 37 weeks
AEDF 33–34 weeks
REDF 30–32 weeks
Severe fetal compromise Earlier / immediate
Remember

FGR + normal Doppler = 38–39

FGR <3rd centile = 37

AEDF = 33–34

REDF = 30–32

The presence of FGR plus oligohydramnios, hypertension or abnormal Dopplers can move delivery earlier. ACOG recommends individualized management and recognizes abnormal Doppler/FGR combinations as high-risk situations.

SECTION 9

9. OLIGOHYDRAMNIOS

Isolated oligohydramnios

ACOG generally considers delivery around:

36+0–37+6 weeks

or at diagnosis if diagnosed later.

Important definition

Single deepest vertical pocket <2 cm

ACOG identifies isolated oligohydramnios as an indication requiring increased surveillance and possible delivery depending on the clinical situation.

With FGR / abnormal Doppler / PET

➔ → Earlier according to the underlying condition.
SECTION 10

10. POLYHYDRAMNIOS

Mild idiopathic polyhydramnios

Usually:

Do not deliver early solely because of mild polyhydramnios.

Moderate/severe

Increased surveillance

Delivery timing individualized

Do not automatically equate polyhydramnios with a fixed early-delivery gestation.

ACOG suggests surveillance from approximately 32–34 weeks for moderate/severe polyhydramnios, but this is a surveillance threshold—not automatically a delivery threshold.

SECTION 11

11. CHOLESTASIS OF PREGNANCY

This is another very high-yield cut-off.

Timing depends heavily on bile acid concentration.

ACOG/Society-style exam framework

Bile acids Typical delivery concept
<40 μmol/L Around 39 weeks
40–99 μmol/L Around 38–39 weeks
≥100 μmol/L 36+0–36+6 weeks

Severe ICP:

➔ ≥100 μmol/L → 36+0–36+6

Do not use fetal surveillance as a guarantee against stillbirth.

SECTION 12

12. PLACENTA PREVIA

If persistent placenta previa requiring CS:

36+0–37+6 weeks

Emergency delivery earlier if:

major bleeding

maternal instability

fetal compromise

labour.

ACOG includes placenta previa and placenta accreta among conditions where medically indicated delivery may appropriately occur before 39 weeks.

SECTION 13

13. PLACENTA ACCRETA SPECTRUM

Planned delivery:

34+0–35+6 weeks

in an appropriate specialist centre if stable.

Earlier for:

bleeding

labour

ROM

maternal instability

fetal compromise.

Exam pearl

PAS = 34–35+6

SECTION 14

14. VASA PREVIA

Planned CS generally:

34+0–37+0 weeks

depending on:

bleeding

contractions

cervical length

inpatient/outpatient management

local protocol.

ACOG discusses planned caesarean before labour and describes a reasonable management window of 34+0–37+0 weeks.

Memory

Vasa previa = 34–37

SECTION 15

15. PRIOR CLASSICAL CS

Planned repeat CS:

36+0–37+0 weeks

Do NOT wait until 39 weeks because of increased uterine rupture risk.

Prior myomectomy entering uterine cavity / significant transmural surgery

Often:

37–38 weeks

depending on operative details.

ACOG specifically identifies prior classical CS and certain myomectomies as indications for medically indicated early-term delivery.

SECTION 16

16. PREVIOUS LOWER-SEGMENT CS

Elective repeat CS

≥39+0 weeks

RCOG similarly recommends ERCS from 39+0 weeks.

VBAC

Spontaneous labour preferred.

If no spontaneous labour:

41+0 weeks = senior review / reassessment

RCOG specifically discusses review at 41+0 weeks if labour has not started.

19. FETAL DEMISE OF ONE TWIN

No single gestational-age cut-off.

Management depends on:

chorionicity

gestation

maternal condition

surviving twin condition

coagulopathy

neurological injury risk.

Do not automatically deliver the surviving twin immediately.

20

PPROM + GBS

SECTION 20

21. GBS

GBS colonisation alone is NOT an indication for elective delivery.

It is an indication for:

Intrapartum antibiotic prophylaxis

unless another obstetric indication exists.

SECTION 21

22. EPILEPSY

Well controlled epilepsy alone:

No routine early delivery solely because of epilepsy.

Delivery timing depends on:

seizures

medications

fetal growth

maternal complications.

SECTION 22

23. SICKLE CELL DISEASE

No universal gestational cut-off solely for sickle-cell disease.

Earlier delivery if:

severe maternal disease

pre-eclampsia

FGR

fetal compromise

recurrent crises.

SECTION 23

24. THALASSAEMIA

No fixed gestational-age delivery solely because of thalassaemia.

Individualize according to:

cardiac disease

anaemia

transfusion dependence

fetal growth

maternal condition.

SECTION 24

25. MATERNAL CARDIAC DISEASE

No universal week.

Delivery timing is based on:

NYHA class

ventricular function

pulmonary hypertension

arrhythmia

aortic disease

maternal deterioration.

Some conditions require delivery regardless of gestation.

SECTION 25

26. MATERNAL AGE

ACOG

Age ≥40 years

Consider delivery:

39+0–39+6 weeks

because of increasing stillbirth/neonatal risk.

Age 35–39

Age alone does not mandate early delivery.

SECTION 26

27. IVF PREGNANCY

ACOG recommends considering antenatal surveillance from approximately:

36+0 weeks

for pregnancies conceived by IVF.

This is primarily a surveillance threshold, not automatically a delivery threshold.

SECTION 27

28. OBESITY

ACOG considers antenatal surveillance according to BMI:

BMI Possible surveillance
35–39.9 From ~37 weeks
≥40 From ~34 weeks

Again:

Surveillance ≠ automatic delivery.

ACOG specifically recommends individualized surveillance in obesity because stillbirth risk rises with increasing BMI and advancing gestation.

SECTION 28

29. PREVIOUS STILLBIRTH

No universal single delivery week.

Management is individualized according to:

cause of previous stillbirth

recurrence risk

maternal anxiety

fetal growth

surveillance.

Usually increased surveillance and consideration of delivery around:

39 weeks

unless a specific recurrent cause indicates earlier birth.

SECTION 29

30. ABRUPTION

Acute significant abruption

Immediate delivery if:

maternal instability

fetal compromise

major bleeding

coagulopathy.

Chronic/recurrent abruption

No universal fixed week.

ACOG recommends individualized management and increased surveillance.

SECTION 30

31. FETAL ANOMALY

There is no universal gestational cut-off.

Depends on:

lethal vs non-lethal anomaly

fetal compromise

hydrops

cardiac failure

airway obstruction

chromosomal diagnosis

parental wishes

neonatal/pediatric surgical capability.

SECTION 31

32. HYDROPS

No single gestational cut-off.

If:

fetal compromise

severe anemia

cardiac failure

infection

maternal mirror syndrome

➔ → delivery/intervention may be required regardless of gestation.
SECTION 32

33. ALLOIMMUNISATION

No single week.

Delivery depends on:

MCA-PSV

fetal anaemia

IUT history

hydrops

fetal testing.

ACOG specifically lists alloimmunization with known/suspected fetal effects among conditions that can warrant medically indicated early delivery.

SECTION 33

34. VELAMENTOUS CORD INSERTION / SINGLE UMBILICAL ARTERY

Not automatically an indication for early delivery.

ACOG suggests:

Weekly surveillance from ~36 weeks

for velamentous cord insertion or SUA in appropriate circumstances.

SECTION 34

35. LOW PAPP-A / ABNORMAL SERUM MARKERS

These generally lead to:

serial growth scans

fetal surveillance

rather than automatic early delivery.

For example, ACOG suggests surveillance from approximately 36 weeks for low PAPP-A or elevated inhibin A in specified circumstances.

SECTION 35

36. OVERT DIABETIC VASCULAR DISEASE

Pregestational diabetes with:

nephropathy

retinopathy

vascular disease

poor glycaemic control

➔ → individualized early delivery.

Do not automatically wait for 39 weeks.

SECTION 36

37. RENAL DISEASE

No single week.

Earlier delivery if:

severe hypertension

renal deterioration

pre-eclampsia

FGR

fetal compromise.

SECTION 37

38. THYROID DISEASE

Well-controlled thyroid disease:

No routine early delivery.

Poorly controlled severe disease:

➔ → individualized.
SECTION 38

39. SYSTEMIC LUPUS / AUTOIMMUNE DISEASE

No fixed week.

Deliver according to:

pre-eclampsia

FGR

placental insufficiency

maternal organ involvement

fetal compromise.

SECTION 39

40. INTRAUTERINE INFECTION / CHORIOAMNIONITIS

This is NOT a “wait until X weeks” condition.

Once clinical chorioamnionitis is diagnosed:

Start antibiotics + expedite delivery

regardless of gestational age.

SECTION 40

41. UTERINE RUPTURE

Immediate laparotomy/delivery.

No gestational-age cut-off.

SECTION 41

42. CORD PROLAPSE

Immediate delivery if fetus viable and delivery is indicated.

Usually emergency CS unless vaginal delivery is imminent.

SECTION 42

43. NON-REASSURING FETAL STATUS

No fixed gestational age.

If persistent severe fetal compromise:

Deliver regardless of gestation when fetal benefit outweighs prematurity risk.

SECTION 43

44. ECLAMPSIA

No gestational cut-off.

After maternal stabilization:

Delivery is definitive treatment.

SECTION 44

45. HELLP

No simple “37-week rule.”

If:

maternal deterioration

uncontrolled hypertension

DIC

liver complications

renal dysfunction

neurological symptoms

fetal compromise

➔ → delivery regardless of gestation after appropriate stabilization.

At viable gestations, expectant management is generally not appropriate when severe maternal disease is present.

46

PLACENTAL ABRUPTION + DIC

Immediate delivery and correction of coagulopathy.

Gestational age does not override maternal emergency.

SECTION 45

47. FETAL DEATH

Once IUFD is confirmed:

Usually no need for immediate delivery solely because of gestational age unless:

maternal sepsis

haemorrhage

abruption

DIC

severe maternal illness

other indication.

SECTION 46

48. POST-TERM

41+0

Start/offer induction depending on guideline.

42+0

Post-term.

Delivery should not routinely be delayed beyond this.

ACOG's classification defines ≥42 weeks as post-term and supports delivery by 42 weeks in otherwise uncomplicated pregnancy.
