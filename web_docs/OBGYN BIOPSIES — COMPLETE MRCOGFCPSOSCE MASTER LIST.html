SECTION 6

5. TECHNIQUE — CERVICAL PUNCH BIOPSY

Step 1 — Position

Lithotomy

Good illumination

Insert speculum gently

Step 2 — Visualisation

Identify cervix

Remove mucus

Examine transformation zone

Apply 3–5% acetic acid

Assess acetowhitening

Assess:

margins

vascular pattern

punctation

mosaic

lesion location

Step 3 — Select biopsy site

Biopsy the most abnormal area, particularly:

Dense acetowhite lesion

Coarse punctation

Coarse mosaic

Abnormal vessels

Suspicious lesion

Step 4 — Biopsy

Place biopsy forceps perpendicular to cervix

Take a representative tissue bite

Include adequate stromal tissue

Avoid excessive crushing

Take multiple biopsies when appropriate

Step 5 — Specimen

Immediately place into correctly labelled formalin container.

Step 6 — Haemostasis

Options:

Pressure

Monsel's solution

Silver nitrate

Cautery if required

SECTION 7

6. OSCE — WHAT TO SAY

“I would identify the transformation zone, apply acetic acid, assess for acetowhite changes and abnormal vascular patterns, select the most abnormal area and take a representative punch biopsy using biopsy forceps. I would place the specimen immediately in correctly labelled formalin and achieve haemostasis with pressure or haemostatic agent.”

SECTION 30

33. OVARIAN/PARATUBAL MASS BIOPSY

Important OSCE principle

Do not routinely biopsy a suspicious ovarian mass simply to establish malignancy if definitive surgical management is indicated.

Potential approaches include:

Image-guided biopsy in selected cases

Laparoscopic biopsy

Biopsy of metastatic/peritoneal lesion

Omental biopsy

Lymph-node biopsy

The safest and most diagnostically useful tissue site may be an accessible metastatic lesion rather than puncturing the ovarian mass.

37. SENTINEL LYMPH-NODE BIOPSY

Important in

Vulval cancer

Endometrial cancer

Selected breast cancer pathways

RCOG's gynaecological oncology curriculum specifically includes sentinel lymph-node dissection for vulval cancer and sentinel-node biopsy for endometrial cancer.

Principle

The sentinel node is the first draining lymph node from the primary tumour.

Technique concept

Identify primary tumour.

Inject tracer according to protocol.

Identify lymphatic drainage.

Locate sentinel node.

Remove node.

Send for histopathology/ultrastaging according to tumour protocol.
