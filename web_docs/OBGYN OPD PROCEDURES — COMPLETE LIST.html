SECTION 2

1. PAP SMEAR / CERVICAL SCREENING

Purpose

Screen for high-risk HPV and/or cervical precancer, depending on the screening programme.

In HPV-based programmes, the cervical sample is primarily tested for high-risk HPV, with cytology used for triage when HPV is detected.

Indications

Routine cervical screening according to national programme

Follow-up of previous abnormal screening where indicated

HPV-related surveillance according to protocol

Do NOT use Pap smear as a substitute for investigation of:

Symptomatic postcoital bleeding

Persistent unexplained intermenstrual bleeding

Suspicious-looking cervix

Suspected cervical cancer

Key OSCE statement

"A cervical screening test is a screening test, not a diagnostic test for symptomatic cervical cancer."

A symptomatic woman with a suspicious cervix needs appropriate gynaecological assessment/referral rather than waiting for screening cytology.

Instruments

Basic

Examination couch

Gloves

Light source

Appropriate speculum

Cusco/bivalve

Graves

Sims — generally requires assistant

Cervical sampling device:

Cervical broom/brush

Cytobrush where appropriate

Liquid-based cytology vial

Labels

Request form/electronic request

Lubricant — minimal amount, avoiding contamination of the sampling area

NHS describes cervical screening as examination with a speculum followed by cervical sampling with a soft brush.

2

Speculum insertion

Inspect vulva.

Choose appropriate speculum.

Warm if necessary.

Apply small amount of water-based lubricant to external surface if required.

Separate labia.

Insert speculum gently at approximately 45°, following vaginal axis.

Rotate to horizontal.

Advance gently.

Open blades.

Visualise cervix completely.

3

Examine cervix

Describe:

Colour

Size

Ectropion

Erosion

Ulcer

Polyp

Contact bleeding

Discharge

Suspicious lesion

4

Identify transformation zone

The sampling should adequately sample the relevant cervical epithelium/transformation zone according to the device and local protocol.

5

Take sample

Using the approved cervical sampling brush:

Place against cervix.

Rotate according to manufacturer's instructions.

Ensure adequate epithelial sampling.

Avoid unnecessary trauma.

For liquid-based cytology:

Rinse the brush into the vial according to the manufacturer's instructions.

Secure vial.

Label correctly.

6

Remove speculum

Close blades gently.

Withdraw slowly.

Check patient comfort.

Dispose of equipment appropriately.

7

Finish

Tell patient:

Mild spotting can occur.

Results will follow through the screening system.

Contact clinician if significant/persistent bleeding or other concerning symptoms occur.

PAP SMEAR — COMMON OSCE ERRORS

❌ Taking sample before visualising cervix properly

❌ Excessive lubricant

❌ Using inappropriate sampling device

❌ Sampling only vaginal wall

❌ Failure to sample transformation zone

❌ Incorrect vial labelling

❌ No consent

❌ No chaperone

❌ Not checking pregnancy/symptoms

❌ Calling a screening smear a diagnostic test for cancer

Vulval punch biopsy technique

Explain and consent.

Offer chaperone.

Inspect lesion carefully.

Photograph/diagram if appropriate and consented.

Identify most representative area.

Clean skin.

SECTION 8

7. OUTPATIENT HYSTEROSCOPY

This is another major MRCOG/MCPS OSCE procedure.

7

RCOG's current Green-top Guideline recommends outpatient hysteroscopy as an important diagnostic/therapeutic service, particularly for abnormal uterine bleeding, and emphasises informed consent and pain management.

Indications

Postmenopausal bleeding

Abnormal uterine bleeding

Heavy/prolonged bleeding

Intermenstrual bleeding

Suspected endometrial polyp

Submucous fibroid

Endometrial pathology

Fertility assessment in selected circumstances

Retained/missing IUCD

Following miscarriage in selected cases

RCOG specifically lists abnormal bleeding, postmenopausal bleeding, intracavitary pathology, fertility concerns and missing coil threads among indications.

Types

Diagnostic hysteroscopy

Visualisation only ± biopsy.

Operative hysteroscopy

Polypectomy

Small fibroid removal

Removal of retained IUCD

Endometrial biopsy

Other minor intrauterine procedures

Approach

Vaginoscopic/no-touch approach

Often preferred for outpatient diagnostic hysteroscopy.

Instruments

Hysteroscope

Camera

Light source

Monitor

Saline distension system

Speculum if required

Cervical instruments if required

Biopsy forceps

Tissue removal system

Polyp forceps

Formalin pot

RCOG recommends miniature hysteroscopes for diagnostic outpatient hysteroscopy; current guidance also recommends using the smallest suitable diameter for operative procedures.

BASIC HYSTEROSCOPY TECHNIQUE

Explain procedure.

Confirm indication.

Exclude pregnancy.

Consent.

Chaperone.

Discuss pain-control options.

Position patient.

Vaginoscopic entry where appropriate.

Identify cervix.

Pass hysteroscope gently.

Use normal saline for distension.

Enter uterine cavity.

Inspect systematically:

➔ Cervix → anterior wall → posterior wall → fundus → right tubal ostium → left tubal ostium.

Identify:

polyp

fibroid

hyperplastic/irregular endometrium

adhesions

septum

retained products

Take biopsy if indicated.

Perform minor operative procedure if appropriate.

Remove hysteroscope.

Document findings.

Send tissue for histology.

RCOG notes that outpatient hysteroscopy commonly takes approximately 5–10 minutes for the actual procedure, although additional procedures take longer.

SECTION 17

16. URINE PREGNANCY TEST

Extremely common in gynaecology OPD.

Indications

Before procedures/medications where pregnancy affects management:

Abnormal bleeding

Amenorrhoea

Pelvic pain

IUCD insertion

Hysteroscopy

Endometrial biopsy

Certain imaging/procedures

Technique

Fresh urine

Follow kit instructions

Check control line

Interpret test within manufacturer's specified time.

Interpretation

➔ Positive: pregnancy likely → assess location/viability according to symptoms and gestation.

Negative: pregnancy not detected, but very early pregnancy can produce a false-negative result.
