11

Ferritin

Ferritin is the most useful laboratory marker of iron stores, but it is an acute-phase reactant. RCOG specifically highlights this limitation.

Ferritin Interpretation
<15 µg/L iron deficiency strongly supported
<30 µg/L depleted/low iron stores

Low ferritin = iron deficiency until proven otherwise.

But:

Normal ferritin ≠ necessarily adequate iron

if inflammation is present.

PART III — COAGULATION

Pregnancy is a physiological hypercoagulable state.

14

Fibrinogen

Non-pregnant:

~2–4 g/L

Pregnancy:

~4–6 g/L

Can reach approximately:

6–7 g/L near term

Critical obstetric point

In major obstetric haemorrhage:

fibrinogen <2 g/L is abnormal and concerning for severe haemostatic impairment.

Unlike the non-pregnant patient, a low/“normal” fibrinogen in a bleeding pregnant patient can represent serious depletion.

SECTION 18

PART X — HbA1c

For pre-existing diabetes, HbA1c is used for risk assessment.

A commonly used preconception/antenatal target is:

HbA1c <48 mmol/mol (6.5%)

if achievable without problematic hypoglycaemia.

Very high-risk level

HbA1c ≥86 mmol/mol (10%)

is associated with very high pregnancy risk and should prompt specialist management/conception counselling.

Important NICE distinction

HbA1c is not used to diagnose gestational diabetes in routine pregnancy testing. NICE recommends the 75-g OGTT for GDM.

PART XI — THYROID FUNCTION

Pregnancy alters thyroid physiology considerably.

TSH

Typical non-pregnant:

~0.4–4.0 mIU/L

Pregnancy:

➔ ↓ especially in first trimester

may rise again later

Important

Do not automatically apply a generic non-pregnant TSH range to pregnancy.

Use pregnancy/trimester-specific laboratory reference intervals where available.

NICE recommends TSH-based testing and then FT4 depending on whether TSH is high or low; it does not prescribe one universal pregnancy TSH reference interval.

FT4

Typical:

~10–22 pmol/L

But assay-specific and pregnancy-specific ranges should be used.

TPO antibodies

Positive:

usually defined by the laboratory's assay cut-off.

Important for

Hashimoto disease

pregnancy thyroid dysfunction risk

postpartum thyroiditis risk

SECTION 21

PART XIII — FERTILITY / OVULATION TESTS

Progesterone

NICE recommends

mid-luteal progesterone

for confirmation of ovulation.

For a 28-day cycle:

Day 21

For longer/irregular cycles:

approximately 7 days before expected menstruation, e.g.:

day 28 in a 35-day cycle.

NICE recommends repeating weekly if necessary in prolonged irregular cycles.
Important exam threshold

Traditionally:

>30 nmol/L

is strongly supportive of ovulation.

However, modern interpretation should consider timing and laboratory assay; a single lower value does not necessarily prove anovulation.

SECTION 23

PART XV — AMH

AMH has no universal RCOG/NICE numerical “normal” cut-off.

Current NICE 2026 guidance is especially important:

Do not use AMH to predict spontaneous pregnancy.

AMH can be used to predict ovarian response in assisted conception.

AFC and AMH are useful for ovarian response counselling.

FSH should not be used as an ovarian-response predictor.

Exam categories often used clinically

Approximate:

AMH Interpretation
<0.5 ng/mL very low
0.5–1.0 low
1–3.5 commonly considered satisfactory
>3.5–4 high; consider PCOS depending on context

But these are not NICE diagnostic cut-offs.

SECTION 24

PART XVI — hCG

β-hCG

Approximate early pregnancy pattern:

detectable approximately 8–11 days after conception

rises rapidly in early pregnancy

approximately doubles every 48–72 h in early viable pregnancies, although this is not a rigid diagnostic rule.

Discriminatory-zone concept

Historically:

~1500–3500 IU/L

may be used with transvaginal ultrasound as a contextual threshold for visualisation of an intrauterine pregnancy.

Important

A single hCG value cannot diagnose viability or location.

Serial hCG + ultrasound + clinical picture are required.

SECTION 47

SECTION 48

PART XL — GYNAECOLOGY: CA-125

CA-125 is not a screening test for ovarian cancer in asymptomatic women.

For women ≥40 with persistent symptoms suggestive of ovarian cancer, NICE 2026 uses age-specific thresholds:

Age CA-125 threshold
40–49 ≥35 IU/mL
50–59 ≥31 IU/mL
60–69 ≥24 IU/mL
70–79 ≥25 IU/mL
≥80 ≥31 IU/mL
Important

A raised CA-125 does not equal ovarian cancer.

It may rise with:

endometriosis

menstruation

pregnancy

fibroids

PID

liver disease

heart failure.

NICE specifically says CA-125 should not be used to diagnose endometriosis.

The most important RCOG/NICE distinction

Don't memorise every laboratory value as a rigid “normal/abnormal” cut-off.

For exams and clinical practice, divide values into:

A

Physiological pregnancy ranges

➔ → Hb ↓, WBC ↑, creatinine ↓, ALP ↑, fibrinogen ↑, D-dimer ↑, albumin ↓.
B

Formal diagnostic thresholds

➔ → 140/90, 160/110, PCR 30, ACR 8, creatinine 90, ALT 40, platelets 150.
C

Disease-severity thresholds

➔ → ALT 70, bile acids 100, MCA-PSV 1.5 MoM, Hb 85, fibrinogen 2 g/L.
D

Screening thresholds

➔ → aneuploidy ≥1:150, RMI ≥250.
E

Assay-dependent reference values

➔ → TSH, FT4, ferritin, AMH, testosterone, prolactin, vitamin levels, etc. should be interpreted using the relevant laboratory/assay and clinical context.

NICE's current antenatal framework recommends booking FBC, blood group/RhD and infectious-disease/haemoglobinopathy screening, with repeat FBC and antibody testing at 28 weeks. NICE's current fertility guideline was updated in March 2026, so older MRCOG notes containing older AMH/FSH or semen reference values should be checked against the new recommendations.
