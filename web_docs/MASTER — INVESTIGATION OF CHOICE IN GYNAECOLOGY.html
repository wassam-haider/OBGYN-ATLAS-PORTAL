SECTION 1

Overview & Core Guidance

Obstetrics excluded

IOC = investigation of choice / usual first-line definitive investigation.

GS = gold standard/definitive test where different from first-line.

“Clinical” means that a physical examination/history itself is the principal diagnostic assessment rather than imaging or laboratory testing.

10. PID

This needs correction in your original table.

PID is primarily a clinical diagnosis.

The investigation is not simply:

“NAAT + TVUS = first-line diagnosis.”

Instead:

IOC:

Clinical assessment + pelvic examination

with:

pregnancy test

NAAT for Chlamydia

NAAT/culture for Gonorrhoea

M

genitalium testing where available/appropriate

urine/vaginal testing

inflammatory markers where useful

TVUS if TOA/pyosalpinx or another pelvic pathology is suspected.

A positive gonorrhoea/chlamydia/M. genitalium test supports PID, but a negative result does not exclude PID. BASHH explicitly emphasizes this.

PID — diagnostic hierarchy

➔ Clinical diagnosis → STI testing → ultrasound if complication suspected → laparoscopy in selected uncertain/severe cases.

Diagnostic Cut-offs Matrix

Core associations, key diagnostic parameters, and rapid exam differentiation:

📌 1. MENSTRUAL DISORDERS
  • ● Heavy menstrual bleeding (HMB): History + examination; investigation depends on suspected cause
  • ● HMB + suspected submucosal fibroid/polyp/endometrial pathology: Outpatient hysteroscopy
  • ● HMB + bulky uterus/pelvic mass: Pelvic ultrasound, preferably TVUS
  • ● HMB + suspected adenomyosis: TVUS
  • ● HMB + suspected large fibroid: Pelvic ultrasound
  • ● HMB + high risk of endometrial pathology: Hysteroscopy + endometrial biopsy
📌 2. POSTMENOPAUSAL BLEEDING & MENOPAUSE
  • ● Postmenopausal bleeding (PMB): TVUS in many UK pathways / direct cancer-pathway assessment according to local pathway
  • ● PMB + thickened/abnormal endometrium: Hysteroscopy + endometrial biopsy
  • ● Suspected endometrial cancer: Endometrial biopsy/histology
  • ● Endometrial polyp: Hysteroscopy
  • ● Endometrial hyperplasia: Endometrial biopsy
  • ● Atypical hyperplasia/EIN: Endometrial histology
📌 3. UTERINE STRUCTURAL DISEASE
  • ● Uterine fibroids: TVUS
  • ● Submucosal fibroid: Hysteroscopy ± ultrasound
  • ● Intramural fibroid: TVUS
  • ● Subserosal fibroid: TVUS
  • ● Cervical fibroid: TVUS/TAUS
  • ● Adenomyosis: TVUS
📌 4. ENDOMETRIOSIS & CHRONIC PELVIC PAIN
  • ● Suspected endometriosis: TVUS
  • ● Ovarian endometrioma: TVUS
  • ● Deep endometriosis: Specialist TVUS or pelvic MRI
  • ● Bowel endometriosis: Specialist TVUS/MRI
  • ● Bladder endometriosis: Specialist TVUS/MRI
  • ● Ureteric endometriosis: Specialist TVUS/MRI
📌 5. OVARIAN & ADNEXAL CONDITIONS
  • ● Simple ovarian cyst, premenopausal: TVUS
  • ● Complex ovarian cyst: TVUS
  • ● Ovarian endometrioma: TVUS
  • ● Dermoid cyst: TVUS
  • ● Functional ovarian cyst: TVUS
  • ● Hemorrhagic ovarian cyst: TVUS
📌 6. PCOS & REPRODUCTIVE ENDOCRINOLOGY
  • ● PCOS: Clinical + biochemical assessment
  • ● PCOS with irregular cycles/hyperandrogenism: Serum total/free testosterone ± SHBG
  • ● PCOS morphology: TVUS
  • ● PCOS: Exclude mimics
  • ● Hyperprolactinaemia: Serum prolactin
  • ● Cushing syndrome suspected: Endocrine testing
📌 7. INFERTILITY
  • ● Infertility – initial female assessment: History + ovulation assessment + pelvic assessment
  • ● Confirm ovulation: Mid-luteal serum progesterone
  • ● Irregular/anovulatory cycles: Progesterone + endocrine evaluation
  • ● Tubal patency, low-risk woman: HSG
  • ● Alternative tubal test: HyCoSy
  • ● Suspected tubal disease/PID/endometriosis: Laparoscopy + dye
📌 8. VAGINITIS / VULVOVAGINAL INFECTIONS
  • ● Bacterial vaginosis: Clinical assessment + vaginal microscopy
  • ● BV: Vaginal Gram stain
  • ● Vulvovaginal candidiasis: Microscopy of vaginal discharge
  • ● Recurrent candidiasis: Fungal culture/speciation
  • ● Trichomoniasis: NAAT
  • ● Trichomoniasis if NAAT unavailable: Wet mount microscopy
📌 11. VULVAL DISEASE
  • ● Lichen sclerosus: Clinical examination
  • ● Atypical lichen sclerosus: Vulval biopsy
  • ● Lichen planus: Clinical examination ± biopsy
  • ● Vulval intraepithelial neoplasia: Vulval biopsy
  • ● Vulval cancer: Incisional/punch biopsy
  • ● Vulval ulcer: Clinical + STI testing; biopsy if persistent/atypical
📌 12. CERVICAL DISEASE
  • ● Cervical screening: Primary high-risk HPV test
  • ● HPV positive + abnormal cytology: Colposcopy
  • ● Persistent HPV: Surveillance/colposcopy according to screening algorithm
  • ● CIN: Colposcopy-directed biopsy
  • ● Suspected cervical cancer: Colposcopy + biopsy
  • ● Visible suspicious cervical lesion: Urgent biopsy/colposcopy pathway
