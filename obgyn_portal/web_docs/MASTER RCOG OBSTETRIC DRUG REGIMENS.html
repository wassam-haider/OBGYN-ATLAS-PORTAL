1. HYPERTENSIVE DISORDERS OF PREGNANCY

A

Chronic hypertension / gestational hypertension

First step

Confirm BP.

Assess for pre-eclampsia.

FBC, platelets, creatinine, LFTs.

Urine protein assessment.

Fetal surveillance according to gestation/risk.

Drug of choice

LABETALOL PO

Typical starting dose:

100–200 mg orally 2–3 times/day

Titrate according to BP.

Usual maximum:

2.4 g/day

Alternative

Nifedipine MR

10–20 mg once or twice daily initially

titrate according to response.

Methyldopa

250 mg 2–3 times/day

titrate as necessary.

Duration

Continue while antihypertensive treatment is required; reassess postpartum.

Labetalol contraindications

asthma/severe bronchospasm

bradycardia

heart block

decompensated heart failure.

Adverse effects

fatigue

dizziness

hypotension

bradycardia

bronchospasm.

Methyldopa adverse effects

sedation

depression

dizziness

hepatotoxicity

haemolytic anaemia rarely.

Important

ACE inhibitors and ARBs are contraindicated in pregnancy.

NICE recommends labetalol first-line for hypertension in pregnancy, with nifedipine or methyldopa as alternatives.

SECTION 2

2. ACUTE SEVERE HYPERTENSION

BP ≥160 systolic OR ≥110 diastolic

First step

TREAT IMMEDIATELY

Do not wait for laboratory results.

DOC — IV LABETALOL

Common UK emergency regimen:

20 mg IV over ~2 minutes

If BP remains severe:

40 mg IV

then:

80 mg IV

with BP reassessment between doses according to local protocol.

Alternative — oral immediate-release nifedipine

10 mg PO

may be repeated/titrated according to response.

Alternative — IV hydralazine

5–10 mg IV

repeat/titrate according to BP.

Hydralazine preparation

Use the concentration supplied by the hospital pharmacy and administer slowly according to the local IV protocol.

NICE specifically recognizes labetalol, oral nifedipine and IV hydralazine as options for severe hypertension.

Labetalol contraindications

asthma

severe bradycardia

heart block

cardiac failure.

Nifedipine adverse effects

flushing

tachycardia

dizziness

hypotension.

Hydralazine adverse effects

maternal hypotension

tachycardia

headache

nausea.

IGFBP-1 or

PAMG-1 where appropriate.

Assess:

temperature

WBC

CRP

DOC

ERYTHROMYCIN

250 mg PO QDS

for:

10 days OR until established labour

whichever occurs first.

Avoid

CO-AMOXICLAV

as routine PPROM prophylaxis.

Adverse effects

nausea

abdominal pain

diarrhoea

Contraindications

Tocolysis

NOT ROUTINE IN PPROM.

MgSO₄ for neuroprotection when indicated.

24 mg

6 mg IM every 12 hours ×4 doses

Duration

NICE recommends corticosteroids routinely from 24+0 to 33+6 weeks when preterm birth is suspected/diagnosed/planned, considers them at 34+0–35+6 weeks, and allows a single repeat course in selected women <34 weeks. No more than two courses should be given for preterm birth.

ACOG for comparison where UK guidance differs

RCOG's current catalogue includes dedicated guidance covering PPH, GBS, PPROM, preterm birth, thrombosis, placenta praevia/PAS, FGR, thyroid disease, sepsis, multiple pregnancy and other obstetric conditions.

ACOG should therefore be used as the secondary comparison, not mixed into the RCOG regimen. For example, ACOG's hypertension guidance confirms the same major emergency-treatment framework, while its PPH guidance similarly includes uterotonics and TXA.

One final safety point

A truly exhaustive list of every obstetric medication would also include anaesthetic agents, blood products, antiarrhythmics, antibiotics for every organism, fetal cardiac drugs, chemotherapy/immunotherapy for maternal malignancy, psychiatric medicines, epilepsy medicines, transplant medicines and drugs for every maternal medical disorder. Those do not have one universal RCOG "drug of choice." The table above therefore focuses on the core obstetric conditions and obstetric-specific medications that are realistically tested in MRCOG/MCPS and commonly encountered clinically, while flagging specialist situations rather than inventing a universal dose.
