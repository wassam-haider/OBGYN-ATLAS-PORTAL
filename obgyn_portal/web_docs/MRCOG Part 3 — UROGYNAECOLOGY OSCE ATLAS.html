SECTION 1

Overview & Core Guidance

RCOG specifically expects Part 3 candidates to understand the anatomy, pathophysiology, epidemiology, aetiology and investigation of urinary/faecal incontinence, prolapse, urinary infection and lower urinary tract disorders, and to know when specialist help is required.

SECTION 3

2. IMPORTANT NORMAL NUMBERS

Parameter Important value
Normal bladder capacity ~300–500 mL
Normal voided volume commonly ~200–400 mL
Normal frequency approximately 6–8/day
Nocturia ideally 0–1/night
Post-void residual ideally <50 mL; <100 mL often acceptable depending on context
Significant retention commonly >300 mL
Recurrent UTI ≥2/6 months OR ≥3/12 months
POP-Q reference Hymen = 0 cm
POP-Q above hymen negative
POP-Q below hymen positive
PFM training for SUI ≥8 contractions × 3/day
SUI PFMT duration ≥3 months
POP stage I/II PFMT ≥16 weeks
OAB bladder training ≥6 weeks

The recurrent-UTI definition of ≥2 UTIs in 6 months or ≥3 in 12 months is specifically used by NICE.

NICE recommends a minimum 3-day bladder diary, routine urine dipstick testing in women presenting with urinary incontinence, and supervised pelvic-floor training for at least 3 months for stress/mixed incontinence.

SECTION 4

3. PELVIC ORGAN PROLAPSE

Definition

Descent of one or more of:

anterior vaginal wall

posterior vaginal wall

uterus/cervix

vaginal apex/vault

into or beyond the vagina.

Commonly associated with:

urinary symptoms

bowel symptoms

sexual dysfunction

pelvic pressure/bulge

ICS recommends POP-Q rather than simply describing a “cystocele” or “rectocele” because POP-Q objectively documents the location and degree of descent.

SECTION 9

8. POP-Q STAGING

Stage 0

No prolapse.

Stage I

Most distal prolapse is:

>1 cm above hymen

Example:

−2 cm

Stage II

Most distal point:

between −1 cm and +1 cm

So:

−1

0

+1

Stage III

Most distal point:

>1 cm beyond hymen but not complete vaginal eversion

Example:

+3 cm

Stage IV

Essentially complete eversion.

The leading edge is within approximately 2 cm of total vaginal length.

Memory

0 = none

I = high

II = hymen

III = out

IV = almost/all out

Most distal point = Ba +3

POP-Q Stage III

SECTION 16

15. OCCULT STRESS INCONTINENCE WITH PROLAPSE

Very important OSCE concept.

A woman may have:

No SUI while prolapse is unreduced

but develop leakage after reduction.

This is:

Occult SUI

Always ask about urinary symptoms and perform a stress test with prolapse reduced when appropriate. ICS specifically highlights the importance of detecting occult SUI before prolapse surgery.

SECTION 58

57. POST-VOID RESIDUAL — INTERPRETATION

There is no single universally applicable “abnormal” PVR cutoff.

Practical interpretation:

<50 mL

Generally reassuring.

50–100 mL

Usually acceptable depending on symptoms/context.

>100–200 mL

➔ Potentially abnormal → interpret clinically.

>300 mL

Significant retention is likely.

Very large PVR

Consider:

acute/chronic retention

obstruction

detrusor underactivity

OSCE principle: trend + symptoms + bladder volume matter more than one isolated number.

SECTION 59

58. PVR MEASUREMENT

Best practice

Measure soon after voiding.

Methods:

bladder ultrasound

catheterisation

Record:

voided volume + residual volume

Important

Do not simply say:

“PVR is abnormal.”

Say:

“I would interpret the PVR alongside voided volume, symptoms, renal function and the clinical context.”

For incontinence

For investigations

“I would not request investigations simply because they are available; I would tailor them to the clinical question.”

For urodynamics

“This woman does not necessarily require urodynamics if she has uncomplicated demonstrable SUI, but I would perform urodynamics if the diagnosis is unclear, there is voiding dysfunction, anterior/apical prolapse or previous SUI surgery.”
